# Portfilo Content Planning

_Last updated: 2026-09-06_

## Purpose

This document defines the lightweight v0 system Portfilo uses to decide which ideas deserve reporting time.

It does not replace:

- `EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md`;
- `RESEARCH_STANDARD.md`;
- `PUBLISHING_STANDARD.md`;
- `COPY_STYLE.md`.

The boundary is:

```text
PLANNING
Signal -> Story question -> Editorial gate -> Priority -> Research assignment

RESEARCH + PUBLISHING
Research brief -> Sources -> Evidence -> Contradictions -> Story decision -> Draft -> Verification -> Publish
```

The planning system exists to improve editorial selection, not to create a larger administrative process.

## Core principle

**The unit of planning is a question, not a headline.**

Weak candidate:

> AI adoption in the Philippines

Better candidate:

> What actually prevents a small Philippine company from deploying an AI customer-support system?

The question must be specific enough to investigate without assuming the conclusion.

A working headline may be explored later. The final headline is governed by the evidence and the Portfilo copy and editorial standards.

## Approved internal editorial focus

Portfilo remains a business and technology publication. Its approved internal editorial thesis is:

> **Portfilo focuses on understanding how technology is changing companies, industries, work, and the process of building businesses, with the Philippines as its primary reporting context and AI as its strongest current acquisition and reporting wedge.**

This is an internal editorial and prioritization statement. It is **not** a tagline, homepage promise, or automatic authorization to rewrite public institutional copy.

Three priority lanes guide assignment selection:

### AI + Work

Models, products, adoption, implementation, workflows, jobs, economics, governance, and the practical consequences of AI inside companies and industries.

AI is a current reporting and acquisition wedge, not Portfilo's entire identity. A weak AI story does not become publishable merely because AI is strategically important.

### Digital Economy Infrastructure

Payments, fintech, IT-BPM, cloud, data centers, platforms, cybersecurity, connectivity, and other systems that materially enable or constrain the digital economy.

### Building Companies

Product, distribution, sales, pricing, hiring, funding, operations, business models, and the decisions involved in creating and growing companies.

These lanes do **not** replace the public topic taxonomy:

`Business / Technology / Startups / AI / Building`

A story may fit more than one strategic lane or none. Strategic fit influences **priority only after** the normal editorial gate is passed.

Philippine reporting context should be used when geography materially affects the company, market, regulation, infrastructure, workforce, customer, or consequence being examined. Global developments can belong on Portfilo without a manufactured Philippine angle.

## Reader needs

Every candidate should identify one primary reader need.

### Update

Reader question:

> What changed?

Use when the main value is establishing a verified development or current state.

### Understand

Reader question:

> How does this work?

Use when the main value is explaining a mechanism, system, product, market, regulation, or process.

### Interpret

Reader question:

> What does this mean?

Use when the main value is context, consequence, tradeoffs, competing interpretations, or what established evidence suggests.

### Decide

Reader question:

> What should I know before making this decision?

Use for research-backed decision support without turning Portfilo into generic self-help or motivational content.

Reader need is separate from content type.

For example, an `Analysis` may primarily serve `Interpret`, while an `Explained` story may serve `Understand`. A `Reported` story may serve any of the four depending on the question.

Do not add more reader-need categories until actual publishing shows that the four-category model is insufficient.

## Editorial gate

An idea is not an assignment merely because it is interesting or timely.

Before substantial research begins, it should survive six gates.

### 1. Question

Is there a specific question Portfilo can actually investigate?

Reject or reframe topics that are only broad subjects, trends, or themes.

### 2. Importance

Why would the answer matter?

Importance can come from:

- material consequences for companies, workers, customers, builders, or markets;
- a recurring decision readers face;
- a poorly understood mechanism;
- a meaningful change in technology, business, regulation, pricing, access, or behavior;
- a question that is widely discussed but weakly evidenced.

Do not confuse popularity with importance.

### 3. Timing

Why investigate this now?

Valid answers include:

- something material changed;
- a decision or deadline is approaching;
- new evidence or data became available;
- a market or technology matured enough to evaluate;
- existing explanations are materially stale;
- an evergreen decision is repeatedly faced and remains poorly answered.

`Why now?` is not a requirement for breaking news.

### 4. Contribution

What could Portfilo add that existing coverage does not already provide well?

A substantial story should have a plausible path to at least one meaningful contribution such as:

- original interview;
- primary-source research;
- original calculation;
- original dataset or structured sample;
- useful synthesis of fragmented or difficult evidence;
- case study;
- Philippine-specific investigation when the geography genuinely affects the question;
- a comparison that has not been assembled clearly;
- a conclusion that may genuinely emerge from the reporting.

Rewriting existing articles is not enough.

### 5. Evidence

Is there a credible path to evidence?

Before assignment, identify likely primary or strong secondary sources where practical.

A promising question with no realistic evidence path should be held, reframed, or killed rather than converted into unsupported commentary.

### 6. Feasibility

Can Portfilo report this properly with the available time, access, skills, and resources?

Consider:

- source access;
- interview access;
- data availability;
- technical complexity;
- legal or privacy concerns;
- expected research time;
- whether the question can be scoped down without damaging its value.

Feasibility must also reflect **Portfilo's current reporting position**, not an imagined mature newsroom. At assignment time, assess which sources are realistically reachable given the publication's current relationships, prior response history, warm introductions, public contact routes, reputation, time horizon, and the specificity of the request.

This assessment determines the outreach strategy and how much the story may depend on interviews. It does not determine whether a person or institution is important enough to contact, and it does not make an easy-to-reach source more credible than a difficult one.

For an important but low-probability source, Portfilo may still make the attempt. The assignment should not become indefinitely blocked by that reply unless the source is genuinely necessary to establish the story. Use public records, datasets, calculations, alternative knowledgeable sources, or a narrower framing where they can answer the question rigorously.

A good idea can still be the wrong assignment right now.

## Gate outcomes

Use one of four outcomes:

### PASS

The candidate is strong enough to enter prioritization and potentially become a research assignment.

### REFRAME

The subject is promising, but the question, scope, timing, contribution, or evidence path needs to change.

### HOLD

The candidate may become stronger when new evidence, access, timing, or capacity becomes available.

### KILL

The question lacks sufficient importance, originality, evidence, feasibility, or Portfilo value.

Killing a weak candidate is a successful editorial outcome.

## Existing-coverage check

Before approving substantial work, make a quick competitive and source check:

> Has someone already answered this well?

Possible outcomes:

### No

This is a useful information-gap signal, subject to normal evidence and feasibility checks.

### Partly

Record exactly what remains unanswered or insufficiently evidenced.

### Yes, but circumstances changed

Record what new development, evidence, market condition, or consequence justifies another story.

### Yes, comprehensively

Do not publish a weaker rewrite. Reframe or kill the candidate unless Portfilo can make a materially different contribution.

Coverage by another publication is not itself a reason to reject a subject. Important subjects will often be covered by many outlets.

The question is whether Portfilo can add something worthwhile.

## Prioritization

Candidates that pass the editorial gate receive one priority band:

- `HIGH`
- `MEDIUM`
- `LOW`

Do not use a 100-point score or another formula that implies false precision.

Priority is editorial judgment informed by:

- consequence;
- information gap;
- timeliness;
- originality potential;
- evidence availability;
- Portfilo fit;
- strategic-lane fit;
- useful lifespan;
- reporting effort.

Strategic fit is a **priority input, not an editorial gate**. It may help choose between otherwise worthy assignments. It may not rescue a weak question, weak evidence path, low-value rewrite, or infeasible assignment.

A numerically measurable factor does not automatically deserve more weight than an editorially important one.

### Editorial veto

Priority can change at any time.

A candidate may be downgraded or killed if:

- the premise becomes false;
- the evidence is weaker than expected;
- access disappears;
- the question proves trivial;
- another publication produces a definitive version first;
- the likely conclusion is not supportable;
- a more important assignment displaces it.

Time already spent is not a reason to publish.

## Candidate record

Keep the planning record compact.

Use this structure:

```text
STORY QUESTION

Primary topic:
Business / Technology / Startups / AI / Building

Strategic lane:
AI + Work / Digital Economy Infrastructure / Building Companies / None

Likely content type:
Reported / Explained / Guide / Profile / Analysis / Perspective / Field Notes

Primary reader need:
Update / Understand / Interpret / Decide

Why it matters:

Why now:

Possible Portfilo contribution:

Evidence path:

Access / outreach plan:
Who is realistically reachable now, which sources are essential versus optional, and what non-interview evidence can carry the story if replies do not arrive?

Existing coverage / information gap:

Scope / likely difficulty:

Derivative potential:
Article / Brief / chart / social / video / other

Priority:
HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW

Decision:
PASS / REFRAME / HOLD / KILL
```

The likely content type is provisional. Research determines what the final story actually is.

## Copy Style integration

`COPY_STYLE.md` applies during planning, but copy style does not decide which subjects are worthy of coverage.

### Pitch framing check

Before approving a candidate, check that the story question:

- uses concrete nouns and verbs where possible;
- does not rely on hype;
- does not use slogan language;
- does not create a curiosity gap instead of stating the question;
- does not force a Philippine angle where geography does not materially affect the subject;
- does not assume the conclusion;
- does not rely on generic `future of`, `game-changing`, `revolutionary`, or similar framing when a more concrete question exists;
- does not sound like generic AI-generated publication copy.

The planning question should remain useful after promotional language is removed.

### Headline and dek check

Final headline and dek approval happens after the evidence stabilizes.

They must follow both `COPY_STYLE.md` and `EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md`.

The final language must:

- reflect the actual reported finding or question;
- preserve material scope;
- preserve uncertainty;
- avoid stronger causality or certainty than the evidence supports;
- use plain, specific language;
- avoid repeating the same promise across headline and dek.

The headline must not outrun the evidence.

## Distribution and derivative formats

A planning record may note derivative potential, but distribution potential is not an editorial gate.

A story being easy to turn into a video, social post, chart, or carousel does not make the reporting more important.

The rule remains:

**Research once, publish many.**

Derivative formats must come from the same verified research packet and may not introduce unsupported factual claims.

## Work-in-progress limits

Portfilo v0 is initially operated with limited editorial capacity.

Default limits:

- maximum one flagship investigation in active production at once;
- generally no more than three substantial stories in active research at once.

These are operating limits, not publishing quotas.

The idea pool may contain many more candidates.

Do not create a required weekly article count merely to fill a schedule.

The pipeline should determine output.

## Fast-moving factual updates

Rigor should not create slowness for its own sake.

A short factual update may use a faster path when:

- the development is verified;
- the source is authoritative and clearly attributed;
- the scope is narrow;
- Portfilo does not imply consequences that have not been researched.

For fast-moving AI developments, `AI Update` may be used as an **internal operating label** for this narrow path. It is not a new public topic or Astro `contentType`.

An AI Update should answer only what can be established quickly and responsibly, usually:

- what materially changed;
- what the company, model, product, policy, or research release actually says;
- what is directly observable or independently verifiable;
- what remains unknown.

It may be distributed through social or Portfilo Brief without requiring a full website story. If the development requires explanation, testing, or consequential interpretation, create a separate planning candidate and use the normal gate.

`Portfilo Test` is the approved internal format for an original product/model/agent/workflow test or comparison. It is governed by `docs/PORTFILO_TEST_STANDARD.md` and does not bypass this planning gate. A Test candidate must still establish a worthwhile question, contribution, evidence path, feasibility, and scope before a test protocol is built.

Methodology approval does not authorize any specific Test. Each Test requires its own assignment decision and frozen brief before final scored runs.

`AI Showcase` is not a separate research format. A showcase, screenshot sequence, video, carousel, social post, or newsletter item may present evidence from a completed Portfilo Test only as a derivative format. Do not turn vendor demos, casual prompting, one-off impressions, or cherry-picked runs into evidence by calling them a Test or showcase.

A larger question raised by any fast update should become a separate planning candidate and follow the normal gate.

## Editorial launch research

The first real use of this system is the separate Editorial Launch Research project in `research/editorial-launch/`.

That research should determine what Portfilo investigates first rather than assuming the answer in advance.

Expected workspace:

```text
research/
└── editorial-launch/
    ├── brief.md
    ├── audience-signals.md
    ├── competitor-map.md
    ├── information-gaps.md
    ├── candidate-stories.md
    ├── candidate-series.md
    ├── evaluation.md
    └── recommendation.md
```

`candidate-stories.md` is required so the research does not assume that Portfilo must launch with a series.

The research may recommend:

- one standalone flagship;
- several unrelated strong stories;
- one coherent launch package;
- a series.

The expected candidate pool may be broad, for example 20-30 credible ideas, but this is not a quota.

A plausible final launch package may be one flagship plus two to four supporting stories, but the evidence should determine the actual number.

Do not manufacture stories to fill homepage slots.

## Relationship to research packets

Once a candidate becomes a substantial assignment, create the appropriate packet under `research/stories/` and follow `RESEARCH_STANDARD.md`.

For a Portfilo Test assignment, use `research/tests/<slug>/` and `research/PORTFILO_TEST_TEMPLATE.md` under the additional rules in `docs/PORTFILO_TEST_STANDARD.md`.

The research packet, not the planning record, becomes the source of truth for factual claims.

Planning records may contain hypotheses, leads, and provisional framing. They are not evidence.

## Review loop

After Portfilo has enough real publishing history, review whether the planning system is helping.

Possible revisit triggers:

- consistently too many candidates die after expensive research;
- the four reader needs fail to describe actual useful work;
- work-in-progress limits materially impair timely coverage;
- prioritization repeatedly selects stories that underperform their intended reader need;
- a repeated editorial problem cannot be solved through the existing gates;
- multiple editors require clearer assignment ownership or workflow state.

As Portfilo gains publication history, relationships, response history, and audience reach, also revisit the access assumptions used during feasibility checks. Sources that are unrealistic today may become realistic later; the reverse can also happen. Do not permanently downgrade a class of reporting because access was weak at an earlier stage.

Do not expand the system in anticipation of hypothetical scale.

## Research basis

The v0 planning model was adopted after reviewing Portfilo's existing editorial, research, publishing, and copy standards and external newsroom research on audience needs.

Useful reference points include:

- Reuters Institute, `More than 'just the facts': How news audiences think about user needs` (2024): https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2024/more-just-facts-how-news-audiences-think-about-user-needs
- Reuters Institute, `Digital News Report 2024` executive summary: https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2024/dnr-executive-summary
- Smartocto, `User needs explained`: https://smartocto.com/user-needs/

The 2026 vision-alignment pass additionally reviewed current evidence on Philippine digital-economy scale and AI adoption/exposure, publisher investment in original reporting/explanation and specialist distribution, and Google's stated preference for original, locally relevant, expert content in Discover. Those sources support the strategic lanes and AI wedge, but they do not override Portfilo's editorial gates or create public institutional claims automatically.

The research supports using audience needs as an editorial lens, but Portfilo deliberately reduces the broader User Needs 2.0 framework to four needs that fit the v0 publication: Update, Understand, Interpret, and Decide.

The external frameworks are inputs, not rules. Portfilo's own editorial standards remain authoritative.
