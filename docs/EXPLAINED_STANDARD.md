# Portfilo Explained Standard

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

_Status: FOUNDER-APPROVED / CANONICAL COMPANION TO `docs/WRITING_SYSTEM.md`_

## Purpose

This standard records a failure discovered while developing the `starting-a-business` story.

A heavily researched article can still fail as an explainer when the draft tries to display the research process instead of using that research to make the subject easier to understand.

Portfilo's Explained format exists to help the reader understand a system, term, mechanism, or consequential question.

It is not a literature review.

## Core rule

> **Research everything needed for confidence. Show only what the reader needs for understanding.**

Research depth and published source density are different things.

A large research packet does not create an obligation to mention every source, expert, study, dataset, caveat, or contradiction in the article body.

The research packet is the evidentiary foundation. The public article is the explanation built from it.

## What belongs backstage

The following should normally remain in the research packet unless the reader needs them to understand or trust a consequential claim:

- source-by-source summaries;
- lists of famous writers or founders who made similar observations;
- multiple studies establishing the same basic mechanism;
- methodological detail that does not change interpretation;
- caveats that are already handled by narrower wording;
- evidence gathered for contradiction search but not needed in the final explanation;
- repeated proof that Portfilo researched the subject thoroughly.

Do not make the reader watch the newsroom do its homework.

## What belongs in the article

Surface evidence when it performs a real editorial job, such as:

- establishing a fact the reader would reasonably question;
- quantifying scale or prevalence when the number changes understanding;
- distinguishing a common belief from what the evidence actually supports;
- protecting against a misleading generalization;
- supplying Philippine context that materially changes the explanation;
- showing a counterexample or contradiction that changes the answer;
- giving one concrete example that makes an abstract mechanism legible.

When several sources support the same ordinary explanatory point, prefer the clearest supported statement rather than a parade of citations.

## Source restraint

For an Explained story, do not organize paragraphs around sources by default.

Weak pattern:

> Source A says X. Source B says Y. Study C finds Z.

Preferred pattern:

> Explain the mechanism in plain language. Introduce a source only where the source itself adds necessary authority, precision, scale, or contradiction.

Names are not evidence of clarity. Famous-founder authority should never substitute for explanation.

## Reader-first structure

An Explained article should normally be organized around the reader's real questions or the mechanism itself, not around the order in which research was gathered.

A useful test:

> If the source names and citations were temporarily hidden, would the reader still understand the subject?

If not, the draft may be reporting the research rather than explaining the subject.

## Plain-language rule

Research terminology should be translated before it reaches the reader unless the term itself matters.

For example, if the research describes entrepreneurship as `experimentation under uncertainty`, the public explanation may simply say that a new business has to make decisions before it knows which ones will work.

Keep the technical term only when naming it improves understanding rather than displaying sophistication.

## Evidence compression

When multiple sources establish the same point:

1. identify the narrow claim the article actually needs;
2. choose the strongest or most relevant evidence for the public piece;
3. keep the remaining support in the packet;
4. preserve important limitations through accurate wording even if the underlying methodological discussion stays backstage.

The goal is not fewer facts for its own sake. The goal is the least evidence display needed for a trustworthy explanation.

## No fake profundity

Explained prose should not turn a simple mechanism into an aphorism merely to make the article sound authored.

Delete or rewrite sentences whose main job is to:

- sound quotable;
- create a dramatic contrast;
- turn an ordinary fact into a `hard truth`;
- produce a polished `not X, but Y` insight;
- summarize the previous paragraph in elevated language;
- make the writer sound cleverer than the explanation requires.

Prefer the literal sentence when it carries the same meaning.

## Ending rule

Explained stories should stop when the original question has been answered.

Do not automatically convert the final section into:

- coaching;
- a moral;
- founder motivation;
- a resilience message;
- a numbered takeaway framework;
- a slogan.

A bounded implication is enough.

## Draft review test

Before an Explained draft is shown for founder/editor review, ask:

1. Can a reader state the answer in ordinary language after reading?
2. Is every visible source doing work for the reader?
3. Could any research paragraph be replaced by one clearer explanatory paragraph without losing accuracy?
4. Are citations supporting the explanation rather than becoming the structure?
5. Did the draft accidentally become Analysis, Perspective, Guide, or a literature review?
6. Did polished AI phrasing survive because it sounded impressive rather than because it helped?
7. Does the article feel written for the audience rather than for the research record?

If the answer to the last question is no, rewrite before proceeding.

## Origin and scope

This rule was explicitly approved by the founder/editor on 2026-09-07 after rejecting early `starting-a-business` drafts for excessive source display and AI-shaped prose.

It applies prospectively to all Portfilo `Explained` stories and should be used alongside:

- `docs/WRITING_SYSTEM.md`;
- `docs/STYLE_RESEARCH_STANDARD.md`;
- `docs/RESEARCH_STANDARD.md`;
- `docs/COPY_STYLE.md`.

Existing published stories are not grandfathered as style precedent. They may be separately audited against the current writing system before being retained, revised, unpublished, or removed.
