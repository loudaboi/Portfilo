# Portfilo v1 Creative Direction

_Last updated: 2026-09-14_

## Status

**FOUNDER-APPROVED / CURRENT CREATIVE AUTHORITY**

This document supersedes the previous Obsidian Precision visual direction and its implementation-specific design system.

Approved direction:

# Responsive Identity

Portfilo should not be treated as a static brand applied to a website.

The identity itself should behave like software: responsive, stateful, and capable of changing behavior across Work, Services, Hustle, Journal, and the studio layer while remaining recognizably Portfilo.

## Why the previous direction was rejected

The implemented Obsidian Precision homepage was founder-rejected after rendered review.

The failure was not a minor QA issue.

The rendered result relied too heavily on a now-generic creative-tech recipe:

- permanent dark canvas;
- oversized sans-serif typography;
- thin rules;
- large empty fields;
- muted gray hierarchy;
- conventional section stacking;
- reveal animation as the primary motion language.

It was technically valid but visually generic, flat, and insufficiently distinctive.

Do not polish or incrementally refine that homepage into the next design.

Treat it as rejected implementation evidence.

## Creative objective

Portfilo should feel like an independent software studio from the near future, designed with a strong and recognizable point of view rather than assembled from contemporary agency conventions.

The system should feel:

- digital;
- intelligent;
- contemporary;
- capable;
- visually ambitious;
- controlled;
- specific to Portfilo;
- alive through meaningful state and motion.

Premium quality must come from authorship and coherence, not darkness, empty space, chrome, or generic luxury cues.

## Core idea

The Portfilo identity should be able to respond to context.

Potential behaviors to explore include:

- variable width or weight;
- compression and expansion;
- reconfiguration;
- typographic state changes;
- spatial rearrangement;
- content-responsive identity states;
- transitions between commercial, editorial, and human surfaces.

These are exploration territories, not approved effects.

The identity must remain legible and useful.

## Identity

### Wordmark

The current italic Portfilo wordmark is **retired as the default design constraint**.

It may remain in Git history and current runtime until replacement work is approved, but future identity exploration must not preserve its geometry merely because it exists.

The new wordmark should be designed as part of the Responsive Identity system.

It should be evaluated for:

- static legibility;
- small UI use;
- large display use;
- light and dark environments;
- motion;
- variable or responsive behavior;
- editorial use;
- Work/product presentation;
- social and browser identity.

### Symbol

A standalone symbol is **open, not required**.

Do not invent a generic:

- P;
- PF monogram;
- star;
- spark;
- cube;
- bracket;
- portal;
- grid;
- AI mark.

A symbol should exist only if it emerges naturally from the identity system and performs a real job such as favicon, compact UI identity, transition state, social avatar, or product mark.

The current favicon/symbol has no authority over the new direction.

## Typography

Typography is now an identity problem, not merely a hierarchy token.

The current Instrument Sans + IBM Plex Mono implementation remains usable in the rejected runtime but is **not locked for the redesign**.

Identity exploration may test:

- variable type;
- custom lettering;
- modified commercial/open type;
- controlled typographic transformations;
- different reading/display families where justified.

Do not select typefaces only because they look futuristic or fashionable.

## Color and material

Do not lock Portfilo to permanent black.

The new system may move between:

- off-white;
- ink black;
- graphite;
- neutral/silver values;
- one controlled signal color;
- contextual project/editorial color.

Silver should be explored as a behavior, highlight, tonal relationship, or digital material cue.

Do not turn Portfilo into literal metallic hardware, a chrome interface, or sci-fi industrial rendering.

Different surfaces may have different atmospheres while remaining part of the same identity.

## Surface behavior

### Work

Work may become the most spatial and product-led surface.

Real project interfaces and evidence should eventually carry substantial visual weight.

### Services

Services may use the identity system interactively rather than defaulting to static rows/cards.

The exact interaction is not yet approved.

### Hustle

Hustle may deliberately loosen the digital system and let authentic photography/human context dominate.

### Journal

Journal may collapse into a quieter editorial state optimized for reading and information.

The contrast between these surfaces is desirable.

They do not need identical layouts or backgrounds.

## Motion

Motion should be identity and state behavior, not generic polish.

The new system should explore motion that can:

- transform identity state;
- reorganize information;
- connect surfaces;
- expose hidden structure;
- communicate selection;
- connect project/media transitions;
- change spatial relationships.

Do not treat fade-up-on-scroll as the primary motion language.

GSAP remains an available implementation dependency.

Native scroll remains the default unless a later approved concept establishes a concrete need otherwise.

## Spatial / GPU technology

WebGL, canvas, shaders, and Three.js are **not approved by default**, but they are no longer categorically excluded.

The rule is:

> No GPU/spatial technology without an approved concept that materially benefits from it.

A concept must justify the dependency through identity, information, or product value rather than awards-site spectacle.

## Navigation and usability

Experimental identity behavior must not make navigation confusing.

Portfilo may explore unconventional composition, but visitors must still be able to understand and access:

- Work;
- Services;
- Hustle;
- Journal;
- Contact.

Accessibility, keyboard use, reduced motion, responsive behavior, and performance remain non-negotiable.

## Product architecture retained

The commercial/editorial product model remains:

`Work / Services / Hustle / Journal / Contact`

Newsletter remains embedded distribution/acquisition.

The approved **Problem-led software** positioning territory remains current.

## Current anti-patterns

Do not default to:

- permanent black + giant white grotesk;
- generic creative-agency grids;
- repeated thin-rule sections;
- bento grids;
- generic dark SaaS;
- neon AI gradients;
- fake terminal UI;
- glassmorphism;
- decorative chrome blobs;
- meaningless 3D;
- generic floating devices;
- random particle fields;
- motion added only to signal premium;
- fabricated project media;
- fake dashboards;
- fake case studies.

## Approved direction decision

The founder reviewed three creative territories:

1. Responsive Identity;
2. Software Instrument;
3. Spatial Editorial.

**Responsive Identity is selected.**

Software Instrument and Spatial Editorial remain useful research references, not parallel implementation directions.

One idea from Spatial Editorial is retained:

> Portfilo surfaces may have different atmospheres. The identity system should connect them rather than forcing visual uniformity.
