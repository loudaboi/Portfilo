# Portfilo v1 Identity Exploration

_Last updated: 2026-09-14_

## Status

**EXPLORATION / NOT YET FOUNDER-APPROVED**

Parent authority:

`docs/V1_CREATIVE_DIRECTION.md`

Current preference:

**simple premium wordmark + simple standalone symbol**

The symbol should emerge from the same form language as the wordmark rather than being invented as a separate decorative icon.

## Shared criteria

Every direction must work as:

- a static wordmark;
- a small header identity;
- a compact favicon/social mark;
- black on light;
- light on dark;
- a large display asset;
- an identity that can support motion without requiring the logo itself to constantly morph.

Reject:

- generic P/PF monograms;
- stars/sparks;
- literal portals;
- AI symbols;
- arbitrary chrome;
- fake futuristic geometry;
- logos whose only idea is a fashionable typeface.

## Concept A — Ligature

### Core idea

Build the identity around the natural `fi` sequence inside **Portfilo**.

The wordmark remains simple, but the `f` and `i` are custom-drawn as one controlled ligature.

The ligature becomes the standalone symbol when abstracted from the word.

### Why it belongs to Portfilo

Unlike a generic P monogram, the `fi` is unusually specific to the name.

It gives the wordmark one memorable intervention without forcing every letter to be stylized.

The symbol and wordmark are therefore the same design system.

### Form territory

Explore:

- a shared vertical stem;
- one deliberate cut/notch;
- a controlled relationship between the f crossbar and i dot;
- compact proportions that remain legible at favicon size;
- a symbol that can read abstractly before it reads literally as `fi`.

Avoid:

- obvious coding brackets;
- arrow clichés;
- making the i dot a spark/star;
- excessive ligature complexity.

### Wordmark character

Preferred:

- refined grotesk or hybrid sans;
- medium-to-tight spacing;
- custom `fi`;
- subtle optical work on P, r, t, l, and terminal o.

The overall word should feel calm. The ligature carries the identity.

### Motion potential

The `fi` relationship can separate/rejoin during transitions while the static mark remains unchanged.

Motion is optional behavior, not required for recognition.

### Strength

Highest chance of producing a symbol that feels genuinely derived from the Portfilo name.

### Risk

A literal `fi` ligature can become typographically ordinary if the geometry is not distinctive enough.

## Concept B — Twin Counter

### Core idea

Portfilo contains two `o` characters.

Design a custom circular counter/form that appears twice in the wordmark and becomes the basis of the standalone symbol.

The two occurrences create rhythm inside the word rather than relying on one decorative letter.

### Why it belongs to Portfilo

The repeated form is already present in the name.

The identity can use the relationship between the first and final `o` as a recurring compositional device.

The symbol could be derived from two related counterforms rather than a letter monogram.

### Form territory

Explore:

- circular vs slightly squared counters;
- a single directional cut shared by both o forms;
- offset/paired counterforms for the compact symbol;
- a relationship between open and closed states;
- positive/negative versions that survive tiny sizes.

Avoid:

- literal chain links;
- infinity symbols;
- portal imagery;
- app-logo circles with no typographic relationship.

### Wordmark character

Preferred:

- uppercase or small-cap leaning;
- very controlled geometry;
- restrained contrast;
- custom o forms that are noticeable only after a second look.

This is the most minimal/fashion-adjacent direction.

### Motion potential

The paired forms can move between alignment and offset as a navigation/state device.

The wordmark itself remains static in normal use.

### Strength

Strongest quiet-luxury potential.

### Risk

Circular symbols are common. The counter geometry must be unusually well resolved to avoid looking generic.

## Concept C — Cut Rhythm

### Core idea

Create one precise cut/terminal rule that appears selectively across several Portfilo letters.

The wordmark is not based on one hero glyph. Its identity comes from a recurring construction rule.

The standalone symbol is a compact composition made from that same cut geometry.

### Why it belongs to Portfilo

This gives Portfilo a recognizable typographic grammar rather than a decorative logo trick.

It also fits the software-studio side of the company because the identity behaves like a reusable rule/system.

The meaning remains visual rather than literal.

### Form territory

Explore:

- one consistent diagonal or horizontal terminal;
- controlled cuts in letters such as P, t, f, l, or o;
- a compact two-part emblem derived from the same terminal angle;
- strong negative space;
- no more than one signature construction rule.

Avoid:

- stencil-tech aesthetics;
- racing/sports slashes;
- cyberpunk cuts;
- applying the cut to every possible letter.

### Wordmark character

Preferred:

- custom grotesk;
- slightly wider and more architectural than Concept A;
- generous optical spacing;
- one repeated detail visible across the word.

### Motion potential

The cut can become a masking/reveal rule throughout the wider identity.

This direction has the strongest bridge between static branding and Responsive Identity behavior.

### Strength

Most system-oriented and most expandable into the website.

### Risk

If overdone it becomes a generic technology wordmark.

## Current recommendation

Explore **Concept A — Ligature** first, with the restraint and proportion discipline of Concept B.

Reason:

- it derives directly from the Portfilo name;
- it gives the symbol a legitimate origin;
- it can stay extremely simple;
- it avoids generic P/PF monograms;
- it can bridge studio, editorial, and fashion-adjacent presentation;
- it supports Responsive Identity behavior without forcing a complicated logo.

Concept C should remain the fallback if the ligature cannot produce a sufficiently distinctive compact mark.
