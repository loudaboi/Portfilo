# Portfilo Editorial Standard

_Last updated: 2026-09-10_

## Mission

Portfilo should help readers understand consequential business and technology developments through useful, specific, evidence-aware journalism and analysis.

The Philippines is Portfilo's primary reporting ground when geography materially affects the company, market, regulation, infrastructure, workforce, customer, or consequence being examined. Global developments can belong without a manufactured Philippine angle.

Portfilo is not a motivation feed, content farm, startup cheerleader, AI hype feed, or generic self-improvement publication.

## Editorial direction

Current internal attention priorities are defined in:

- `docs/PROJECT_STATE.md`;
- `docs/EDITORIAL_INTELLIGENCE.md`;
- `docs/CONTENT_PLANNING.md`.

The current priority areas are AI + Work, Startups + Building Companies, and Digital Economy + Infrastructure, while major business/technology developments outside those lanes may still belong when they are materially useful.

These are internal attention priorities, not public taxonomy labels or publishing quotas.

## Core editorial principles

### 1. Accuracy before fluency

A sentence that sounds convincing but cannot be supported does not belong in a factual story.

Do not publish a factual claim because it is plausible, common knowledge on social media, or generated confidently by AI.

### 2. Research before prose

The reporting question and evidence come before the article.

The workflow is not:

`topic -> AI -> article`

It is:

`question -> research -> evidence -> story decision -> draft -> verification -> publication`

Fast factual News may use the narrower verified-development path defined in `docs/PUBLISHING_STANDARD.md`.

### 3. Useful specificity

Prefer concrete context over generic business or startup advice.

When Philippine conditions materially affect the question, investigate them specifically.

Weak:
> Startups should focus on product-market fit.

Better:
> Before calling a Philippine startup product-market fit, what evidence shows customers return, pay, or expand their usage?

### 4. Distinguish kinds of claims

Portfilo should keep these conceptually separate:

- **Fact** - directly supported by credible evidence.
- **Reported claim** - attributed to a person or organization but not independently established as underlying fact.
- **Estimate** - calculated from stated inputs/assumptions.
- **Inference** - Portfilo's interpretation of evidence.
- **Opinion / recommendation** - Portfilo's judgment.

Readers should not have to guess which category a consequential statement belongs to.

### 5. Preserve uncertainty

If the evidence cannot establish something, do not invent certainty.

The first options are usually to omit the unsupported claim, narrow it, or attribute it precisely.

When a material uncertainty itself affects the reader's interpretation, state it accurately.

Prefer evidence-specific wording such as:

> Public filings reviewed for this story do not disclose her personal income.

Over vague or performative wording that centers the publication merely to signal rigor.

Do not convert missing evidence into fake precision.

### 6. Verification disclosures require editorial judgment

Phrases such as `Portfilo could not independently verify`, `Portfilo was unable to confirm`, or `Portfilo did not receive a response` are not default credibility language.

Before using a verification or non-response disclosure, ask whether the reader materially needs that information to understand the evidentiary status, fairness, or limitation of the story.

Use a disclosure when it is genuinely consequential, for example when:

- a material claim from an interested, anonymous, single, or secondhand source remains necessary to the story and omission of the verification status could imply independent confirmation;
- a serious allegation or materially negative portrayal requires a fair opportunity to respond and the relevant party could not be reached after reasonable efforts;
- the absence of public evidence is itself part of the finding;
- a consequential company metric, performance claim, financial claim, or other assertion cannot be corroborated and must still be reported because the claim itself is newsworthy;
- a methodology or evidence limitation would materially change how a reasonable reader interprets the result.

Do not use a disclosure merely because:

- information is normally private or was never expected to be public;
- an interview request was unanswered but the story does not depend on that response;
- the same epistemic status can be conveyed more precisely through attribution;
- adding `Portfilo could not...` makes the story sound more investigative;
- the sentence describes the newsroom's effort more than the subject readers need to understand.

Prefer the most specific formulation available. For example:

> The company says the system cut handling time by 30%. It did not publish the baseline, sample, or measurement period.

may be more informative than:

> Portfilo could not independently verify the 30% figure.

If a material claim cannot be verified and has no sufficient reason to remain in the story, leave it out rather than publishing it with a disclaimer.

For unusual, high-risk, legally sensitive, or fairness-sensitive cases, check the relevant professional or legal standard before deciding whether and how to disclose verification limits or unsuccessful contact attempts.

### 7. Headlines must not outrun evidence

A headline may be sharper than the body, but never more certain than the reporting.

Avoid clickbait that changes:

- `some` into `all`;
- correlation into causation;
- an advertised salary into actual earnings;
- anecdotes into population-level facts;
- an estimate into a measured number.

### 8. Local context is part of the value when it is real

When relevant, investigate how global ideas interact with Philippine realities such as:

- purchasing power and consumer behavior;
- taxes and mandatory contributions;
- family obligations;
- geography and commuting;
- local labor and talent markets;
- startup financing and access to capital;
- AI adoption and access to compute, data, and technical talent;
- digital infrastructure;
- regulation;
- distribution and payments;
- culture and professional networks.

Do not force a Philippine angle when it adds no value.

### 9. Original contribution

A substantial Portfilo story should contain at least one meaningful contribution such as:

- original interview;
- primary-source research;
- original calculation;
- original dataset or structured sample;
- useful synthesis of difficult information;
- Philippine-specific application where geography materially affects the question;
- case study;
- a conclusion that genuinely emerged from research.

Rewriting existing articles is not enough.

## News, Why, and Hustle

The public editorial architecture is:

```text
NEWS
What changed?

WHY
Why is this happening?
How does this work?
What is changing underneath?

HUSTLE
Who is building or operating it?
How was it built, grown, survived, pivoted, or run?
```

News can be concise current reporting when a development itself is the useful thing to establish.

Why is question-led deeper work. It can examine mechanisms, causes, consequences, patterns, contradictions, systems, markets, technologies, or other questions that require deeper reporting or interpretation.

Hustle is people-led reported work about founders, operators, and business owners. It should examine real decisions, constraints, operations, failures, pivots, growth, and outcomes rather than functioning as promotional success-story content or hustle-culture motivation.

The public section is separate from `contentType`. For example, an `explained`, `analysis`, `perspective`, or `reported` piece may belong under Why, while a `profile` or `reported` piece may belong under Hustle when that section job fits.

Story ideas do not have to originate in News discovery. `docs/CONTENT_PLANNING.md` governs substantial editorial selection.

## AI coverage

AI is a primary Portfilo topic, but it does not receive a lower evidence standard because the field moves quickly.

When covering AI:

- distinguish a product demo from a deployed system;
- distinguish vendor claims from independently established performance;
- state which model, product, version, date, market, or availability condition matters;
- treat benchmarks as evidence about the benchmark, not automatic proof of real-world usefulness;
- do not infer broad job losses, productivity gains, or economic effects from a single capability demonstration;
- verify pricing, access, rollout status, and material product claims when they matter to the story;
- identify meaningful uncertainty when capabilities, policies, or market conditions are changing quickly;
- avoid language that treats AI progress as inevitable, magical, or universally beneficial.

Use **AI** as the primary public topic when AI itself is the main thing the reader needs to understand.

If the larger question is primarily about a company, startup, product, technology, or execution decision, use whichever value in the current public taxonomy best describes the actual primary subject.

Current public topic values remain:

`Business / Technology / Startup / AI`

Do not invent `Building`, `Work`, `Infrastructure`, or another internal lane as a runtime topic value unless a separate taxonomy decision changes the schema.

## Content types

Use explicit editorial types.

### Reported

Original reporting, interviews, documents, data, or direct observation.

### Explained

Clarifies a complex system or question using evidence.

### Guide

Research-backed practical instructions or decision support.

### Profile

A reported examination of a person, company, group, or path. A profile is not a rewritten biography or press release.

### Analysis

Portfilo's interpretation of established evidence.

### Perspective

Clearly identified argument or opinion.

### Field Notes

Observations from events, workplaces, communities, interviews, or field reporting. Separate observation from generalization.

### Partner

Commercial content. Must be unmistakably labeled and subject to separate commercial rules.

## AI use

AI can assist with:

- query generation;
- source discovery leads;
- document organization;
- summarization;
- extracting candidate claims;
- comparing sources;
- identifying contradictions;
- calculation/code assistance;
- interview-question development;
- structure;
- copy editing;
- adversarial review.

AI must not be treated as:

- a source;
- evidence;
- verification;
- a substitute for reporting;
- authority for a quotation, statistic, attribution, law, or current fact.

Any factual material surfaced by AI must be independently checked before publication.

## Profiles and interviews

Separate:

1. what the subject says;
2. what Portfilo can independently verify;
3. what other credible sources say;
4. Portfilo's interpretation.

Do not turn self-description into independent fact.

Quotes must come from a real source available to the reporting process. Never reconstruct or improve a quote while presenting it as verbatim.

## Data and numbers

For consequential numbers, ask:

- who measured it?
- what period?
- what population?
- what sample?
- average or median?
- nominal or adjusted?
- administrative data or survey?
- advertised amount or realized amount?
- what limitations matter?
- does another credible dataset conflict?

Do not hide limitations that materially affect interpretation.

## Corrections

Portfilo corrects factual errors transparently.

Material corrections should remain attached to the story with:

- correction date;
- what was wrong;
- what is correct.

Do not silently rewrite a consequential error in a way that conceals the correction.

Minor typographical or formatting fixes do not require a formal correction unless they change meaning.

## Commercial independence

Sponsors, advertisers, partners, or affiliates may not determine the factual conclusion of editorial coverage.

Commercial material must be clearly labeled.

Do not disguise advertising as independent reporting.

If a commercial relationship is relevant to the reader's interpretation of a story, disclose it.

## Tone

Portfilo should be:

- precise;
- curious;
- skeptical without being cynical;
- readable without being simplistic;
- confident when evidence is strong;
- explicit when evidence is weak.

Avoid:

- fake profundity;
- generic motivational language;
- inflated certainty;
- corporate jargon;
- formulaic AI openings;
- unnecessary listicles;
- repetitive summaries;
- filler conclusions.

## Publication test

Before publication, ask:

1. What did we actually find?
2. What evidence supports the central claim?
3. What would a skeptical reader challenge?
4. Did we preserve the most important caveat?
5. Is the headline fair?
6. Are facts, estimates, inference, and opinion distinguishable?
7. Does the story materially help the reader?
8. Would we be comfortable showing the source trail?
9. Does every verification/non-response disclosure materially help the reader, or is it newsroom self-reference that should be removed or rewritten more precisely?
