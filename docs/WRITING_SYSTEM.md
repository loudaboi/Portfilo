# Portfilo Writing System

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

## Purpose

This system exists to prevent a specific failure mode:

> verified ideas and evidence being presented through generic, invented, or AI-shaped prose simply because the prose sounds finished.

Portfilo already separates evidence from AI output. This document adds the equivalent discipline for structure, voice, and wording.

It applies to:

- story structure and drafting;
- headlines and deks;
- optional story metadata-description overrides;
- homepage and section copy;
- About, Standards, Subscribe, confirmation, privacy, and error surfaces;
- newsletter and distribution copy;
- any sentence that speaks on behalf of Portfilo.

It does not replace:

- `docs/EDITORIAL_STANDARD.md`;
- `docs/RESEARCH_STANDARD.md`;
- `docs/EDITORIAL_PACKAGING_STANDARD.md`;
- `docs/PUBLISHING_STANDARD.md`;
- `docs/COPY_STYLE.md`.

Those documents govern evidence, editorial integrity, story packaging, publication, and copy. This document governs **how a writing form is selected and how prose is allowed to be produced**.

## Rule zero

> **AI output has zero stylistic authority.**

A model can propose wording, structures, transitions, headlines, and edits.

Those proposals are not justified because the model produced them, because they sound polished, or because they resemble journalism.

A public writing decision must come from at least one of these sources:

1. the actual evidence and shape of the material;
2. an approved Portfilo writing recipe in this document;
3. documented professional practice researched for the relevant form;
4. an approved Portfilo institutional position;
5. an explicit human editorial decision.

AI may execute a chosen form. It may not silently invent the form, the institutional position, or the voice and then treat its own output as precedent.

## The distinction Portfilo must preserve

Writing has several kinds of authority. Do not collapse them.

### Evidentiary authority

What can be stated as fact, reported claim, estimate, inference, or opinion.

Source of truth:

- research packet;
- primary/secondary evidence;
- verified public records;
- explicit editorial judgment where the content type permits it.

AI has zero evidentiary authority.

### Structural authority

Why the story is arranged in a particular order.

Source of truth:

- the reader need;
- the evidence shape;
- the content type;
- an approved writing recipe;
- researched professional form when the recipe is insufficient.

AI has zero automatic structural authority.

### Institutional authority

What Portfilo says it is, believes, promises, intends, values, does, or does not do.

Source of truth:

- canonical repository documents;
- actual product behavior;
- explicit founder/editor approval.

A generated sentence such as `Portfilo is interested in...`, `Portfilo believes...`, or `Our mission is...` is not valid merely because it fits the strategy. It requires provenance.

### Voice authority

Why a sentence sounds the way it does.

Source of truth:

- the job of the piece;
- Portfilo's approved copy rules;
- the subject matter;
- human editorial judgment;
- structural lessons from professional examples.

Do not imitate another writer's or publication's distinctive wording, rhythm, metaphors, or voice. Examples may teach **decisions**, not sentences.

## Form follows material

Portfilo does not have one house article shape.

A breaking update, explainer, analysis, Perspective, profile, and guide solve different information problems.

Before drafting, decide:

1. What does the reader need from this piece: `Update`, `Understand`, `Interpret`, or `Decide`?
2. What does the evidence actually contain?
3. What content type is accurate?
4. Which writing form best exposes that evidence with the least distortion?

Do not choose a structure because it makes the prose feel more sophisticated.

Do not turn every story into:

- an anecdotal opening;
- a question-led explainer;
- a five-section framework;
- a `not X, but Y` argument;
- a narrative;
- an op-ed;
- an inverted pyramid.

The material chooses among forms.

## Story-style brief

Every substantial new story must have a `style-brief.md` before Draft 1.

A short Perspective or narrow update may use a compact brief, but the decision still has to exist before prose.

Use:

`research/STYLE_BRIEF_TEMPLATE.md`

The brief records:

- content type;
- reader need;
- writing job;
- evidence shape;
- selected Portfilo recipe;
- why that recipe fits;
- deviations from the recipe;
- any professional form research used;
- any human-edited exemplars reviewed;
- structural lessons taken from those exemplars;
- what must **not** be imitated;
- lead job;
- middle logic;
- ending job;
- headline and dek jobs;
- whether an optional metadata-description override is justified;
- specific AI-slop risks for the assignment.

If the writer cannot explain the structure before drafting, the story is not ready to draft.

## Style research hierarchy

When an existing recipe does not fit, research the form in this order.

### 1. The material itself

Inspect the reporting before looking for stylistic inspiration.

Ask:

- Is there a genuine event or development to lead with?
- Is there a strong observed scene?
- Is the reader primarily asking a question?
- Does the evidence form a chronology?
- Does it form categories or mechanisms?
- Is there a central argument with meaningful counterevidence?
- Is the useful output a decision or set of steps?

### 2. Professional guidance

Prefer documented newsroom, journalism-education, content-design, technical-writing, legal, regulatory, or domain guidance appropriate to the artifact.

Record the reference in the style brief.

### 3. Human-edited exemplars

When useful, review a small set of reputable, human-edited examples that solve the same writing problem.

Use them to study:

- where the answer appears;
- how context is sequenced;
- how evidence and interpretation are separated;
- section length;
- use of chronology, questions, scenes, or blocks;
- how limitations and counterarguments are handled;
- how the ending functions.

Do not harvest:

- signature phrases;
- sentence patterns distinctive to one writer;
- metaphors;
- jokes;
- cadence;
- branded terminology;
- another publication's institutional voice.

### 4. Explicit editorial invention

Sometimes Portfilo will need a new structure.

That is allowed when the editor can explain why the existing recipes do not fit and what the new structure is doing for the reader.

Record the decision before drafting.

`AI suggested this` is not sufficient rationale.

## Portfilo story-form recipes

These are defaults, not templates to fill mechanically.

### Reported

Use when the primary value is new reporting: events, interviews, documents, data, or observation.

#### Update-led reported story

Best when the important thing is what happened now.

Default motion:

1. lead with the most consequential verified development;
2. establish who/what/when/where as needed;
3. explain why the development matters now;
4. add evidence, attribution, context, and affected parties in descending or logical importance;
5. include contradiction, uncertainty, or response where material;
6. end when the useful information is exhausted.

The inverted pyramid is appropriate when readers benefit from receiving the essential information immediately.

Do not force a narrative ending onto a straightforward update.

#### Feature/enterprise reported story

Best when the reporting contains a meaningful scene, person, pattern, or system that benefits from explanation.

Possible motion:

1. observed/reported scene or concrete case;
2. nut section that states what the story establishes and why the opening matters;
3. evidence blocks that develop the central finding;
4. counterevidence, limits, or affected perspectives;
5. ending grounded in reported material rather than a moral written by the narrator.

An anecdotal lead is allowed only when the reporting actually contains a representative or revealing anecdote.

Never manufacture a scene to make the story feel journalistic.

### Explained

Use when the primary job is to make a system, term, mechanism, or consequential question understandable.

Default motion:

1. name the question or term quickly;
2. give the clearest defensible answer early;
3. establish scope and material limitation;
4. divide the explanation into logical blocks based on the reader's real sub-questions or the system's components;
5. use evidence/examples inside each block;
6. distinguish what is established from what remains unknown;
7. stop when the original question is answered.

Possible block logics:

- definition -> mechanism -> consequence -> limitation;
- current state -> how it works -> who is affected -> unresolved question;
- comparison by criteria;
- question-led sections where the questions are genuine and non-repetitive.

Do not convert the evidence into a numbered framework merely because frameworks scan well.

### Analysis

Use when Portfilo's interpretation of established evidence is the central value.

Default motion:

1. state the development, evidence base, or pattern being analyzed;
2. state the main interpretation early enough that readers know the analytical claim;
3. show the evidence that produces that interpretation;
4. test plausible competing explanations;
5. distinguish inference from established fact;
6. explain what would change the interpretation or where the evidence stops;
7. conclude with the narrow implication the evidence supports.

Analysis must not hide its inferential steps behind authoritative prose.

If the piece can be written without showing why the interpretation follows from evidence, it is not ready.

### Perspective

Use for a clearly identified argument, judgment, or point of view.

Default motion:

1. begin with a concrete observation, event, tension, or experience that genuinely belongs to the argument;
2. establish the thesis early;
3. develop a small number of points with evidence, examples, or direct experience appropriate to the claim;
4. acknowledge the strongest obvious counterargument, limitation, or failure mode;
5. respond to it fairly rather than using a straw man;
6. end by advancing or returning to the argument, not by repeating the thesis in more elevated language.

A Perspective is not exempt from evidence when it makes factual claims.

Avoid:

- motivational escalation;
- invented universals about `people`, `founders`, `readers`, or `companies`;
- strings of rhetorical questions used as substitutes for argument;
- dramatic one-line paragraphs throughout the piece;
- symmetrical slogans doing the work of evidence;
- conclusions that merely announce Portfilo's values.

### Guide

Use when the reader needs to do something or make a decision.

Default motion:

1. state the task or decision;
2. state who/what the guidance applies to;
3. give the decision criteria, prerequisites, or inputs;
4. present steps/options in the order the reader needs them;
5. attach evidence, cost, tradeoff, or risk where it changes the decision;
6. explain exceptions and failure conditions;
7. give the next action only if there is a real next action.

Guide prose should be operational. Do not hide instructions inside an essay.

### Profile

Use for a reported examination of a person, company, group, or path.

Default motion depends on the reporting.

Possible structure:

1. revealing observed/reported scene or concrete detail;
2. nut section explaining what the profile is actually examining;
3. thematic or chronological blocks built from observation, interviews, records, history, and independent context;
4. tensions or contradictions in the subject's self-description;
5. relevant voices beyond the subject;
6. ending grounded in a reported detail, unresolved tension, or current reality.

A profile is selective by design. It should reveal a subject through meaningful evidence, not summarize an entire biography or reproduce a press narrative.

### Field Notes

Use when direct observation is itself valuable.

Default motion:

1. state where/when/what was observed;
2. show a small number of specific observations;
3. supply only the context needed to interpret them;
4. distinguish observation from inference explicitly when the distinction matters;
5. identify what the observation cannot establish;
6. end with the most useful bounded takeaway or open question.

Do not turn a room, event, visit, or handful of conversations into a population claim.

### Partner

Commercial material uses a separate commercial brief and must be unmistakably labeled.

It may use an appropriate informational or guide structure, but:

- commercial requirements cannot create factual claims;
- sponsored claims require evidence or precise attribution;
- partner copy must not imitate independent editorial framing in a way that obscures the commercial relationship;
- disclosures must be literal.

## Site and product copy recipes

Site copy is not miniature editorial writing. It should normally disappear into the task.

### Navigation and actions

Source of truth: actual destination or action.

Prefer familiar labels and active verbs.

Examples of legitimate jobs:

- `Stories`
- `About`
- `Standards`
- `Subscribe`
- `Read story`
- `Confirm email`

Do not brand a control merely to make it sound distinctive.

### Status, success, and error copy

Source of truth: actual system state.

Default motion:

1. what happened;
2. what the user needs to do next, if anything.

Do not use personality to obscure a failure.

Do not blame the user for system errors.

### About and institutional copy

Source of truth: approved institutional facts and positions.

Every sentence should be traceable to one of:

- actual coverage/product behavior;
- canonical editorial standards;
- explicit founder/editor approval.

If the sentence's main job is to make Portfilo sound thoughtful, ambitious, clear, rigorous, curious, or different, it is probably a deletion candidate.

### Standards, methodology, corrections, and privacy

Source of truth: actual procedure, evidence rule, correction record, or data flow.

Use literal language.

Do not add rhetorical polish that changes precision.

### Subscribe and newsletter copy

Source of truth: actual email product behavior.

State:

- product name;
- what arrives;
- cadence if known;
- confirmation requirement when relevant;
- unsubscribe/privacy information when relevant.

Do not use subscription copy to explain Portfilo's worldview.

## Institutional-copy provenance rule

Before approving a line that speaks for Portfilo, classify it.

### Functional

Describes what a control, page, route, or feature does.

Evidence: implementation.

### Operational

Describes a real service condition, cadence, provider, process, or state.

Evidence: configuration/documented system behavior.

### Editorial / institutional

Describes what Portfilo covers, publishes, requires, believes, intends, or promises.

Evidence: canonical Portfilo document or explicit editor/founder approval.

### Legal / privacy

Describes collection, processing, disclosure, correction, terms, or rights.

Evidence: actual data flow and applicable policy/legal review.

### Promotional

Claims a quality or benefit such as `best`, `smart`, `essential`, `trusted`, `rigorous`, `practical`, `clear`, or `must-read`.

Default: reject unless there is a specific approved reason and support for the claim.

During the site-copy audit, record the class and provenance for every nontrivial public line.

## Story packaging: headline + dek

For stories, the complete reader-facing editorial package is **headline + dek**. `docs/EDITORIAL_PACKAGING_STANDARD.md` governs the detailed workflow.

Do not force a third reader-facing story-summary sentence into the package merely because metadata exists.

### Headline

Names the development, finding, question, tension, or argument.

### Dek

Adds useful editorial information that the headline does not carry, such as:

- the clearest defensible answer;
- what the story examined when that matters;
- the answer's scope;
- a meaningful finding;
- a material limitation;
- why the question matters now.

The same approved dek should travel with the headline across the story page, homepage, and Stories archive.

### Metadata description

Story metadata defaults to the dek.

A separate `metaDescription` is optional and metadata-only. Use it only when the approved dek has a concrete problem when separated from the headline on search, social, or structured-data surfaces.

Do not create a metadata override merely because:

- a third sentence feels expected;
- SEO folklore suggests a target character count;
- the writer wants to summarize the body again.

If the dek works as metadata, stop.

## Drafting contract

Before an AI-assisted Draft 1 begins, the writer/model must have:

- the approved evidence or approved Perspective premise;
- content type;
- primary reader need;
- story decision;
- `style-brief.md`;
- selected form;
- explicit factual limitations;
- known institutional-copy constraints when Portfilo itself is discussed.

During drafting:

- do not introduce a consequential factual claim absent from the evidence source of truth;
- do not invent a Portfilo belief, mission, intention, reader behavior, or editorial philosophy;
- do not add a scene, quote, character, statistic, or observed detail that was not reported;
- do not create false specificity to make prose feel human;
- do not preserve a weak sentence because it sounds polished;
- do not use another publication's prose as a hidden template.

The model is a drafting instrument, not the authority deciding what the writing should sound like.

## Structural audit before line editing

Do not polish sentences before checking the architecture.

Ask:

1. What is the piece's single writing job?
2. Does the reader receive the important answer soon enough for this form?
3. Does each section have a distinct job?
4. Is any section present only because the structure feels too simple without it?
5. Does the sequence follow logic, chronology, evidence, or reader questions rather than AI symmetry?
6. Is the strongest caveat or counterargument in the right place?
7. Would removing a section improve the piece?

If the structure fails, rewrite the structure before the prose.

## AI-slop audit

Run this after factual/structural integrity is stable.

### Paragraph scan

Read only the first sentence of every paragraph.

Check whether they:

- advance rather than restate;
- form a coherent progression;
- repeat the same grammatical construction;
- sound like section summaries generated from an outline.

### Headings-only scan

Read only headings/subheads.

Check for:

- repeated slogans;
- vague nouns;
- all headings following the same rhetorical pattern;
- headings that could belong to any generic publication.

### Read-aloud scan

Read the piece aloud.

Cut language that is difficult to say naturally unless the subject requires technical precision.

### Deletion scan

Search for sentences whose job is only to:

- explain the strategy behind the previous sentence;
- praise the publication;
- make an obvious transition feel polished;
- restate the thesis;
- produce rhythm;
- create a three-part rhetorical flourish;
- make a simple point sound profound.

Delete them unless they add real information.

### Pattern scan

Flag clusters of:

- `not X, but Y`;
- `it is not... it is...`;
- repeated rhetorical questions;
- repeated sentence fragments;
- repeated triples;
- repeated `sometimes... sometimes...`;
- `the point is...`;
- `this is why...`;
- `what matters is...`;
- `at its core`;
- `in practice` used as filler rather than a concrete distinction;
- institutional verbs such as `believes`, `cares`, `wants`, or `is interested in` without provenance.

None is automatically banned. Repetition without necessity is the failure.

## Final writing gate

Before a public story or significant public-copy revision is approved:

1. **Evidence gate** — factual claims are supported or correctly qualified.
2. **Form gate** — the selected form still matches the material.
3. **Provenance gate** — institutional claims are traceable to an approved source.
4. **Structure gate** — sections progress without filler or duplication.
5. **Human-language gate** — the copy survives read-aloud and deletion checks.
6. **Slop gate** — no repeated AI-shaped rhetorical pattern dominates the piece.
7. **Headline/dek gate** — the two-field reader-facing package is accurate, useful, non-duplicative, and works in the actual layout.
8. **Metadata gate** — the dek works as default metadata, or a separate `metaDescription` override has a documented concrete reason.
9. **Visual gate** — the writing works in the actual layout on desktop and mobile.
10. **Human approval gate** — publication or major institutional positioning requires explicit approval at the relevant phase boundary.

## Existing Portfilo work

This system is prospective and corrective.

Existing public copy and existing stories do not become approved examples merely because they are already in the repository or already published.

C027, C009, and C030 must each receive a new style brief and structure/prose audit before their writing is considered final under this system.

The site itself must receive a separate copy-inventory/provenance audit.

Do not use existing Portfilo prose as a style exemplar until it has passed that audit.

## Change control

A writing recipe may be revised when:

- a story exposes a recurring failure;
- professional guidance materially improves the form;
- real reader/editor feedback reveals a problem;
- Portfilo develops a stable, human-approved internal pattern worth preserving.

Do not turn one successful sentence or one successful story into a universal rule.

Record material changes in this document and `docs/PROJECT_STATE.md`.
