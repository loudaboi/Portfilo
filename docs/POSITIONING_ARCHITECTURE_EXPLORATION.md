# Portfilo Positioning Architecture Decision

_Status: APPROVED for v0._

_Last updated: 2026-09-02_

## Decision

Portfilo v0 will use **Architecture C: Content-first / no tagline**.

The user approved this architecture after rejecting the previous public-copy revision as vague, geographically forced, over-explained, and recognizably AI-shaped.

This decision replaces the assumption that Portfilo needs a large public positioning sentence.

## What failed

The rejected revision included lines such as:

- `Practical intelligence for ambitious Filipinos.`
- `Business and tech, from the Philippines.`
- `Business and tech make more sense when you start from here.`
- `Some stories start here. Others start somewhere else and matter here anyway.`

The problem was not only wording. Public copy was being asked to explain too much internal strategy at once, including geography, audience, scope, usefulness, rigor, and permission to cover global subjects.

That produced abstract bridge sentences instead of product clarity.

## Research finding

BackScoop was the primary reference for the reset.

Its strongest public copy is product-led: it names the subject, states what the reader gets, states cadence or format when known, and then shows the work. Its personality comes largely from story selection, headlines, writing, and product execution rather than a long brand manifesto.

Morning Brew and The Hustle reinforced the same broader lesson in different ways: a publication can use very little institutional copy when its product and editorial output are legible.

The lesson for Portfilo is not to copy any of those publications' wording. It is to reduce the amount of work assigned to brand copy.

## Approved architecture

### Masthead

Use the Portfilo wordmark.

No permanent tagline.

No geographic slogan.

When the layout needs a small center descriptor before global navigation is implemented, a neutral taxonomy line may be used:

> Business / Technology / Startups / AI / Building

This is functional coverage information, not a brand promise.

### Homepage

The homepage should show Portfilo before explaining Portfilo.

The current large identity block is specifically reopened because its scale creates pressure to invent a giant slogan.

Preferred direction:

- substantially reduce the identity block; or
- repurpose it into the lead story/current editorial focus.

The featured story should become the main homepage statement.

A temporary one-line coverage descriptor may be used while fixture journalism remains:

> Stories about companies, products, markets, and the people building them.

This is optional and should not automatically become a permanent tagline.

Any homepage About bridge should be short and factual. It should not try to prove trust or explain geographic philosophy.

### About

About should explain the publication plainly.

It should cover:

- what Portfilo is;
- what it covers;
- the kinds of stories it publishes;
- who may find it useful;
- a concise bridge to Standards.

It should not contain a geographic manifesto, motivational audience slogan, or repeated claims of intelligence, usefulness, clarity, or trust.

Founder treatment remains minimal.

### Subscribe

Portfilo Brief is a product surface.

Preferred pattern:

> Portfilo Brief
>
> New Portfilo stories by email.

Then the form and operational facts.

Do not use Subscribe to restate Portfilo's mission, audience theory, standards, or geography.

### Standards

Standards proves rigor through concrete procedures.

Do not move that responsibility into homepage marketing copy.

### Geography

Philippine relevance remains part of reporting and early distribution, but it is not required masthead or hero language.

Use Philippine context when the story itself requires it.

Do not force `Philippines`, `Filipino`, `here`, `local`, or similar language into brand copy merely to prove identity.

A global story does not need a manufactured Philippine justification to belong on Portfilo.

### Audience

The intended early audience remains younger readers around the founder's peer group who are curious about business, technology, startups, AI, companies, careers, and building.

This is an internal editorial and distribution focus, not a public tagline.

Do not label Portfilo as `Gen Z media`, `for young Filipinos`, or `for ambitious Filipinos` by default.

## Why Architecture C won

Compared with the coverage-first and reader-job-first alternatives, Architecture C had the strongest combination of:

- human feel;
- future flexibility;
- low AI-copy risk;
- low geographic-positioning pressure;
- ability to let journalism carry the brand;
- honesty before Portfilo has a fixed publishing cadence.

Its main cost is that the function and scale of the homepage identity hero must be reconsidered. That is an approved exception to the otherwise frozen visual direction.

## Human-language gate

Before any new public line is implemented, ask:

1. Does this sentence add information?
2. Would a normal person say it aloud?
3. Can it be shorter without losing meaning?
4. Is it describing the product or explaining internal strategy?
5. Does the reader need this before seeing a story?
6. Would removing it make the page clearer?

If removing it makes the page clearer, remove it.

## Canonical copy guide

`docs/COPY_STYLE.md` has been revised to reflect this approved architecture and is canonical for the next copy round.

## Next task

Prepare a coordinated implementation candidate for:

1. Homepage information hierarchy and copy;
2. About copy;
3. Subscribe copy;
4. shared masthead/footer language;
5. Standards, Story, and Series shared brand language only.

The candidate should show exact proposed copy and any narrowly required Homepage structural changes.

Do not implement the public-page replacement until the user approves that candidate.
