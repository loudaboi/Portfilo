The Department of Information and Communications Technology and the Cybercrime Investigation and Coordinating Center have directed government bodies and critical infrastructure operators to carry out urgent cybersecurity checks within **24 hours** of receiving a joint advisory.

The directive covers national government agencies, government-owned and controlled corporations, local government units, and operators of Critical Information Infrastructure. Each organization must give its agency head or chief executive a one-page assessment identifying its most serious cybersecurity risks, actions already taken, and any assistance it requires. ([Newsbytes.PH](https://newsbytes.ph/2026/09/10/dict-cicc-order-24-hour-cyber-checks-after-attacks-on-govt-websites/))

The prescribed measures include fixing critical vulnerabilities, enforcing multi-factor authentication on privileged accounts, removing unnecessary internet exposure, strengthening security monitoring, checking whether backups can be restored, reviewing third-party access, and testing incident-response and service-continuity procedures. ([Newsbytes.PH](https://newsbytes.ph/2026/09/10/dict-cicc-order-24-hour-cyber-checks-after-attacks-on-govt-websites/))

The advisory follows recent incidents involving the **Department of Migrant Workers and Department of Labor and Employment**. DICT confirmed unauthorized access involving the DMW website and a web defacement involving a DOLE web host. The affected services were taken offline while technical teams carried out forensic investigation, recovery and security hardening. ([Philippine News Agency](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1283582))

DICT said its initial assessment found no compromise of sensitive databases or personally identifiable information in either incident. The agency also investigated a reported ransomware attack against the Philippine Ports Authority but said its review found no ransomware activity or system compromise there. ([Philippine News Agency](https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1283582))

Under the new readiness framework, organizations will be classified as **green, amber or red**. Red conditions require immediate reporting and response, while amber conditions require remediation and monitoring. A green status calls for continued vigilance and does not mean an organization has no cybersecurity risk. ([TechWatch Philippines](https://techwatchph.com/2026/09/09/dict-cicc-adopt-green-amber-red-framework-for-government-cyber-readiness/))
