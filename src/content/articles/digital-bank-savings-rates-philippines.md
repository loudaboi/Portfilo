The gap can look almost absurd.

[BDO](https://www.bdo.com.ph/personal/accounts/savings/peso-savings/atm-savings) and [BPI](https://www.bpi.com.ph/personal/bank/savings-accounts/regular-savings) currently list ordinary savings rates of **0.0625% a year**. [Maya Bank](https://www.mayabank.ph/highinterestsavings/) lists a **3% base rate** on Maya Savings.

That is a 48-fold difference before any promotional boost.

The usual explanation is that digital banks have no branches, so they save on rent, tellers and physical infrastructure and pass some of those savings to depositors.

That helps, but it is not the whole answer.

## Deposits are something banks pay for

A savings deposit is not only money sitting in an account. For the bank, it is also funding.

That makes the interest rate a price. A bank that wants more deposits can choose to pay more for them.

This matters especially for newer digital banks that do not have the decades-old deposit bases of large incumbent banks. Fitch said in 2023 that Philippine digital banks were competing aggressively for deposits while they built scale, with many relying heavily on price to attract customers, [according to The Philippine Star](https://www.philstar.com/business/2023/12/05/2316427/price-focused-strategy-philippines-digital-banks-not-sustainable-fitch).

The current numbers show that higher pricing in the sector is real.

In June 2026, the [Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Statistics/Selected%20Performance%20Indicators/94_data.aspx) reported a **4.15% funding cost** for the Digital Bank Group. The comparable figure for universal and commercial banks was **1.87%**.

Funding cost is broader than the rate on one savings account, but the direction is clear: digital banks as a group are paying materially more for the money they use to fund their businesses.

## The other side of the balance sheet matters too

Paying more for deposits only works if the bank can earn enough on the assets those funds support.

Here the difference is just as large.

BSP data for June 2026 put the Digital Bank Group's **earning-asset yield at 16.97%**. For universal and commercial banks, it was **5.94%**.

The digital-bank group's net interest margin was **12.84%**, compared with **4.28%** for the large-bank group.

That does not mean a digital bank takes a peso from your savings account, lends that exact peso at 16.97%, then pockets the difference. Banks hold different mixes of loans, securities, cash and other assets.

It does show why the savings-rate comparison cannot be understood by looking only at the deposit side. Digital banks are paying more for funding, but the assets on the other side of their balance sheets are also generating much higher yields as a group.

That gives them more room to offer deposit rates that would look unusually expensive for a traditional bank with a different asset mix.

## No branches does not mean no costs

Digital banks do have a structural difference.

Under [BSP Circular No. 1105](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Regulations/Issuances/2020/c1105.pdf), a digital bank is a distinct regulatory class designed to offer financial products and services end-to-end through digital platforms without physical branches providing those services.

So the branch-cost advantage is real in the narrow sense: there is no traditional branch network to maintain.

But current operating data do not show a sector that is simply cheaper to run.

In June 2026, digital banks had a **75.39% cost-to-income ratio**, compared with **54.02%** for universal and commercial banks. The Digital Bank Group was also still loss-making in aggregate, with BSP reporting an annualized net loss of about **P5.25 billion** and a negative return on assets.

Those are group figures, not the financial condition of every individual digital bank. Some banks can perform better than the group average.

Still, they make one point clear: higher savings rates are not just spare profit being handed back to depositors because an app is cheaper than a branch.

Digital banks still spend on technology, compliance, customer acquisition, servicing, credit operations and growth.

## The biggest rate on the ad may not be the normal rate

There is another reason digital-bank rates can look even more dramatic than their base economics.

Some of the highest advertised rates are promotions.

Maya's current offer advertises **up to 15% a year**, but its base rate is 3%. The remaining boost depends on promotional conditions, applies only to qualifying users and is capped at a limited balance under the current campaign.

That makes the headline rate useful for attracting attention and moving money into the bank without requiring the bank to pay 15% on every depositor's full balance indefinitely.

Rates can also change as competition, funding needs and market conditions change. A high deposit rate is a product decision, not a permanent law of digital banking.

So why do digital banks pay so much more?

Because deposits are valuable to them.

Newer digital banks are competing to attract funding and customers, and they are willing to pay more to do it. Their higher-yielding asset mix gives them more room to absorb that funding cost. Operating without a traditional branch network helps, but current BSP data show that the business is more complicated than simply having lower overhead.

The high savings rate is not free money from the bank. It is the price the bank has chosen to pay for deposits and growth.
