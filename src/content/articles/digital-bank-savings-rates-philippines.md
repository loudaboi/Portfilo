[BDO](https://www.bdo.com.ph/personal/accounts/savings/peso-savings/atm-savings) and [BPI](https://www.bpi.com.ph/personal/bank/savings-accounts/regular-savings) currently list ordinary savings rates of **0.0625% a year**. [Maya Bank](https://www.mayabank.ph/highinterestsavings/) lists a **3% base rate** on Maya Savings.

That is a 48-fold difference before any promotional boost.

The usual explanation is that digital banks have no branches, so they spend less on rent, tellers and physical infrastructure and can pass some of those savings to depositors.

That is part of the answer. It is not the whole business model.

## A savings deposit is funding for the bank

Money in a savings account is not only sitting there waiting for the depositor to withdraw it. For the bank, deposits are also a source of funding.

That makes the interest rate a price.

A bank that wants more deposits can choose to pay more to attract them. That matters for newer digital banks that do not have the same long-established deposit bases as large incumbents.

Fitch said in 2023 that Philippine digital banks were competing aggressively for deposits while building scale, with many relying heavily on price to attract customers, [according to The Philippine Star](https://www.philstar.com/business/2023/12/05/2316427/price-focused-strategy-philippines-digital-banks-not-sustainable-fitch).

Current BSP data show that digital banks are still paying materially more for funding.

In June 2026, the [Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Statistics/Selected%20Performance%20Indicators/94_data.aspx) reported a **4.15% funding cost** for the Digital Bank Group. For universal and commercial banks, the comparable figure was **1.87%**.

Funding cost is broader than the rate on one savings account, but the comparison shows that higher deposit pricing is not just advertising. Digital banks as a group are actually paying more for the money they use to fund their businesses.

## They also earn more on the other side

Paying more for deposits makes more sense if the bank can also earn more from the assets those deposits help support.

That is what the current group-level data show.

In June 2026, the Digital Bank Group's **earning-asset yield was 16.97%**. For universal and commercial banks, it was **5.94%**.

That does not mean a digital bank takes a peso from your savings account, lends that same peso out at 16.97%, then pockets the difference. Banks hold different mixes of loans, securities, cash and other assets.

But it does show why the deposit rate cannot be understood on its own.

Digital banks are paying more for funding, while the assets on the other side of their balance sheets are also producing much higher yields as a group. That gives them more room to carry a higher cost of deposits than a traditional bank with a different asset mix.

## No branches does not mean cheap to run

Digital banks do avoid one major category of physical infrastructure.

Under [BSP Circular No. 1105](https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Regulations/Issuances/2020/c1105.pdf), a digital bank is a distinct regulatory class designed to provide financial products and services end-to-end through digital platforms without physical branches providing those services.

So the branch-cost advantage is real.

But the current numbers do not show a sector that is simply cheaper to operate.

In June 2026, the Digital Bank Group had a **75.39% cost-to-income ratio**, compared with **54.02%** for universal and commercial banks. The group was also still loss-making in aggregate, with BSP reporting an annualized net loss of about **P5.25 billion**.

Those are sector averages, not the financial condition of every individual digital bank. Some banks can perform better than the group.

Still, the broader point matters: higher savings rates are not just spare profit being handed back to depositors because an app costs less than a branch network.

Digital banks still spend on technology, compliance, customer acquisition, servicing, credit operations and growth.

## The biggest rates are often promotional

Some of the highest advertised rates also make the gap look larger than the normal base rate.

Maya currently advertises **up to 15% a year**, but its base rate is 3%. The additional interest depends on promotional conditions, applies only to qualifying users and is capped at a limited balance under the current campaign.

That lets the bank use a very high headline rate to attract attention and deposits without paying 15% on every depositor's full balance indefinitely.

Rates can also change as competition, funding needs and market conditions change. A high savings rate is a product decision, not a permanent feature of digital banking.

So why do digital banks pay so much more?

Because deposits are valuable to them.

Newer digital banks are competing for funding and customers, and they are willing to pay more to get them. Their higher-yielding asset mix gives them more room to absorb that cost. Operating without a traditional branch network helps, but current BSP data show that lower overhead is only part of the explanation.

The high savings rate is the price the bank has chosen to pay for deposits and growth.
