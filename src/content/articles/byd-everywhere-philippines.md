BYD sold 4,780 vehicles in the Philippines in 2024. A year later, it reported 26,122. By the end of August 2026, it had already sold 28,399 more.

That growth is large enough to explain why the brand feels much more visible than it did two years ago. But the more useful detail is what kind of BYDs people are actually buying.

Of the 28,399 vehicles BYD reported selling from January through August this year, 22,555 were DM-i plug-in hybrids. That is about **79% of its volume**. Full battery-electric vehicles accounted for the remaining 5,844.

So BYD's Philippine rise is not mainly a story about drivers switching directly from gasoline cars to fully electric ones. It is largely a story about plug-in hybrids becoming a practical bridge into electrified driving.

## The plug-in hybrid matters

A plug-in hybrid can run on battery power but retains a gasoline engine. That changes the tradeoff for buyers who are interested in lower fuel use but are not ready to depend entirely on charging.

That tradeoff lines up with broader Philippine consumer research. [BusinessWorld, reporting on Deloitte's 2026 automotive consumer study](https://www.bworldonline.com/economy/2026/05/21/751367/hybrids-evs-gain-momentum-as-energy-crisis-deepens-deloitte/), said 34% of Filipino respondents were interested in hybrid and electric vehicles, up from 27% a year earlier. Lower fuel costs were cited by 62% of respondents who preferred electrified vehicles, while 48% identified a lack of charging stations as a concern.

Those findings are not a survey of BYD buyers, so they cannot prove why a particular person chose a DM-i model. But they help explain why a vehicle that can use electricity without relying completely on public charging fits the conditions of the Philippine market.

BYD's own sales history points in the same direction. The Sealion 6 DM-i, launched locally in July 2024, accounted for 2,669 of BYD's 4,780 Philippine sales that year. In other words, the plug-in-hybrid part of the story was already visible before the 2026 fuel-price shock.

## BYD also moved into ordinary car-buying price ranges

BYD did not enter the market only with expensive, premium electric vehicles.

Its July 2026 Philippine price list included the Seal 5 DM-i Essential at **P948,000**, the Atto 2 DM-i Dynamic at **P1.048 million**, the Sealion 5 DM-i at **P1.268 million**, and the Sealion 6 DM-i at **P1.598 million**. The battery-electric Seagull was listed at **P968,000**. [BYD's current price list is published here](https://bydcarsphilippines.com/pricelist).

That does not mean every BYD is cheaper than a comparable gasoline or hybrid car. Vehicle size, equipment, warranty, financing, service coverage and resale expectations differ. The stronger point is that BYD now offers electrified vehicles across price bands that overlap with mainstream car shopping rather than confining them to a premium niche.

Philippine policy has also reduced some of the structural cost of selling electrified vehicles. [Executive Order 62](https://lawphil.net/executive/execord/eo2024/eo_62_2024.html) extended zero tariff treatment through 2028 to covered battery-electric, hybrid and plug-in-hybrid vehicles. The [Electric Vehicle Industry Development Act](https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra2022/ra_11697_2022.html) provides incentives including exemptions from vehicle volume reduction programs, while the tax code gives pure EVs and hybrids preferential automobile excise-tax treatment.

Those policies did not suddenly appear in 2026, so they do not explain the timing of BYD's surge by themselves. They are better understood as conditions that made aggressive pricing and adoption easier.

## The brand became physically harder to miss

Sales are only one part of why BYD now appears to be everywhere.

Its retail network expanded at exceptional speed. BYD later reported that it went from 25 dealerships at the end of 2024 to 79 operational dealerships at the end of 2025, then 81 in 2026. One Ayala SEC filing gives a slightly different end-2024 figure of 27, but either baseline leads to the same conclusion: the network roughly tripled in a year.

BYD has also repeatedly put vehicles in high-traffic locations through mall-based Tech Tour events and test-drive activations across Metro Manila, Laguna, Pampanga and Cebu.

That distinction matters. More BYDs on the road increase actual fleet visibility. More dealerships, mall displays and test-drive events increase brand visibility even before every display turns into a sale.

ACMobility, Ayala's mobility platform and BYD's Philippine distributor, also gave the brand an existing structure for dealerships, parts, service, financing connections and charging infrastructure. That does not prove Ayala's involvement caused people to buy BYDs. It does mean BYD did not have to build every part of its Philippine retail and ownership network from scratch.

## BYD is benefiting from a market shift bigger than BYD

The broader Philippine vehicle market is also moving toward electrified powertrains.

Among CAMPI and TMA member brands, which do not include BYD, sales of electrified vehicles rose **132.7% in the first half of 2026** even as total vehicle sales among those members fell 11.4%, [according to industry data reported by BusinessMirror](https://businessmirror.com.ph/2026/07/23/vehicle-sales-retreat-as-oil-prices-surge-in-january-june/).

That matters because it prevents an easy but misleading explanation. BYD did not create the entire shift toward hybrids and EVs. Other brands are benefiting from it too.

The 2026 fuel-price shock probably added pressure in the same direction. Philippine automotive industry officials have linked higher fuel costs to changing vehicle preferences this year. But BYD's major jump happened in 2025, and its best-selling plug-in hybrid was already driving growth in 2024. Fuel prices can help explain why demand accelerated in 2026. They cannot explain where the rise started.

There is also a global supply-side context. BYD has been expanding overseas at the same time China's domestic car market has weakened. [Reuters reported](https://www.reuters.com/business/autos-transportation/byds-sales-extend-growth-streak-strong-exports-2026-09-01/) that BYD shipped 189,466 vehicles overseas in August 2026, up 134.5% from a year earlier. More than half of its first-half revenue came from overseas operations.

That shows the Philippines is part of a much larger international expansion. It does not establish that Chinese market pressure caused BYD's Philippine pricing or local demand.

## What "everywhere" is really capturing

There is no single number that can tell us exactly how much of BYD's Philippine rise came from its plug-in-hybrid technology, prices, dealerships, policy incentives, fuel costs or global expansion strategy.

The evidence does show that several things happened at once.

BYD found most of its Philippine volume in plug-in hybrids rather than full EVs. It put those vehicles into mainstream price ranges. Its distributor multiplied the number of places where buyers could see, test, buy and service them. At the same time, Filipino consumers became more interested in electrified vehicles generally, while fuel costs and public-charging limitations made the middle ground offered by plug-in hybrids more relevant.

So when BYD suddenly seems to be everywhere, part of that impression is more cars on the road. Part of it is a much larger retail and marketing footprint. And beneath both is a product mix that happens to fit a market that wants some of the economics of electric driving without having to make the full leap to a battery-only car.
