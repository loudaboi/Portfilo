## Research question

> Who ultimately pays when qualifying small-merchant digital payments become free to the merchant?

This is a research question, not an assumption that costs are shifted in one particular way.

## Reader payoff

Explain the economics underneath the new payment-pricing rules instead of repeating the announcement. Readers should understand which fees are regulated, which costs remain, how providers can respond, and what the change means for qualifying merchants and the payment system.

## Working hypothesis

**Hypothesis, not conclusion:** Eliminating an explicit merchant-facing fee does not eliminate the underlying cost of processing and operating the payment rail. The economic burden may be absorbed, cross-subsidized, repriced elsewhere, reduced through scale/efficiency, or distributed differently across providers and users.

## Disproof condition

The hypothesis should be revised if the BSP/PPMI rules or provider economics show that the relevant processing cost is effectively eliminated rather than redistributed, or if provider-level evidence is too opaque to determine incidence beyond theoretical possibilities.

## Scope

Focus on the 2026 Philippine rules affecting qualifying small-merchant payments and the providers/businesses directly subject to or affected by them.

The story must distinguish:

- regulated transfer/merchant fees from unrelated service charges;
- merchant-facing price from provider processing cost;
- explicit charges from indirect recovery mechanisms;
- observed provider behavior from theoretical incentives;
- rule requirements from voluntary pricing choices.

## Potential evidence

- BSP circulars, memoranda, FAQs, and official releases;
- PPMI rules/documentation where available;
- bank, e-wallet, and payment-provider fee schedules;
- merchant-acquiring or payment-gateway documentation;
- provider financial statements and payment economics where sufficiently granular;
- interviews with payment providers, merchants, economists, and payments specialists;
- transparent calculations showing which conclusions are observed and which are modeled.
