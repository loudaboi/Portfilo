## Original research question

This question passed launch selection, but the first full public-evidence pass found that its premise is not defined precisely enough to support drafting.

## Current research outcome

`REFRAME`

### Reframed working question

> What does `zero fees for small merchant payments` actually mean under the BSP's 2026 payment rules?

This is now the active C017 research question.

The original economic-incidence question remains a possible second layer or follow-on if the zero-fee implementation can first be established clearly.

## Why the question changed

The public record contains a material mismatch that must be resolved before Portfilo can responsibly ask who bears the cost.

### 1. The 2024 exposure draft was explicit

The BSP's 2024 exposure draft proposed:

- a `micro-merchant` definition;
- a PHP250,000 monthly aggregate gross-receipts threshold;
- an explicit waiver of merchant fees for classified micro-merchants.

### 2. The final 2026 circular is materially different

Signed Circular No. 1238 does not reproduce that micro-merchant definition or explicit waiver in the reviewed text.

Instead, its merchant-acquisition pricing provision says merchant fees may be charged and requires those fees to be reasonable, transparent, market-based, proportionate to service cost, and supported by cost analysis.

### 3. A same-day BSP memorandum still says zero fees have been implemented

Memorandum No. M-2026-025 says the lifting of the InstaPay/PESONet fee-increase moratorium is grounded in the `implementation of zero fees for small merchant payments`.

The memorandum does not identify the eligibility definition, payment rail, waived fee layer, or implementation instrument.

### 4. Standard public merchant pricing is still nonzero

Current public QR/QRPh pricing from GCash, Maya, and PayMongo displays nonzero merchant rates.

Those price cards do **not** prove that a qualifying small-merchant subset lacks special treatment. They do show that a universal interpretation of `all small merchants now pay zero merchant fees` cannot be assumed from standard public pricing.

## Reader payoff

The reframed story should tell readers what the 2026 policy actually covers rather than repeat ambiguous shorthand.

A successful answer should establish, as specifically as the evidence permits:

- who qualifies;
- what `small merchant` means in the operative rule;
- which payment type or rail is covered;
- which fee is zero;
- who would otherwise pay it;
- what document or clearing arrangement implements the rule;
- how the policy relates to merchant MDRs still shown on provider websites.

Only after those mechanics are clear should Portfilo assess the underlying economic incidence.

## Working hypothesis

**Hypothesis, not conclusion:** the phrase `zero fees for small merchant payments` refers to a narrower implementation condition than a blanket prohibition on merchant payment fees, potentially through a rule or clearing arrangement not stated in the merchant-pricing text of Circular 1238 itself.

This hypothesis must be discarded if the authoritative implementation record shows otherwise.

## Disproof conditions

Reframe again or stop if:

- the phrase is only descriptive policy language and there is no distinct operative zero-fee treatment that can be documented;
- available official material is too ambiguous to establish who qualifies or what charge is waived;
- the relevant rule is not public and no sufficiently authoritative clarification is available;
- current provider pricing cannot be compared meaningfully with the covered payment arrangement;
- a definitive existing source already explains the mechanics comprehensively, leaving no material Portfilo contribution.

## Original C017 economic question

The initial hypothesis remains economically plausible: making a merchant-facing payment price zero would not eliminate all costs of processing the payment.

BSP documents themselves identify cost categories and recognize cost allocation/cross-subsidy mechanisms.

However, the packet currently cannot establish **which cost is being waived for which merchants**. Therefore it cannot yet establish who ultimately bears it.

## Story-decision rationale

### Why `REFRAME`, not `PUBLISH`

The original question assumes a sufficiently defined zero-fee regime. That assumption is not supported cleanly enough by the current final rule set and public provider pricing.

### Why `REFRAME`, not `KILL`

The contradiction produces a sharper and more foundational information gap. Readers first need a verified explanation of what the BSP's `zero fees for small merchant payments` language actually means.
