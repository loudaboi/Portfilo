## Original research question

> Who ultimately pays when qualifying small-merchant digital payments become free to the merchant?

The first full public-evidence pass found that this premise was not defined precisely enough to support drafting.

## Current research outcome

`REFRAME / CONTINUE`

### Reframed working question

> What does `zero fees for small merchant payments` actually mean under the BSP's 2026 payment rules?

The original economic-incidence question remains a possible second layer or follow-on only after the zero-fee implementation is established clearly.

## What the second public-document pass established

A dedicated implementation-basis audit is now recorded in `implementation-basis.md`.

The strongest current conclusions are:

1. Signed BSP Circular No. 1238 permits Operators of Payment Systems with Merchant Acquisition Licenses to charge merchant fees when pricing is reasonable, transparent, market-based, proportionate to service cost, and supported by cost analysis.
2. The final circular does not reproduce the `micro-merchant` definition or explicit fee-waiver clause contained in the 2024 exposure draft.
3. BSP Memorandum No. M-2026-025 nevertheless says the lifting of the InstaPay/PESONet fee moratorium is grounded in the `implementation of zero fees for small merchant payments`, without defining the qualifying merchant, payment rail, fee layer, or implementation instrument.
4. BSP Memorandum No. M-2024-015 describes zero-fee P2M payments for microenterprises as an inclusive-pricing practice, while also saying merchant fees may be charged under clearing rules and payment costs may be cross-subsidized.
5. Current PPMI material says QR Ph P2M is free to consumers.
6. BSP's 29 July 2026 BSP/PPMI launch material separately says merchants shoulder QR Ph transaction fees.
7. Current public QR/QR Ph provider pricing remains nonzero for standard merchant products.

These points make it unsafe to state that Circular 1238 itself created a universal merchant-side zero-fee rule or that the PHP250,000 threshold in the 2024 exposure draft automatically survived into the final framework.

## Decisive unresolved question

The currently indexed public BSP/PPMI record reviewed does not identify the final operative instrument that explains the phrase in M-2026-025.

At least three interpretations remain open:

- consumer-side fee-free QR Ph P2M payments;
- a merchant-side microenterprise waiver implemented through a separate BSP/PPMI/ACH/participant arrangement;
- policy shorthand carried forward from the earlier zero-fee design.

Portfilo must not choose among these without authoritative support.

## Reader payoff

A successful story should tell readers what the 2026 policy actually covers rather than repeat ambiguous shorthand.

The story should establish, as specifically as the evidence permits:

- who qualifies;
- what `small merchant` means in the operative framework;
- which payment type or rail is covered;
- which fee is zero;
- who would otherwise pay it;
- what document or clearing arrangement implements the rule;
- how the policy relates to merchant MDRs still shown on provider websites.

Only after those mechanics are clear should Portfilo assess the underlying economic incidence.

## Working hypothesis

**Hypothesis, not conclusion:** the phrase `zero fees for small merchant payments` refers to a narrower implementation condition than a blanket prohibition on merchant payment fees, potentially involving a fee layer or industry arrangement not stated in Circular 1238's merchant-pricing text.

This hypothesis must be discarded if an authoritative implementation record shows otherwise.

## Disproof conditions

Reframe again or stop if:

- the phrase is only descriptive policy language and there is no distinct operative zero-fee treatment that can be documented;
- authoritative clarification shows that the story has been framed around the wrong fee layer;
- available official material remains too ambiguous to establish who qualifies or what charge is waived;
- the relevant rule cannot be established to a standard suitable for publication;
- a definitive existing source explains the mechanics comprehensively, leaving no material Portfilo contribution.

## Original C017 economic question

The initial hypothesis remains economically plausible: making a payment price zero for one party would not eliminate all underlying payment-system costs.

BSP documents themselves identify cost categories and recognize cost allocation/cross-subsidy mechanisms.

However, the packet still cannot establish **which price is zero for which merchant/payment segment**. Therefore it cannot yet establish who ultimately bears the cost.

## Story-decision rationale

### Why `REFRAME / CONTINUE`, not `PUBLISH`

The public record now establishes the documentary mismatch well, but not the final intended meaning of `zero fees for small merchant payments` in M-2026-025.

Publishing now would require choosing among competing interpretations or claiming that no separate implementation exists. Neither is supported strongly enough.

### Why not `KILL`

The contradiction creates a sharper information gap, and several public summaries appear to state more than Circular 1238 itself contains. A narrow authoritative clarification could convert the packet into a strong document-driven explainer quickly.
