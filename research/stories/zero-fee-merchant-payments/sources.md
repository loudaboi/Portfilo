# C017 Source Inventory - Zero-Fee Small-Merchant Payments

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Source policy

This file is a working source inventory under `docs/RESEARCH_STANDARD.md`.

The assignment began with the question:

> Who ultimately pays when qualifying small-merchant digital payments become free to the merchant?

The first public-evidence pass found that the premise itself needs verification. The signed 2026 circular, the accompanying memorandum, the earlier exposure draft, and current public merchant pricing do not line up cleanly enough to assume a universal zero-fee merchant regime.

Access date for sources below: 2026-09-03 unless otherwise stated.

## S001 - BSP Circular No. 1238, Series of 2026

**Source:** Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas  
**URL:** https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Regulations/Issuances/2026/1238.pdf  
**Type / tier:** Signed regulatory issuance / Tier A  
**Date:** 2026-06-17

**Why it matters:** This is the controlling signed circular for the 2026 amendments to the National Retail Payment System Framework and the Regulatory Framework for Merchant Payment Acceptance Activities.

**Relevant provisions:**

- BSFIs must support payment fees with analysis of actual costs;
- off-us P2P EFT pricing should not materially differ from on-us pricing plus directly attributable switch cost;
- electronic-payment fees are expected to be below manual/OTC fees;
- P2P recipients must receive the full amount without deduction;
- Ops-MALs may charge merchant fees, but the pricing must be reasonable, transparent, market-based, proportionate to service cost, and supported by cost analysis;
- Appendix 201-2 identifies cost categories including authorization, infrastructure, IT, clearing, switch, settlement, security/fraud, marketing, customer support, and overhead.

**Important limitation:** The signed circular does **not** contain the micro-merchant definition or explicit micro-merchant fee-waiver clause that appeared in the 2024 exposure draft.

## S002 - BSP Memorandum No. M-2026-025

**Source:** Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas  
**URL:** https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Regulations/Issuances/2026/M-2026-025.pdf  
**Type / tier:** Official memorandum / Tier A  
**Date:** 2026-06-17

**Why it matters:** Lifts the moratorium on increases in InstaPay and PESONet fees concurrently with Circular 1238.

**Relevant statement:** The memorandum says the lifting is grounded in the **implementation of zero fees for small merchant payments** and the establishment of a P2P pricing structure.

**Important limitation:** The memorandum does not identify the operational rule, clearing rule, eligibility test, payment rail, or provider implementation through which the stated small-merchant zero-fee condition is established.

## S003 - BSP Memorandum No. M-2024-015, Guidelines on Pricing of Electronic Payments

**Source:** Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas  
**URL:** https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Regulations/Issuances/2024/M-2024-015.pdf  
**Type / tier:** Official regulatory guidance / Tier A  
**Date:** 2024-05-24

**Why it matters:** Provides the BSP's earlier framework for how electronic-payment costs may be recovered and distributed.

**Relevant provisions:**

- merchant fees may be charged for P2M or biller EFTs under agreed clearing rules;
- providers should use cost analysis and appropriate cost allocation;
- payment-service costs may be recovered from revenues generated by related or linked services;
- for banks, e-payments can be treated as ancillary services and subsidized by income from main products/services;
- inclusive pricing practices such as zero fees for small P2M payments to microenterprises are presented as practices that may widen adoption and support long-run system sustainability.

**Use:** Strong evidence that removing one visible payment fee does not imply the underlying payment service has zero economic cost, and that cross-subsidy is an explicitly contemplated mechanism.

## S004 - 2024 BSP exposure draft on zero fees

**Source:** Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas  
**URL:** https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Regulations/Issuances%20of%20Policy%20Exposure%20Drafts/Exposure_Draft_Circular_on_the_Policy_on_Zero_Fees.pdf  
**Type / tier:** Official exposure draft / Tier A for proposal history, not final law  
**Date:** 2024 exposure draft

**Why it matters:** The draft explicitly proposed:

- a `micro-merchant` definition tied to micro-enterprise status and monthly aggregate gross receipts not exceeding PHP250,000;
- fee-free personal P2P transactions within a proposed threshold;
- waiver of merchant fees for classified micro-merchants;
- a compliance date of 2025-04-01.

**Important limitation:** This is not the final 2026 rule. The explicit micro-merchant definition and waiver clause shown in this draft do not appear in signed Circular 1238.

## S012 - PayMongo 2026 BSP merchant-rule tracker

**Source:** PayMongo

**Type / tier:** Industry interpretation / Tier C

**Important limitation:** PayMongo is a regulated payment provider with commercial interests. Its interpretation is useful as a lead and cross-check, not controlling legal authority.

## Immediate source gap

The public record still lacks one decisive item:

> the authoritative rule, clearing arrangement, BSP/PPMI implementation document, or official explanatory text that states exactly which `small merchant payments` are zero-fee, who qualifies, which party's fee is waived, which payment rail is covered, and how the condition coexists with current public merchant MDR schedules.

Until that is established, the original C017 question should not assume that every qualifying small merchant is universally receiving digital payments without merchant charges.
