## Reader payoff

Explain the business mechanics behind the unusually large savings-rate gap between BSP-licensed digital banks and ordinary savings accounts at large traditional banks.

The useful answer should distinguish:

- an ordinary base savings rate from a promotional or conditional headline rate;
- lower branch-related costs from the full operating-cost picture;
- deposit pricing from the yields banks earn on loans and other earning assets;
- customer-acquisition strategy from a permanent structural advantage;
- sector averages from the economics of any one digital bank;
- BSP-licensed digital banks from other banks that operate mainly through apps but belong to a different regulatory bank class.

## Working hypothesis

The rate gap appears to come from several mechanisms rather than one simple cost advantage:

1. **Digital banks compete aggressively for deposits.** They are newer franchises without the same legacy deposit bases as large incumbent banks, so a higher savings rate can function as both funding cost and customer-acquisition spend.
2. **Their assets earn much higher yields.** BSP data show the digital-bank group carrying a much higher earning-asset yield and net interest margin than universal and commercial banks, giving the sector more room to pay for deposits.
3. **Branchless distribution removes some physical-network costs, but it does not make digital banks cheap overall.** Current BSP data show the digital-bank group still has a higher cost-to-income ratio and remains loss-making in aggregate.
4. **The biggest advertised rates are often narrow promotions.** Maya's current headline rate, for example, is built on a lower base rate and bonus conditions with a balance cap.
5. **Deposit rates are adjustable.** Banks can reprice them as funding needs, competition, policy rates and customer-acquisition economics change.

This is a working mechanism, not a claim that every digital bank follows the same strategy or has the same economics.

## Disproof / reframe conditions

The article must narrow or change if research shows that:

- the current rate gap is not material;
- higher digital-bank rates are mostly stale promotional claims rather than currently available products;
- BSP group data cannot support a meaningful comparison of funding cost and earning-asset yield;
- the evidence supports only the generic branch-cost explanation and adds no Philippine-specific insight;
- the relationship between higher asset yields and higher deposit pricing is too indirect to explain responsibly;
- formal digital-bank classification is too easily confused with app-first banks outside the BSP digital-bank group.

If the economics cannot be explained without overstating causality, the piece should become a narrower explainer about **how deposit rates are priced** rather than why digital banks can always pay more.

## Scope

Geography:
- Philippines.

Period:
- primarily 2025 through September 2026;
- earlier material only where needed to explain the digital-bank framework or long-running pricing strategy.

Population / institutions:
- BSP-licensed digital banks;
- large universal/commercial banks used only as reference points for ordinary savings-rate economics.

Key terms:
- **Digital bank:** the BSP regulatory class defined in Circular No. 1105 as a bank offering financial products and services end-to-end through digital platforms/electronic channels without physical branches or branch-lite units offering financial products and services.
- **Base savings rate:** the rate available without completing additional promotional missions or meeting temporary campaign conditions.
- **Funding cost:** the cost a bank pays on interest-bearing liabilities and other funding.
- **Earning-asset yield:** interest income relative to average earning assets.
- **Net interest margin:** net interest income relative to average earning assets.

Exclusions:
- do not rank the "best" digital bank;
- do not recommend where readers should place savings;
- do not imply that the highest advertised rate applies to all balances or all users;
- do not call a digital bank safer or riskier solely from its savings rate;
- do not imply that branchlessness alone explains current pricing;
- do not treat sector averages as the financial statements of an individual bank;
- do not conflate CIMB, SeaBank, OwnBank or other app-first banks with the BSP's formal digital-bank group unless their regulatory classification is explicitly identified.

## Existing coverage / contribution

Existing Philippine explainers often reduce the answer to:

> no branches -> lower operating costs -> higher savings rates.

That is directionally useful but incomplete.

Portfilo's contribution is to test that explanation against current banking data. The June 2026 BSP numbers show a more interesting picture: digital banks pay materially more for funding and earn materially more on earning assets, while their aggregate cost-to-income ratio remains worse than large banks and the group is still loss-making.

The piece can therefore explain high savings rates as a **business-model and funding decision**, not simply as money saved on rent and tellers.

## Research outcome

**PUBLISH / SUBJECT RESEARCH COMPLETE**

The evidence is strong enough for a concise Why article.

The strongest defensible thesis is:

> Philippine digital banks pay more for deposits because deposits are a competitive source of funding and customer acquisition for newer banks, while their higher-yielding asset mix gives them more room to absorb that cost. Branchless operations help, but current BSP data show that "lower overhead" is not the whole explanation.
