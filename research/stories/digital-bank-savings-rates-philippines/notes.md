# Philippine Digital Bank Savings Rates Research Notes

_Last updated: 2026-09-11_

## Current working answer

Do not treat this as article copy.

The research suggests that high digital-bank savings rates are best understood as part of the bank's funding and growth model.

A newer bank needs deposits. A high savings rate gives people a concrete reason to move cash into an unfamiliar institution. That makes the interest expense partly the price of funding and partly the price of acquiring/retaining customers.

Current BSP data show that digital banks are indeed paying more for funding, but they are also earning much more on their assets:

June 2026:
- digital-bank funding cost: 4.15%
- universal/commercial-bank funding cost: 1.87%
- digital-bank earning-asset yield: 16.97%
- universal/commercial-bank earning-asset yield: 5.94%
- digital-bank NIM: 12.84%
- universal/commercial-bank NIM: 4.28%

The simple "no branches" explanation is incomplete because digital banks still had:
- 75.39% cost-to-income ratio;
- negative 3.14% annualized ROA;
- negative P5.252 billion annualized group profit/loss.

So the useful realization is not:

> digital banks have low costs, so they give the savings back to customers.

It is closer to:

> digital banks are willing to pay more for deposits because deposits help them fund and grow the franchise, and their higher-yielding asset mix gives them more room to carry that funding cost. Branchless distribution helps, but the current sector is not broadly cheap or highly profitable.

## Useful calculations

### Ordinary savings-rate gap

Maya base vs BDO/BPI ordinary savings:

3.00 / 0.0625 = **48x**

Tonik Solo Stash vs BDO/BPI ordinary savings:

4.00 / 0.0625 = **64x**

UNO 3.50 vs BDO/BPI ordinary savings:

3.50 / 0.0625 = **56x**

These are rate ratios only. They should not be presented as a total-product ranking.

### Funding cost

4.15 / 1.87 = **2.22x**

The digital-bank group's funding cost was about 2.2 times the universal/commercial-bank group's in June 2026.

### Earning-asset yield

16.97 / 5.94 = **2.86x**

### Net interest margin

12.84 / 4.28 = **3.00x**

### Deposit deployment

82.462 / 171.545 = **48.07%**

This matches BSP's reported loans-to-deposits ratio.

## Strongest possible article spine

A concise Why could likely move through four ideas:

1. **The rate gap is genuinely large**
   - establish the current contrast using base/ordinary savings rates, not promo hype.

2. **Deposits are something digital banks are competing to buy**
   - deposits are funding;
   - newer franchises have reason to pay more to attract them;
   - interest can double as acquisition/retention economics.

3. **Their asset side looks different too**
   - higher funding cost;
   - much higher earning-asset yield;
   - wider NIM;
   - do not imply risk-free arbitrage.

4. **"No branches" helps, but does not explain the whole business**
   - no physical branch network by regulatory design;
   - yet aggregate cost-to-income is higher and the group is loss-making;
   - large headline rates are often capped/conditional and can be repriced.

Possible ending realization:
The high rate is not a gift. It is a price the bank is choosing to pay for deposits and growth.

Do not use that sentence as final copy without the normal drafting gate.

## Alternative angles considered

### "Why are digital banks growing so fast in the Philippines?"
Too broad. It would combine convenience, financial inclusion, rate competition, payments, credit and regulation into one loose article.

### "Why are Philippine digital banks still losing money?"
Strong business question, supported by current BSP data, but less immediately recognizable to ordinary readers and complicated by individual banks already reaching profitability.

Could become a later Why if the group remains loss-making while more individual banks report profits.

### "Why is the BSP allowing more digital banks?"
More regulatory/news-driven. Useful, but less connected to an everyday reader observation than the savings-rate gap.

## Formal classification note

The article should use the BSP definition of Digital Banks.

As of current BSP material, the six authorized digital banks are:
- GoTyme Bank
- Maya Bank
- Overseas Filipino Bank
- Tonik Digital Bank
- UnionDigital Bank
- UNObank

Do not casually include CIMB, SeaBank, OwnBank or similar institutions in formal Digital Bank Group comparisons unless their bank classification is stated separately.
