# Philippine Digital Bank Savings Rates Contradiction Search

_Last updated: 2026-09-11_

## Purpose

Challenge the easy explanation before drafting.

## C1 - Lower branch costs are not the whole answer

The common explanation is that digital banks have no branches, therefore they have lower overhead, therefore they can pay savers more.

The first two steps are directionally plausible, and Philippine policy material has used that logic. But current BSP data show the digital-bank group with a 75.39% cost-to-income ratio in June 2026 versus 54.02% for universal/commercial banks.

Implication:
Do not write that digital banks are simply "cheaper to run." Say they avoid a physical branch network, while the current sector still carries substantial technology, acquisition, servicing, compliance, credit and scaling costs.

## C2 - A higher earning-asset yield is not free money

Digital banks' earning-asset yield was 16.97% in June 2026 versus 5.94% for the universal/commercial bank group.

That gives more room for a higher funding cost, but high yields can come with different asset mixes and risk.

Digital-bank consumer loans had materially higher delinquency/NPL ratios than the large-bank group in current BSP data.

Implication:
Do not imply that digital banks have discovered a riskless spread. Their economics involve higher-yielding assets and higher credit/operating costs.

## C3 - High NIM does not equal high profitability

Digital-bank NIM was 12.84% in June 2026, about three times the large-bank group's 4.28%.

Yet the digital-bank group still reported an annualized loss and negative ROA.

Implication:
Do not say high savings rates are easily funded from enormous profits. Interest spread is only one part of the income statement.

## C4 - The biggest advertised rate is often not the normal rate

Maya's current "up to 15%" offer is built on a 3% base plus a promotional boost with qualification rules, a P100,000 cap and a limited eligibility period.

UNO also runs bonus-rate campaigns tied to fresh funds and targeted customers.

Implication:
Use base rates when explaining structural pricing. Use promotional rates only to explain customer-acquisition mechanics.

## C5 - Not every "digital bank" in consumer conversation is a BSP digital bank

CIMB, SeaBank, OwnBank and similar app-first institutions often appear on lists of "digital banks" even though the BSP has a specific six-bank Digital Bank Group.

Implication:
Define the formal regulatory class. Do not mix group-level BSP data with institutions outside that group and then draw institution-level conclusions.

## C6 - Group averages hide very different strategies

Maya, Tonik, UNO, GoTyme, UnionDigital and OFBank do not have identical products, customer bases, funding needs or lending strategies.

Some individual digital banks have reported profitability while the formal sector remains loss-making in aggregate.

Implication:
Keep the article at the level of sector mechanics unless an institution-specific financial statement supports a narrower claim.

## C7 - Traditional banks can also offer high-yield or digital savings products

The relevant contrast is not "digital always high, traditional always low."

Large banks may offer time deposits, promotional accounts or digitally opened products with different yields.

Implication:
Compare ordinary liquid savings products when demonstrating the gap. Do not generalize from one account type to every deposit product.

## C8 - Deposit pricing moves with market conditions

Bank deposit rates are not permanent product constants. Policy rates, competition, liquidity, funding needs and growth strategy can change them.

Recent digital-bank rate changes and promotions show repricing in practice.

Implication:
Any numeric comparison is time-sensitive and must be rechecked immediately before publication.

## C9 - Deposit insurance does not answer every safety question

PDIC coverage up to P1 million applies per depositor, per bank for insured deposits.

Implication:
The article can explain insurance if readers may mistake a high rate for an unregulated product, but it should not turn into a broad safety endorsement or financial recommendation.

## Current adversarial conclusion

The easy story is too simple.

The stronger explanation is:

- branchless distribution removes some physical-network cost;
- newer digital banks compete harder for deposits and customers;
- they are willing to carry a higher funding cost;
- their earning assets generate much higher yields and wider interest margins;
- promotional rates can be tightly capped or behavior-linked;
- high operating and credit costs mean the sector can still lose money despite the wide spread.

That mechanism is more defensible than "digital banks pay more because they have no branches."
