## Reader payoff

Explain why a Chinese electrified-vehicle brand went from a marginal Philippine presence to one of the most visible car brands in roughly two years, without reducing the answer to `EVs are popular`.

The useful answer should distinguish:

- growth in BYD specifically from growth in electrified vehicles generally;
- battery-electric demand from plug-in-hybrid demand;
- structural enablers such as tariffs and EVIDA incentives from 2026-specific accelerants;
- physical visibility from dealership expansion versus actual retail sales;
- company claims about value, reliability and ownership cost from independently established facts.

## Working hypothesis

BYD's rapid Philippine rise is probably a multi-factor result rather than one cause.

The current hypothesis is that four mechanisms matter most:

1. **Product-market fit:** BYD's Super DM-i plug-in hybrids let buyers adopt electrified driving without depending completely on public charging.
2. **Mainstream pricing:** BYD placed several electrified models near or below familiar mainstream-car price points.
3. **Distribution and ownership infrastructure:** ACMobility rapidly expanded dealerships, after-sales touchpoints, charging and financing access.
4. **Policy and market timing:** zero tariffs, EVIDA incentives and the 2026 fuel-price shock improved the relative appeal of electrified vehicles while BYD was already expanding globally.

A fifth mechanism, BYD's global export push amid weak Chinese domestic demand, may explain supply-side urgency but must not be treated as proof of why Filipino buyers chose the brand.

This is a hypothesis, not a conclusion.

## Disproof / reframe conditions

The thesis must be narrowed or changed if research shows that:

- BYD's growth is mostly explained by dealership count rather than demand;
- plug-in-hybrid dominance is unique to BYD and does not reflect a broader Philippine preference for reduced charging dependence;
- price comparisons do not hold after comparing genuinely similar vehicles;
- fuel-price timing does not line up with the sales acceleration;
- policy incentives are too old or too weak to explain the recent surge;
- ACMobility's distribution role cannot be tied to reduced adoption friction beyond company assertions;
- independent evidence cannot support a causal ranking among the proposed mechanisms.

If the causal answer remains too uncertain, reframe to a narrower evidence-led question such as why BYD's Philippine growth is overwhelmingly being driven by plug-in hybrids rather than full EVs.

## Initial contribution test

Existing Philippine coverage is strong on BYD sales milestones, launches and product reviews. It is weaker on combining the following into one defensible causal explanation:

- BYD's 2024-2026 sales trajectory;
- the dominance of DM-i plug-in hybrids in that growth;
- the unusually fast dealership build-out;
- current mainstream price positioning;
- the broader 2026 surge in electrified vehicles;
- policy incentives and the fuel-price shock;
- BYD's simultaneous global export push.

The potential Portfilo contribution is a synthesis that tests which of those mechanisms actually explain the visible Philippine phenomenon rather than repeating BYD's own explanation.

## Evidence threshold review

The subject-research threshold is sufficiently cleared for a carefully qualified Why story:

- the 2024, 2025 and January-August 2026 BYD sales sequence is verified;
- the 2026 DM-i versus BEV mix is source-traceable and calculated;
- the rapid dealership expansion is verified, with the 25-versus-27 end-2024 discrepancy preserved;
- current pricing supports a limited claim that BYD competes across mainstream price bands, not a blanket `BYD is cheaper` claim;
- EVIDA, tariff and excise-tax treatment are verified from primary legal/government sources;
- broader Philippine xEV growth is established independently of BYD;
- Deloitte's 2026 Philippine consumer findings independently support fuel-cost motivation, charging friction and increasing openness to alternative powertrains;
- the 2026 fuel-price shock remains an attributed accelerant rather than a proven root cause;
- BYD's global export push remains supply-side context rather than a proven cause of Philippine demand;
- unsupported buyer-specific claims about trust, reliability, financing preference or exact purchase motivation are excluded unless further direct reporting establishes them;
- meaningful counterevidence and alternative explanations are recorded.

The remaining source gaps are optional strengthening opportunities rather than blockers if the story stays within those limits.

## Research outcome

**PUBLISH**

This is a research outcome only. It does not authorize Draft 1, website publication, or release.

The evidence supports a useful Why story built around a non-obvious correction: BYD's Philippine surge is predominantly a plug-in-hybrid story, inside a broader shift toward electrified vehicles. The strongest defensible explanation is layered rather than monocausal: DM-i product fit, rapid physical distribution, mainstream price positioning, favorable policy, broader electrified-vehicle demand, a 2026 fuel-cost accelerant, and BYD's wider overseas expansion.

Exact causal weights remain unknowable from the available evidence and must not be fabricated.
