## Primary-source verification spot check

Verification:

Confirmed by U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics Business Employment Dynamics material.

Important boundary:

These are **establishments**, not founders, and survival does not reveal motive for closure or equal success.

Draft claim:

Confirmed by NBER Working Paper 34755.

Important boundary:

The stronger funding/employment/revenue comparison is **conditional on survival**. It must not be written as if shutdown itself caused superior later performance.

## Main weaknesses

### 1. The first section overgeneralizes from startup practitioner frameworks

Current motion:

Graham, Blank, and Altman are introduced correctly as startup practitioners, but the draft then says the distinction between product creation and business discovery `survives beyond venture-backed technology companies`.

Problem:

The broader research packet supports learning under uncertainty outside Silicon Valley. It does **not** independently establish that Graham-style manual user recruitment or every specific startup operating practice generalizes across restaurants, retailers, agencies, franchises, or other business forms.

Revision:

- keep Graham/Blank/Altman explicitly scoped to startup practice;
- use them to illustrate the proposition that product work can be only one part of startup formation;
- let the later empirical entrepreneurship research, not the famous-founder material, carry the broader uncertainty claim;
- do not imply one early-stage operating method applies to every business.

Severity: **MATERIAL**.

### 2. Several sentences treat noisy observations as stronger evidence than they are

Examples include the current sequence around:

- a rejected proposal exposing a price problem;
- a direct sales conversation revealing an offer problem;
- a manual process showing what should be automated;
- `turn assumptions into evidence`.

Problem:

Any one customer conversation, rejection, or manual workflow can be noisy or misleading. Those events can produce information, but they do not necessarily establish the underlying cause.

Revision:

Prefer language such as:

- `produce information`;
- `test an assumption`;
- `provide a signal that needs more evidence`.

Avoid presenting one observation as if it resolves demand, pricing, or product questions.

Severity: **MATERIAL**.

### 3. `The least glamorous work can carry the most information` is too strong

Problem:

`Most information` is an unsupported comparative claim.

Revision:

Reduce to the narrower point that some manual customer-facing work can produce useful information before scalable systems exist.

Severity: **MINOR BUT REQUIRED**.

### 4. The Philippine evidence block is too source-dense

Draft 1 currently uses:

- Enterprise Survey obstacles;
- Growth and Jobs Report constraints;
- COMPETE for SMEs constraints;
- multiple caveats around survey coverage.

Problem:

The reader only needs enough local evidence to understand that business experimentation occurs inside real operating constraints. The current accumulation starts to resemble a literature review.

The COMPETE material is especially more relevant to SME upgrading/competitiveness than to formation-stage uncertainty.

Revision:

- make the Enterprise Survey the main concrete Philippine source;
- use the Growth and Jobs Report only if it adds a materially different structural point;
- cut or greatly compress COMPETE unless Draft 2 needs its specific skills/finance evidence;
- state the Enterprise Survey population boundary once, precisely;
- avoid naming a specific tiny-business archetype as shorthand for everything outside the sample.

Severity: **MATERIAL**.

### 5. The stress section is accurate but longer than its explanatory job

Problem:

The story is not primarily about founder mental health. Two recent studies plus an older satisfaction counterpoint risk pulling the article into a separate literature review.

Revision:

- keep enough evidence to establish that entrepreneurial work can create recovery/stress costs;
- one strong study plus a concise balancing sentence may be enough;
- retain the distinction that hardship does not equal uniform misery;
- do not imply all founders experience the same stress level.

Severity: **STRUCTURAL / COMPRESSION**.

### 6. The BLS survival material should be simpler

Current draft uses the 34.7% ten-year figure and then adds that several five-year cohorts are around half.

Problem:

The second statistic does little additional explanatory work and creates another number that must be defined and verified.

Revision:

Use one carefully defined survival example unless another figure changes the reader's understanding.

The ten-year BLS figure is sufficient to establish that closure is common while preserving the key distinction that closure is not `giving up`.

Severity: **COMPRESSION**.

### 7. The `Learning to Quit?` paragraph needs a stronger post-treatment-selection warning

Problem:

The wording is technically qualified, but readers can still infer:

> more shutdown -> better surviving companies

as a causal chain.

The NBER abstract explicitly reports the stronger outcomes **conditional on survival** and says the findings are consistent with rational quitting. That is not the same as proving that quitting improves performance.

Revision:

Make the sequence unmistakable:

- treatment increased shutdown;
- separately, conditional on survival, treated ventures showed stronger observed outcomes;
- authors interpret the combination as consistent with earlier shutdown of weaker ventures;
- the study does not establish that shutdown itself caused the later performance difference.

Severity: **MATERIAL CLAIM-PRECISION ISSUE**.

### 8. The final section becomes too prescriptive

Examples:

- `continued effort should earn its place...`;
- `Persistence matters when...`;
- `It becomes harder to defend when...`.

Problem:

Those lines turn an Explained article into coaching. They also make a normative decision rule that the research does not validate as a universal guide.

Revision:

Convert the ending back into explanation:

- persistence is a decision under uncertainty, not a virtue by itself;
- prior investment can bias continuation decisions;
- new effort can sometimes produce information or improve economics, but the evidence does not supply a universal stopping rule;
- the practical difficulty is that productive persistence and costly continuation are not always easy to distinguish with incomplete information.

Stop there. Do not tell the reader what they `should` do.

Severity: **MATERIAL FORM VIOLATION**.

## AI-slop / human-language audit

### 1. Repeated connective formulas

Draft 1 repeatedly uses constructions such as:

- `That is why...`;
- `This does not mean...`;
- `The useful point is...`;
- `The relevant point is...`;
- `This is where...`.

None is individually wrong. The cluster makes the prose feel generated from an explanatory outline.

Revision:

Delete connectors that add no information and let evidence or subheads carry the transition.

### 2. Parallel example strings

The sequence beginning with product/customer/founder/sales-channel examples has useful content but reads as a polished four-beat construction.

Revision:

Keep the strongest two or three examples and develop them naturally rather than preserving symmetry.

### 3. Repeated caveat cadence

The draft uses several `This does not...` / `That does not...` paragraphs after research blocks.

Revision:

Keep material limitations, but integrate them into the factual sentence where possible rather than repeatedly performing a disclaimer after each source.

### 4. Final subhead risk

`What the grind actually contains` is understandable, but it pulls the article back toward the cultural `grind` frame after the body has successfully moved beyond it.

Revision option:

Reassess the final subhead in Draft 2. A literal heading about persistence, judgment, or what the evidence changes may fit the final block better.

Do not replace it merely for novelty.

### 5. Opening line

`Starting a business is hard. That sentence is true enough to be useless.`

Assessment:

**KEEP FOR NOW / REASSESS AFTER DRAFT 2.**

It is direct and performs the approved lead job. It is also rhetorically polished enough that it should survive only if the rest of the opening remains concrete and unforced.

No evidence problem exists because it does not make a quantified claim.
