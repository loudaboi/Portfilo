# Starting a Business Sources

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

## S001 - Paul Graham, Do Things That Don't Scale

URL: https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html

Type / tier: Primary practitioner source. Tier A for what Graham argues and the YC operating examples he reports; not independent evidence for universal business claims.

Date: July 2013

Why it matters:

Graham argues that startups rarely take off automatically and that founders often recruit early users manually and perform labor-intensive work before a scalable system exists.

Known limitations:

- Technology-startup / YC context.
- Practitioner synthesis rather than population-level research.
- Does not establish that all small businesses require the same path.

## S002 - Paul Graham, Before the Startup

URL: https://www.paulgraham.com/before.html

Type / tier: Primary practitioner source.

Date: October 2014

Why it matters:

Frames startup formation as counterintuitive and warns against assuming that familiar school or employment instincts map cleanly to starting a company.

Known limitations:

- Lecture-derived advice aimed partly at potential startup founders.
- Useful for practitioner perspective, not empirical prevalence claims.

## S003 - Sam Altman, Startup Playbook

URL: https://playbook.samaltman.com/

Type / tier: Primary practitioner source.

Why it matters:

Altman describes early startup work as covering idea, team, product, execution, growth, hiring, making money, fundraising, focus, and endurance. He also states that startups are harder and take longer than many founders expect.

Known limitations:

- YC / high-growth startup frame.
- Statements about difficulty are practitioner claims, not population statistics.

## S004 - Sam Altman, Startup Advice

URL: https://blog.samaltman.com/startup-advice

Type / tier: Primary practitioner source.

Why it matters:

Condenses early-stage work into making something people want, talking to users, writing/building, closing sales, hiring, and maintaining unusually high commitment over a long horizon.

Known limitations:

- Venture-startup context.
- Normative advice rather than causal evidence.

## S005 - Steve Blank, Customer Development writing

URLs:

- https://steveblank.com/2012/05/
- https://steveblank.com/2009/08/31/the-customer-development-manifesto-reasons-for-the-revolution-part-1/

Type / tier: Primary practitioner / methodology source.

Why it matters:

Blank argues that a startup begins with hypotheses rather than established facts and that founders must test customer, pricing, channel, and business-model assumptions outside the office.

Known limitations:

- Startup methodology, not a universal law of all firms.
- Some claims are deliberately polemical practitioner guidance.

## S006 - Ben Horowitz, The Struggle

URL: https://a16z.com/the-struggle/

Type / tier: Primary practitioner source.

Date: 2012

Why it matters:

Provides a first-person operating account of the psychological and managerial pressure created by losing customers, employees, competitive battles, cash runway, and confidence.

Known limitations:

- Highly dramatic founder narrative.
- Venture-backed technology-company context.
- Must not be used to imply that all entrepreneurs experience the same intensity.

## S007 - U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Business Employment Dynamics

URLs:

- https://www.bls.gov/spotlight/2024/business-employment-dynamics-twentieth-anniversary/
- https://www.bls.gov/bdm/bdmage.htm
- https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2024/34-7-percent-of-business-establishments-born-in-2013-were-still-operating-in-2023.htm

Type / tier: Tier A primary government data.

Dates: 2024 spotlight; survival tables updated through 2025.

Why it matters:

BLS longitudinal establishment data show that business closure is common. Five-year survival rates for selected U.S. startup-establishment cohorts are roughly around half, while 34.7% of private-sector establishments born in March 2013 were still operating ten years later.

Known limitations:

- U.S. private-sector establishments, not all entrepreneurs or all countries.
- Establishment closure does not identify whether a founder `gave up`, sold, pivoted, retired, or continued elsewhere.
- Establishment survival is not identical to firm survival.

## S008 - Global Entrepreneurship Monitor 2025/2026 Global Report

URL: https://www2.gemconsortium.org/report/gem-20252026-global-report-from-uncertainty-to-opportunity-3

Type / tier: Tier A/B international entrepreneurship research consortium.

Date: February 2026

Why it matters:

GEM reports strong early entrepreneurial activity alongside a `Survival Gap`, with too few early-stage ventures transitioning into established businesses across many economies.

Known limitations:

- Cross-country survey framework and definitions differ from BLS establishment survival.
- Does not imply every exit is negative or irrational.

## S009 - NBER, Learning to Quit? A Multi-Year, Multi-Site Field Experiment with Innovation-Driven Entrepreneurs

URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w34755

Type / tier: Tier A research source; randomized field experiment / NBER working paper.

Date: January 2026

Why it matters:

Randomized study of 553 science- and technology-based startups in 12 U.S. co-working spaces. Entrepreneurship training increased the probability and speed of shutdown, while surviving treated ventures raised funding faster, raised more funding, and had higher employment and revenue. The authors interpret the pattern as consistent with entrepreneurs learning to rationally quit less viable ventures.

Known limitations:

- Innovation-driven U.S. startups, not all businesses.
- Working paper rather than final universal consensus.
- Shutdown is an outcome, not proof that quitting caused later success.

## S010 - NBER, Financing Entrepreneurial Experimentation

URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w21278

Type / tier: Tier A research source; NBER working paper / later published chapter.

Date: 2015 / published 2016

Why it matters:

Treats entrepreneurship under uncertainty as experimentation in which the potential of a venture cannot be known in advance and must be learned through sequential investment and feedback.

Known limitations:

- Strong focus on innovative ventures and financing.
- Conceptual/economic framework rather than a direct measure of ordinary small-business hardship.

## S011 - Journal of Business Venturing, Entrepreneurs' stressors and well-being

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883902618308358

Type / tier: Tier A peer-reviewed research.

Date: 2021

Why it matters:

Diary study of 55 entrepreneurs found daily work stressors reduced psychological detachment from work and were associated with lower well-being the following morning, while some challenge stressors also had positive associations across time.

Known limitations:

- Small sample and short diary window.
- Stress is not uniformly negative and should not be converted into a simple `entrepreneurship makes people miserable` claim.

## S012 - Journal of Business Venturing, Does self-employment increase stress?

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883902625000849

Type / tier: Tier A peer-reviewed research; co-twin control analysis.

Date: January 2026

Why it matters:

Across Finnish and U.S. monozygotic-twin studies, self-employment was positively associated with stress, with long working hours partially explaining the relationship.

Known limitations:

- Self-employment is broader than founding a growth startup.
- Stress does not establish poor overall life satisfaction or venture failure.

## S013 - NBER, Self-Employment: More May Not Be Better

URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w10286

Type / tier: Tier A/B economic research.

Date: 2004

Why it matters:

Reports a tension relevant to the article: self-employed people reported substantial pressure, strain, worry, and exhaustion but also high perceived control and life satisfaction.

Known limitations:

- Older data.
- Self-employment is heterogeneous.
- Useful mainly as counterevidence against equating hardship with simple unhappiness.

## S014 - Philippine Statistics Authority, 2022 ASPBI

URL: https://psa.gov.ph/content/2022-annual-survey-philippine-business-and-industry-aspbi-economywide-final-results

Type / tier: Tier A Philippine government data.

Release date: July 8, 2026

Why it matters:

Provides formal-sector context and business composition. Separate PSA establishment-list and labor-survey materials show that more than one million establishments were in operation in the country in the 2023 establishment frame, while formal business activity spans a wide range of service, retail, manufacturing, and other industries.

Known limitations:

- Not a business-survival dataset.
- Formal establishments only in ASPBI coverage.
- Does not directly establish why businesses close or what founders experience.

## S015 - Asian Development Bank, The Philippines' Ecosystem for Technology Startups

URL: https://seads.adb.org/publication/philippines-ecosystem-technology-startups

Type / tier: Tier A/B institutional research.

Date: 2023

Why it matters:

Identifies constraints reported in the Philippine technology-startup ecosystem, including funding, talent and mentorship, digital infrastructure, and digital literacy.

Known limitations:

- Technology-startup ecosystem only.
- Not current enough to be treated as a 2026 count without checking updates.

## S016 - World Bank, Philippines Growth and Jobs Report 2025

URL: https://www.worldbank.org/en/country/philippines/publication/philippines-growth-and-jobs-report-2025

Type / tier: Tier A/B institutional economic research.

Date: 2025

Why it matters:

Describes constraints on Philippine firm dynamism and scaling, including regulatory complexity, competition, financing, skills scarcity, technology adoption, and recurring climate shocks affecting smaller firms.

Known limitations:

- Economy-level analysis rather than a founder-experience study.
- Should support local structural context, not personal hardship claims.

## S017 - World Bank Enterprise Surveys, Philippines 2023 Country Profile

URL: https://www.enterprisesurveys.org/content/dam/enterprisesurveys/documents/country/Philippines-2023.pdf

Type / tier: Tier A/B institutional firm survey.

Survey period: 2023

Why it matters:

The weighted country profile identifies electricity, practices of the informal sector, access to finance, business licenses and permits, tax rates, transport, corruption, crime, workforce constraints, and political instability among the business-environment obstacles selected by Philippine firms. The profile also shows substantial reliance on internal financing and meaningful differences in reported obstacles by firm size.

Known limitations:

- Covers formal private firms with at least five employees; firms with fewer than five employees are ineligible.
- Firm perceptions of obstacles do not establish causality or individual founder failure.
- Use the weighted country profile for population-facing percentages, not raw case counts from the microdata catalog.

## S018 - NBER, Entrepreneurship as Experimentation

URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w20358

Type / tier: Tier A research source; NBER working paper.

Date: July 2014

Why it matters:

Argues that entrepreneurship has an experimental character because venture outcomes are often highly uncertain and cannot be known fully before investment and market exposure. It provides a broader economic frame for why early entrepreneurial work involves learning rather than only executing a predetermined plan.

Known limitations:

- Conceptual framework with emphasis on innovation and financing environments.
- Does not imply every business decision can or should be treated as a clean experiment.

## S019 - NBER, Hoping to Win, Expected to Lose: Theory and Lessons on Micro Enterprise Development

URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w18325

Type / tier: Tier A research source; economic model plus field evidence / meta-analysis.

Date: August 2012

Why it matters:

Extends experimentation and learning logic beyond technology startups. The paper examines how financial and managerial-capital constraints can limit experimentation among microenterprises and how owners may learn about the profitability of different enterprise choices through investment and experience.

Known limitations:

- Evidence includes Ghanaian microenterprises and a small meta-analysis, not the Philippines.
- Older study and development-economics context.
- Should support the general mechanism, not a claim about Philippine microbusiness outcomes.

## S020 - NBER, Self-Employment Dynamics and the Returns to Entrepreneurship

URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w23168

Type / tier: Tier A research source; dynamic economic model with empirical evidence.

Date: February 2017

Why it matters:

Documents that transitions between paid work and self-employment are common and models self-employment as a setting in which people learn over time about uncertain entrepreneurial earnings. This broadens the learning-under-uncertainty evidence beyond venture-backed startups.

Known limitations:

- U.S. self-employment context.
- Economic-model interpretation rather than direct observation of every founder's reasoning.

## S021 - Journal of Business Venturing, Persist or let it go: Do rational entrepreneurs make decisions rationally?

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883902622000222

Type / tier: Tier A peer-reviewed entrepreneurship research.

Date: 2022

Why it matters:

Three discrete-choice experiments with entrepreneur samples in Australia and the United Kingdom examine persistence decisions for underperforming ventures. The findings show that backward-looking factors such as prior underperformance and personal investment can influence persistence decisions and that self-described rational decision makers do not always behave in a purely prospective way.

Known limitations:

- Choice experiments rather than observed shutdown behavior.
- Australia/UK samples.
- Does not show that persistence is generally irrational.

## S022 - Journal of Business Venturing Insights, The unrelenting entrepreneur

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352673423000495

Type / tier: Tier A peer-reviewed review article.

Date: 2023

Why it matters:

Reviews the fragmented entrepreneurial-persistence literature and cautions against treating persistence as a single simple construct with uniformly positive consequences.

Known limitations:

- Literature review rather than new causal evidence.
- The field itself contains definitional and theoretical fragmentation.

## S023 - Journal of Business Venturing Insights, Pivot, persist or perish?

URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2352673424000118

Type / tier: Tier A peer-reviewed conceptual research.

Date: 2024

Why it matters:

Challenges an overly clean `entrepreneur as scientist` metaphor and argues that experimentation is useful under some kinds of uncertainty but has important boundary conditions. This is valuable counterevidence against turning the article into a simplistic test-and-learn formula.

Known limitations:

- Conceptual analysis rather than a population estimate.

## S024 - World Bank, Philippines COMPETE for SMEs

URL: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099012126091536048/pdf/P507827-6119c7d0-f4ac-40e0-9152-251cf951050f.pdf

Type / tier: Tier A/B institutional project and sector analysis.

Date: 2026

Why it matters:

The project document cites firm analysis and management interviews identifying lack of business, managerial and technical skills, quality certifications, market intelligence and business connections, and long-term finance as constraints to Philippine SME investment and competitiveness.

Known limitations:

- Development-project document, not an entrepreneurship-survival study.
- Addresses SME upgrading and competitiveness more than formation-stage psychology.

## S025 - Asian Development Bank, Measuring Progress on Women's Financial Inclusion and Entrepreneurship in the Philippines

URL: https://www.adb.org/publications/women-financial-inclusion-entrepreneurship-philippines

Type / tier: Tier A/B institutional MSME survey research.

Date: October 2023

Why it matters:

Provides Philippine MSME survey evidence on access to financial services and documents additional barriers faced by women-owned MSMEs. Useful for showing that financing constraints differ across founder groups rather than affecting all entrepreneurs identically.

Known limitations:

- Gender and finance focus.
- Does not measure the entire experience of starting a business.

## Source-use rule

Famous founder and investor writing is useful because it records practitioner observations and operating frameworks. It must not be promoted into universal fact merely because the writers are successful or well known.

The eventual public story should distinguish:

- practitioner observation;
- empirical evidence;
- Philippine structural context;
- Portfilo inference.

The second research pass deliberately broadens the evidence beyond venture-backed technology startups. It still does not justify presenting all businesses as equivalent.
