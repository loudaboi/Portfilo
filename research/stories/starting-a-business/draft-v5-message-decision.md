## Decision

Draft 5 improved the article but remained fragmented because the persistence-and-exit material became a second information problem.

The founder/editor approved a tighter single-message direction for the next draft.

## Reader payoff

The story should help a small-business owner, founder, or person learning about business understand this distinction:

> A business result tells you what happened. It does not automatically tell you what caused it.

A new business is difficult partly because the owner has to keep operating while diagnosing why results are good or bad, and wrong diagnoses can consume real time and money.

## Working message

The article is now about **operating and diagnosing a new business at the same time**.

The core explanation is:

- weak sales are a result, not a diagnosis;
- different possible causes require different responses;
- the owner has to gather better information while the business is already spending resources;
- effort alone does not solve the problem if it is applied to the wrong cause.

## Scope correction

Persistence and exit remain relevant background research because they prevent unsupported `never quit` claims, but they are no longer a major body topic for this article.

The `Learning to Quit?` study should stay backstage unless a later editorial need makes it essential.

The World Bank Philippines Enterprise Survey remains valid research context but is not part of this article unless a later draft develops a specific reader need that requires it.

## Structural constraint

Keep the already approved professional explainer form:

`question -> succinct answer -> explanation`

No predetermined section count.

No secondary quitting article inside the main article.

No visible source merely to demonstrate research depth.
