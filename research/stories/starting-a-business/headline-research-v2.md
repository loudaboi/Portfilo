# Starting a Business Headline Research v2

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

_Status: FRESH EVALUATION AGAINST APPROVED DRAFT 8 / AWAITING FOUNDER-EDITOR HEADLINE DECISION_

## Why this pass exists

The previous headline research was built around an older, broader version of the story that included persistence, quitting, operating constraints, and a wider explanation of why starting a business is hard.

Draft 8 is narrower. The approved body now explains a specific reader problem:

> A bad business result tells you what happened. It does not automatically tell you what caused it.

The headline therefore has to promise that actual article, not the earlier one.

## Professional headline guidance

Poynter headline guidance used for this pass emphasizes:

- accuracy word by word;
- making the story's real point rather than a side issue or mere entry point;
- avoiding over- or underselling;
- specificity over vagueness;
- giving readers a clear sense of what the story will do for them;
- using plain, efficient language rather than cleverness for its own sake.

## Headline job for Draft 8

The headline should make a clear promise around one of these closely related ideas:

1. weak sales do not identify their own cause;
2. a business result is not automatically a diagnosis;
3. starting a business involves learning what its results actually mean before committing more resources.

The headline should not imply:

- a complete business-diagnosis system;
- a guaranteed method for fixing weak sales;
- that weak sales are the only problem new businesses face;
- that the article is about quitting;
- that the article is primarily motivational;
- that the story provides a universal formula for success.

## Candidate evaluation

### Candidate A — `Why weak sales don't tell you what to fix`

Strengths:

- specific and easy to understand;
- names the article's concrete explanatory case;
- promises the exact distinction the body explains;
- useful to a business owner immediately;
- avoids vague `business is hard` framing;
- does not promise a full diagnostic framework.

Weaknesses:

- narrows the article heavily around weak sales;
- does not explicitly say `starting a business`;
- uses second person, which is acceptable but more direct than the body overall.

Editorial assessment:

**RECOMMENDED PRIMARY.**

Reason:

Draft 8 now spends most of its explanatory work on weak sales and what they fail to reveal. This headline makes the strongest reader promise without overselling the answer.

### Candidate B — `What weak sales can and can't tell you about a new business`

Strengths:

- accurate;
- specific;
- clearly explanatory;
- names both the signal and its limitation;
- keeps `new business` visible.

Weaknesses:

- slightly long;
- `can and can't` has a familiar explainer-headline pattern;
- sounds more like an information article than a sharp editorial title.

Editorial assessment:

**STRONG ALTERNATIVE.**

### Candidate C — `A bad business result is not a diagnosis`

Strengths:

- captures the approved reader destination directly;
- concise;
- distinguishes result from cause cleanly.

Weaknesses:

- `diagnosis` is metaphorical and slightly clinical;
- `bad business result` is abstract;
- less immediately concrete than weak sales.

Editorial assessment:

**STRONG CONCEPT / NOT PRIMARY.**

### Candidate D — `Why starting a business means learning what your results actually mean`

Strengths:

- keeps the original broader subject;
- accurately connects starting a business to interpretation of results;
- matches the final body destination.

Weaknesses:

- long;
- `actually mean` can sound conversationally padded;
- less immediate than the weak-sales headline;
- risks sounding like a thesis sentence rather than a headline.

Editorial assessment:

**ACCURATE BUT TOO HEAVY.**

### Candidate E — `When sales are weak, the problem is knowing why`

Strengths:

- natural language;
- captures ambiguity of cause;
- directly relevant to small-business owners.

Weaknesses:

- `the problem` is too singular and absolute;
- can imply that knowing why is the only important problem;
- less precise about what the article explains after the cause remains unclear.

Editorial assessment:

**NOT SELECTED.**

### Candidate F — `Why working harder doesn't fix every early business problem`

Strengths:

- connects back to the original reader question;
- gives a clear practical implication.

Weaknesses:

- sounds closer to advice or motivational counter-programming;
- the body does not systematically compare hard work with other solutions;
- foregrounds the implication rather than the actual explanatory mechanism.

Editorial assessment:

**REJECT.**

## Recommendation

Primary recommendation:

> **Why weak sales don't tell you what to fix**

Why:

The approved article is no longer a broad essay about why entrepreneurship is difficult. It is a focused explainer about why observable business results can have several causes and why acting on the wrong explanation wastes resources.

Weak sales are not merely a lead example in Draft 8. They are the article's developed explanatory case, so using them in the headline is now editorially justified.
