# Starting a Business Style Brief

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

## Assignment

Story ID: `UNASSIGNED`

Working label: `starting-a-business`

Content type: `Explained`

Primary topic: `Building`

Primary reader need: `Understand`

## Writing job

Explain what makes starting a business difficult in practical terms, while showing why persistence can be necessary without treating persistence itself as proof that a business should continue.

## Audience decision

The intended reader can be assumed to understand ordinary business terms such as customers, revenue, pricing, and small business, but not entrepreneurship research or business-survival methodology.

Requires explanation:

- establishment survival versus firm/founder outcomes;
- why business closure is not the same thing as `giving up`;
- entrepreneurship as learning/experimentation under uncertainty;
- persistence versus excessive persistence;
- what Philippine firm constraints can and cannot establish.

Established audience basis:

Portfilo's approved audience includes readers trying to understand, work in, or build within the business and technology economy.

Editorial hypothesis:

Readers considering or building businesses will find a concrete explanation of the work and uncertainty more useful than generic hustle language. Do not present that assumption as measured audience fact.

## Evidence shape

The material contains:

- practitioner observations;
- business-survival data;
- research on entrepreneurship as experimentation and learning;
- evidence on rational shutdown and persistence decisions;
- research on entrepreneurial stress and recovery;
- Philippine institutional evidence on firm-level operating constraints;
- strong counterevidence against a `never quit` thesis.

It does not contain a reported scene or representative founder case that should organize the piece.

## Style-research findings used

Source: `style-research.md`

### Finding 1

Observed practice/research finding:

Professional explainer guidance and the Reuters explainer orient the reader quickly and begin answering before extended background.

Portfilo interpretation:

The article should not spend several paragraphs explaining entrepreneurship culture or famous founders before answering the question.

Resulting editorial decision:

State the central explanatory answer within the first several paragraphs.

### Finding 2

Observed practice/research finding:

Strong explainers break a complex subject into logical reader questions or mechanisms, while descriptive subheads can carry transitions without connective filler.

Portfilo interpretation:

The evidence forms a connected mechanism rather than a chronology or a listicle.

Resulting editorial decision:

Use a small number of descriptive blocks organized by explanatory job, not a rigid Q&A or numbered framework.

### Finding 3

Observed practice/research finding:

The StateImpact example corrects a familiar entrepreneurship assumption early with research, while avoiding a purely academic presentation.

Portfilo interpretation:

The story can acknowledge the cultural language of `grind` and `never quit`, but only as a problem to clarify, not as a straw man or rhetorical hook repeated throughout the piece.

Resulting editorial decision:

Use the `grind` misconception sparingly in the opening, then move to mechanisms and evidence.

### Finding 4

Observed practice/research finding:

The Reuters founder feature explicitly distinguishes one founder's case from wider evidence before generalizing.

Portfilo interpretation:

Individual founder accounts are illustrations, not prevalence evidence.

Resulting editorial decision:

Do not use a founder anecdote as the lead in the current version. If interviews are later added, keep them subordinate to the explainer's evidence structure and state the wider context quickly.

### Finding 5

Observed practice/research finding:

Across the exemplars and professional guidance, counterevidence and limitations work best when they alter the explanation where the relevant claim appears.

Portfilo interpretation:

The closure/persistence contradiction is central to the article, not a caveat to append at the end.

Resulting editorial decision:

Place `closure is not giving up` and rational-shutdown evidence in the core middle logic.

## Selected Portfilo recipe

Selected recipe: `Explained`

Why it fits:

The reader's primary need is to understand a consequential question. The evidence is mechanism-oriented and contains definitional distinctions, empirical findings, practitioner observations, and counterevidence. The Portfilo Explained recipe calls for naming the question quickly, giving the clearest defensible answer early, establishing scope and limitation, dividing the explanation into logical blocks, and stopping when the question is answered.

## Deviations

The default Explained recipe allows question-led sections. This story should **not** use a full Q&A structure because the evidence forms one connected argument about uncertainty, work, constraints, and continuation decisions.

The story should also avoid a case-led opening because the packet currently lacks original reporting strong enough to justify one.

## Human-edited exemplar lessons

Selected transferable decisions:

- Reuters explainer: orient quickly; let descriptive sections carry structure; define important terms and categories; stop without a manufactured moral.
- StateImpact/NPR: a familiar business assumption can be corrected early with evidence; local context should change interpretation rather than decorate it.
- Reuters founder feature: when a personal case appears, distinguish the case from wider evidence before generalizing.

No wording, cadence, metaphors, jokes, branded terms, or institutional voice from these examples may be copied.

## Lead job

The opening should establish that `starting a business is hard` is true but too vague to explain anything useful. It should then name the actual problem: founders have to act before they know which actions will work, while learning from customers, money, operations, and setbacks.

Why this opening fits:

It directly converts the reader's likely familiar framing into the research question without manufacturing a scene or relying on a failure statistic for drama.

Explicitly rejected openings:

- fictional or composite founder scene;
- famous-founder anecdote;
- `X% of businesses fail` shock statistic;
- motivational quote;
- rhetorical sequence such as `Do you have what it takes?`;
- autobiography;
- `Everyone tells you...` generalization without evidence.

## Nut / answer / thesis position

The central answer should appear within the first several paragraphs, after the question is framed but before source-by-source evidence begins.

Proposed bounded answer:

Starting a business is difficult because it requires acting and making resource decisions under uncertainty while learning whether customers want the offer, whether the economics work, and whether continued effort is justified. The difficulty can include manual selling, wrong assumptions, scarce resources, operational friction, stress, and the need to distinguish persistence from stubbornness.

This wording is a structural content requirement, not approved final prose.

## Paragraph development

A normal paragraph should do one of these jobs:

- explain one mechanism;
- state and qualify one consequential finding;
- translate one research concept;
- connect a practitioner observation to independent evidence;
- establish a Philippine constraint and its limitation;
- test the current explanation against counterevidence.

Do not stack unrelated statistics or multiple famous-founder summaries into one paragraph.

## Middle logic

### 1. The work is broader than building the product

Job:

Explain manual customer acquisition, selling, testing pricing/distribution assumptions, and other early work that does not look like the polished image of `building a company`.

Evidence/question carried:

Practitioner sources such as Graham, Blank, and Altman, used as operating observations rather than universal authority.

### 2. The founder is acting before the answer is known

Job:

Explain entrepreneurship as learning and experimentation under uncertainty.

Evidence/question carried:

NBER experimentation/self-employment research plus the distinction between executing a known plan and learning whether the plan is viable.

### 3. Constraints turn uncertainty into pressure

Job:

Explain why money, time, capability, stress, and operating friction make learning costly rather than abstract.

Evidence/question carried:

Stress/recovery research and Philippine evidence on finance, electricity, permits, competition, skills, technology adoption, market access, and related constraints.

The Philippine material should be integrated as concrete context, not isolated in a `Philippines` section solely for branding.

### 4. Persistence is necessary, but it is not automatically progress

Job:

Introduce the article's strongest contradiction and explain why closure cannot be equated with weakness.

Evidence/question carried:

Business-survival definitions, rational-shutdown research, persistence/excessive-persistence research, and limitations of survival as a success measure.

### 5. What the evidence changes about the idea of `grind`

Job:

Synthesize the previous mechanisms into a bounded implication without converting the article into advice or inspiration.

Evidence/question carried:

Portfilo inference from the verified packet: the hard part is partly continuing to act and learn while remaining willing to change or stop when the evidence changes.

These are block jobs, not approved section headings.

## Evidence and example handling

- Put evidence near the claim it supports.
- Preserve source category: practitioner observation, empirical research, government/institutional data, or Portfilo inference.
- Do not use a famous founder's statement to establish prevalence.
- Name populations when using survival or firm data.
- Explain limitations at the point where they alter interpretation.
- Use Philippine constraints only for claims the underlying data can support.
- If interviews are later added, use them as concrete examples and original reporting, not as proof that founders generally behave the same way.
- Avoid quote accumulation. Paraphrase findings where accurate and use direct quotation only when exact language itself is editorially valuable and verified.

## Transition behavior

Transitions should arise from causal or logical progression:

work -> uncertainty -> constraints -> persistence decision -> bounded implication.

Descriptive subheads may perform most large transitions.

Avoid formulas such as:

- `But here's the thing`;
- `And that's where...`;
- `This is where things get interesting`;
- `In other words` repeated mechanically;
- `The lesson is clear`;
- paragraph endings that merely preview the next heading;
- mini-summaries after every section.

## Person and rhetorical devices

First-person use:

None by default. Do not use `we believe`, `we think`, or newsroom-centered phrasing to state Portfilo inference.

Second-person use:

Minimal. It may be used sparingly to make a decision distinction legible, but the piece should not become coaching.

Rhetorical-question use:

Maximum role is the genuine central question or an occasional functional sub-question. Do not use strings of questions to create energy.

Other devices/limits:

- no motivational slogans;
- no dramatic one-line paragraph sequence;
- no invented founder archetype;
- no metaphor required;
- avoid binary `winners vs losers` framing;
- avoid turning `grind` into a recurring branded motif.

## Counterevidence / limitation

Strongest counterevidence:

Persistence is not always the correct response. Business closure does not establish founder weakness, survival does not equal success, and learning can rationally produce earlier shutdown of weaker ventures.

Placement:

Core middle section, after explaining uncertainty and constraints. It must reshape the article's answer rather than appear as a final disclaimer.

Other material limitations:

- startup advice is not all-business evidence;
- U.S. establishment survival data cannot be generalized to Filipino founders;
- Philippine Enterprise Survey results cover formal firms with at least five employees and not all microbusinesses;
- stress evidence does not prove entrepreneurship is uniformly miserable.

## Ending job

End with the narrow implication produced by the evidence: starting a business is not only a test of endurance. It is a process of doing difficult work while learning which effort is producing information, customers, or viable economics and which effort no longer deserves continuation.

The ending should resolve the explanatory tension and stop.

Prohibited ending forms:

- `So keep going`;
- `Never give up on your dreams`;
- `The choice is yours`;
- inspirational founder quote;
- Portfilo mission statement;
- recap list of every section;
- elevated slogan that restates the thesis.

## Voice and vocabulary constraints

Use:

- concrete business nouns: customers, price, cash, permits, sales, time, employees, electricity, financing;
- plain verbs: sell, test, pay, hire, close, change, learn;
- precise evidence labels where needed: `study`, `survey`, `establishments`, `formal firms`;
- `founder` only when the source/population is actually founders;
- `business owner` or `firm` when more accurate.

Avoid or tightly control:

- `hustle`;
- `grindset`;
- `resilience` as an unexplained virtue;
- `mindset`;
- `journey`;
- `entrepreneurial spirit`;
- `visionary`;
- `game-changing`;
- `secret sauce`;
- `fail fast` unless attributed/explained;
- startup jargon not necessary to the evidence;
- language that romanticizes financial insecurity or overwork.

## Institutional-copy provenance

The story should not need to make claims about Portfilo itself.

If a methodological note is required, its authority comes from the story research packet and canonical research/publishing standards, not from newly invented brand language.

## Headline job

The headline must identify the explanatory question or the supported tension around what makes starting a business hard.

It must not imply:

- that most founders give up;
- a universal failure rate;
- that persistence causes success;
- that quitting causes success;
- that the article contains a guaranteed formula for entrepreneurship;
- that Philippine founders are uniquely disadvantaged unless evidence directly supports that claim.

Headline wording remains a later research/editing task after Draft 1 evidence use stabilizes.

## Dek job

The dek should add the scope of the explanation: early work, uncertainty, constraints, and the persistence-versus-exit tension.

It should not repeat the headline in softer language or promise a motivational lesson.

## Description / summary job

Provide a short literal summary suitable for cards, indexes, SEO, and social metadata. It should say that the story explains the work and uncertainty involved in starting a business and why persistence is not the same thing as progress.

Do not duplicate the final dek automatically.

## Assignment-specific AI-slop risks

Risks:

- turning the story into `hard truths` motivational content;
- `not X, but Y` repeated across sections;
- dramatic one-line paragraphs about sacrifice;
- invented claims about what `most founders` feel or do;
- synthetic symmetry such as exactly three challenges and three lessons;
- overusing `in practice`, `ultimately`, `at the end of the day`, `what matters`, or `the reality is`;
- using famous-founder quotes as authority instead of evidence leads;
- converting every research finding into a tidy lesson;
- forcing a Philippine paragraph after every global point;
- using survival statistics as failure statistics;
- writing a heroic ending about perseverance;
- copying Reuters' modular cadence, StateImpact's playfulness, or any practitioner's recognizable prose;
- generating smooth transition sentences that add no information;
- turning caveats into repetitive performative disclaimers.
