# Starting a Business Reader Destination Decision

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

_Status: FOUNDER-EDITOR APPROVED_

## Why this decision exists

Draft 6 improved the story's message but exposed a separate problem: a coherent message is not enough if the article does not deliver a useful change in the reader's understanding.

For Portfilo Explained work, the relevant question is not only `What is the thesis?` but:

> **If the reader gives Portfilo several minutes, what useful understanding do they get in return?**

## Approved reader destination for this story

After reading, a founder, small-business owner, or reader learning about business should understand:

> **A bad business result tells you what happened. It does not automatically tell you what caused it.**

For example, weak sales can result from price, awareness, distribution, demand, the offer, or another cause. Those possible causes can require very different responses.

The reader should therefore understand why starting a business can be difficult even when the owner is willing to work hard: the owner has to operate the business while still learning how to diagnose its results, and a wrong diagnosis can waste limited time and money.

## Useful consequence

The story may point the reader toward this implication without turning into a Guide:

> **More effort is useful only when it is directed at a problem the owner understands well enough to act on, or when the effort itself helps the owner learn what the problem is.**

This is an explanatory destination, not a step-by-step instruction set.

## Boundaries

This story is not currently trying to answer:

- exactly when a founder should quit;
- how to run customer interviews;
- how to diagnose every business problem;
- a complete business-testing framework;
- a universal sequence for starting a business.

Those may be separate Explained or Guide assignments if later justified.

## Draft implication

Every paragraph in the next draft must help move the reader toward this destination.

Research, statistics, examples, transitions, or caveats that do not materially improve this understanding stay backstage.
