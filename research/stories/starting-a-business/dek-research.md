# Starting a Business Dek Research

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

_Status: DEK EVALUATION COMPLETE / AWAITING FOUNDER-EDITOR DECISION_

## Approved headline

> **What actually makes starting a business hard?**

## Approved body direction

Draft 8 is approved.

The article explains that a new business has to make consequential decisions while still learning what its observable results actually mean. Weak sales are the main explanatory case. The reader should understand that a result does not automatically identify its cause, that different causes require different responses, and that the next action can be useful partly for what it helps the business learn before more resources are committed.

## Dek job

Per `docs/COPY_STYLE.md`, the dek must add information rather than translate the headline into another sentence.

For this story, the dek should therefore:

- keep the headline's broad `starting a business` framing;
- introduce the result-versus-cause problem or its resource consequence;
- not turn the story into a weak-sales article;
- not promise a complete diagnostic framework;
- not sound motivational;
- avoid repeating `hard` or merely saying that business takes more than work.

One sentence is preferred if it can do the job cleanly.

## Candidate A

> **New owners have to decide where to spend time and money while still learning what their results actually mean.**

Strengths:

- adds the core mechanism without repeating the headline;
- keeps the subject broader than weak sales;
- names the real stakes: time and money;
- accurately matches Draft 8;
- plain enough to work under a broad question headline.

Weaknesses:

- `results actually mean` repeats wording used inside the article;
- intentionally restrained rather than highly distinctive.

Editorial assessment:

**RECOMMENDED PRIMARY.**

## Candidate B

> **A new business has to keep operating while the owner is still figuring out what caused the results they can see.**

Strengths:

- captures operating and diagnosing at the same time;
- directly expresses the article's central difficulty;
- does not overpromise a solution.

Weaknesses:

- slightly more abstract;
- `results they can see` is less natural than Candidate A;
- does not expose the resource cost as clearly.

Editorial assessment:

**STRONG ALTERNATIVE.**

## Candidate C

> **A bad result can have several causes, and acting on the wrong one can waste limited time and money.**

Strengths:

- very concrete;
- gives the reader the problem and consequence immediately;
- accurate to the body.

Weaknesses:

- begins from `a bad result` instead of the broader experience of starting a business;
- slightly more cautionary in tone;
- gives away less of the article's learning mechanism.

Editorial assessment:

**STRONG ALTERNATIVE / MORE CONSEQUENCE-LED.**

## Candidate D

> **Weak sales can point to price, demand, awareness, distribution or the offer itself. The hard part is deciding what to change before the cause is clear.**

Strengths:

- concrete;
- makes the article easy to understand from the headline/dek pair;
- uses the article's developed example.

Weaknesses:

- over-centers weak sales after the founder/editor explicitly chose a broader headline;
- repeats `hard` from the headline;
- starts to sound like the article is primarily a sales explainer.

Editorial assessment:

**NOT RECOMMENDED.**

## Recommendation

Use Candidate A:

> **New owners have to decide where to spend time and money while still learning what their results actually mean.**

It gives the broad headline a specific answer without collapsing the article into the weak-sales example or promising a guide.
