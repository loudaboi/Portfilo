# Draft 4 Reader-Help Audit

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

_Status: FOUNDER-EDITOR DIRECTION APPROVED / REVISION INPUT_

## Audit question

Would a founder, business owner, or reader learning about business finish Draft 4 understanding something useful that changes how they think about starting a business?

## Verdict

**PARTIAL PASS / REWRITE REQUIRED**

Draft 4 finally uses the approved `question -> succinct answer -> explanation` form, but the editorial selection is still too research-driven.

The article's useful message is simpler than the draft makes it:

> A new business has to make costly decisions before the owner has enough evidence to know which decisions are right. More effort can produce useful information, but more effort is not automatically progress.

Everything in the next draft must help the reader understand that message.

## What survives

- the uncertainty problem;
- weak sales as a concrete example of ambiguous information;
- the distinction between useful work and staying busy;
- the fact that wrong assumptions cost real time or money;
- the persistence-versus-progress contradiction;
- the absence of a universal timetable for when to continue or stop.

## What is removed

### World Bank Philippines Enterprise Survey paragraph

Decision: **REMOVE FROM THIS STORY.**

Reason:

The evidence is accurate but does not answer the central reader question closely enough. It changes the subject from how a new owner learns what is wrong with the business to a broader discussion of Philippine operating constraints.

The survey's sample caveat then forces more methodological explanation, increasing reader effort without enough explanatory payoff.

The source remains useful in the research packet and may belong in a different story about Philippine business conditions.

### Excess methodological detail around `Learning to Quit?`

Decision: **COMPRESS.**

Reason:

The study is useful only because it changes how the reader should understand persistence. The public article does not need a mini literature review.

Keep only enough detail to establish that a randomized study found entrepreneurship training increased earlier shutdown among a defined startup sample, and that the authors interpreted the pattern as consistent with learning to abandon weaker ventures sooner.

Preserve the limitation that the study does not show quitting causes success and does not represent all businesses.

## Reader-help standard for Draft 5

Each paragraph must do at least one of these jobs:

- clarify the central answer;
- make the uncertainty problem concrete;
- explain why a wrong decision has a cost;
- show why more effort can help or fail to help;
- supply evidence that materially changes the reader's understanding;
- close a misleading inference.

If a paragraph mainly proves that Portfilo researched the subject, delete it.

## Message to preserve

The reader should leave understanding:

1. Early business decisions are made with incomplete information.
2. Weak results are signals, not automatic diagnoses.
3. The owner has to keep choosing what to change while money and time are being spent.
4. Persistence can be necessary, but continuing is not the same thing as improving.
5. There is no universal rule that resolves that uncertainty for every business.

These are reader outcomes, not required headings or a numbered public framework.

## Boundary

Do not convert Draft 5 into a guide, checklist, or motivational essay.

Do not add Philippine evidence merely because Portfilo is Philippine-first.

Do not add a source unless the source materially improves understanding or protects a consequential claim.
