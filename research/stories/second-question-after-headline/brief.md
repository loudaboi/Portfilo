Research question:

> **What questions do younger Filipino readers ask after first encountering a business or technology news story, and what do they do next to answer them?**

The question deliberately does not assume that AI is the main tool, that journalism caused the information gap, or that most readers pursue a second step.

## Reader payoff

Show, through observed behavior rather than brand positioning, what happens between first exposure to a business/technology update and a reader feeling that they understand it well enough to move on.

The useful story is not `young people want simpler news`.

It is potentially about:

- what the first unanswered question actually is;
- which questions recur across different kinds of stories;
- whether readers open the full article, search, compare outlets, check official sources, watch a creator, read comments, ask another person, use AI, or simply stop;
- what makes them decide they have enough information;
- where AI fits into that chain, if it appears naturally.

## Working hypothesis

First exposure often establishes that something happened without resolving every question a reader needs to make sense of it.

For some younger Filipino readers, the next step may involve an active sensemaking chain: asking what a term means, how a mechanism works, why a development matters, what it should be compared with, whether the source is credible, or what is still missing.

AI chatbots may be becoming one tool for that second pass, but they are expected to remain a minority behavior. Search, other outlets, creators, official sources, social discussion, and no follow-up at all may be equally or more important.

Possible analytical categories such as `definition`, `mechanism`, `consequence`, `comparison`, and `credibility` are provisional coding hypotheses, not conclusions.

## Disproof / reframe conditions

Reframe or kill the current thesis if:

- most participants do not form a meaningful follow-up question after real news encounters;
- participants commonly form questions but do not pursue answers, making the stronger story about the cost or friction of following up rather than `second-step sensemaking`;
- observed follow-up behavior is almost entirely opening the full article or using conventional search, with no materially new pattern;
- AI appears mainly after interviewer prompting rather than in natural behavior;
- participant behavior changes substantially under the controlled task and does not resemble natural diary behavior;
- the sample cannot be diversified beyond a narrow founder/friend/technology-news circle;
- the findings only reproduce the already-published 2024 Philippine conclusion that younger audiences prefer news that is easier to understand and more interactive;
- the strongest conclusion requires population-level prevalence claims that the qualitative method cannot support;
- the story can only work by blaming journalism, platforms, or readers without evidence establishing causality.

## Scope

### Population

Primary fieldwork population:

- Filipino adults aged `18-34`;
- deliberate mix of `18-24` and `25-34`;
- mix of students and working adults where practical;
- include participants inside and outside technology/business professions;
- include both AI-news users and non-users;
- include geographic variation beyond one immediate peer circle where practical.

The sample will be qualitative and purposive, not representative of all young Filipinos.

### Subject matter

Focus on naturally encountered or controlled-stimulus stories about:

- business;
- technology;
- startups;
- AI;
- companies;
- markets;
- regulation or infrastructure when clearly connected to those beats.

Political news is not the primary scope. It may appear incidentally in participants' natural news diets, but the controlled stimuli should match Portfilo's coverage area.

### What the story will not establish

The reporting will not claim:

- that most Filipino readers need a second explanation after every story;
- that Philippine journalism broadly lacks context;
- that young people are less intelligent, less literate, or less capable of understanding news;
- that AI chatbots are reliable news sources;
- that AI use proves journalism failed;
- that social-media discovery causes misunderstanding;
- that the qualitative sample estimates national prevalence;
- that every unanswered question should have been answered in the original news item.

## Existing coverage / originality boundary

The Reuters Institute's 2026 Digital News Report already establishes that AI chatbots are a minority but growing news interface globally and that follow-up questions are their most common news use across surveyed markets.

Philippine reporting by Yvonne T. Chua at VERA Files further reports that weekly chatbot use for news in the Philippines was 9% in 2026, including 11% among 18-24-year-olds, and that among weekly generative-AI news users, summarisation and follow-up questions were prominent uses.

VERA Files' Celine Isabelle Samson also reported in 2024 on focus groups and one-on-one interviews with 20 Filipinos aged 18-34. Those participants asked for news experiences that were easier to understand and more interactive.

Therefore Portfilo cannot claim originality by merely showing that:

- young people want clearer news;
- Filipinos use social media for news;
- some Filipinos use AI to summarise or ask follow-up questions.

The intended original contribution is narrower:

> **Observe the actual questions and information-seeking pathways that follow a business/technology news encounter, using both natural encounters and a real-time task, then describe those behaviors without converting the sample into a population estimate.**

## Evidence path

### Essential

1. Current Digital News Report / Philippine audience data to establish the larger environment.
2. A small original qualitative sample of younger Filipino readers.
3. Natural encounter records rather than interviews based only on memory.
4. Real-time walkthroughs that show what participants actually do when they want to understand more.
5. Both AI users and non-users so AI is not built into the sample result.
6. Contradiction analysis, including participants who do not follow up or who distrust/avoid AI.

### Optional but useful

- interview with Yvonne T. Chua about interpreting the Philippine DNR findings and their limits;
- interview with Celine Isabelle Samson about what her 2024 youth-news research did and did not examine;
- one Philippine media/news-literacy researcher to challenge causal interpretations;
- examples from publishers or creators only if they explain a behavior already observed in readers rather than replacing reader evidence.

## Reporting threshold

Target `12-15` completed participants.

Minimum threshold before a publish decision: `10` complete participant records with usable diary and interview/walkthrough evidence.

The sample should contain meaningful variation across:

- 18-24 and 25-34;
- frequent and occasional business/technology news users;
- people who use AI for information and people who do not;
- technology/business-adjacent and non-adjacent backgrounds.

A publishable result also needs:

- recurring follow-up-question patterns across multiple participants, not one memorable anecdote;
- documented examples of actual next-step behavior;
- clear negative cases where participants stop, accept the first exposure, or reject tools such as AI;
- quotations or anonymized observed examples collected with explicit consent;
- language that states the sample limits directly.

Do not publish percentages derived from a sample of this size as if they describe Filipino readers generally.
