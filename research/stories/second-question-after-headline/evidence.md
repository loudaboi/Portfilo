# C029 Evidence Ledger

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

This ledger contains documentary evidence available before Portfilo fieldwork. It is not sufficient for drafting the story.

## C01

Candidate claim:

Philippine news use is strongly platform-mediated rather than centered on publisher websites/apps.

Evidence:

Yvonne T. Chua's 2026 Philippine Digital News Report coverage says social media and video networks reached 70% weekly news use in the Philippines, while news websites/apps were at 42%. The Reuters Institute country page independently states that social media remain the dominant source of news and that fewer Filipinos access news directly through TV, radio, or websites.

Source:

- https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/philippines
- https://verafiles.org/articles/filipino-trust-in-news-posts-biggest-fall-digital-news-report-2026

Tier:

A / B

Confidence:

HIGH

Caveat:

Platform-mediated discovery does not itself imply that people misunderstand what they see or that publisher articles are inadequate.

Status:

VERIFIED / QUALIFY CAUSAL INTERPRETATION

## C02

Candidate claim:

AI chatbots are a minority news interface in the Philippines, with higher reported use among younger adults.

Evidence:

Chua reports 2026 weekly chatbot news use in the Philippines at 9%, including 11% among 18-24-year-olds and 3% among respondents 55 and older.

Source:

https://verafiles.org/articles/filipino-trust-in-news-posts-biggest-fall-digital-news-report-2026

Tier:

B, based on original DNR data

Confidence:

HIGH

Caveat:

The detailed country figures should be traced to the underlying DNR dataset/report material where practical before final publication. Nine percent is a minority; do not describe AI as a mainstream replacement for news brands.

Status:

QUALIFY / HIGH CONFIDENCE

## C03

Candidate claim:

Among Filipino weekly generative-AI news users, AI is used for processing and interrogating news, not only for retrieving headlines.

Evidence:

Chua reports that 52% of weekly generative-AI news users asked for summaries, 48% posed follow-up questions, 43% sought the latest news, 39% asked AI to find or assess a source, and 36% used it to simplify stories.

Source:

https://verafiles.org/articles/filipino-trust-in-news-posts-biggest-fall-digital-news-report-2026

Tier:

B, based on original DNR data

Confidence:

HIGH

Caveat:

The denominator is weekly generative-AI news users, not all Filipino adults and not all young Filipinos. Multiple responses may be allowed. Usage does not establish accuracy, trust, or why the original news item was insufficient.

Status:

QUALIFY / CENTRAL BACKGROUND EVIDENCE

## C04

Candidate claim:

Use of AI for news can coexist with low trust in AI answers.

Evidence:

The same 2026 Philippine DNR coverage reports trust in AI-chatbot answers at 15%, below trust in social-media news at 19% and below overall news trust at 28%.

Source:

https://verafiles.org/articles/filipino-trust-in-news-posts-biggest-fall-digital-news-report-2026

Tier:

B, based on original DNR data

Confidence:

HIGH

Caveat:

Trust measures are subjective survey judgments. They do not measure factual accuracy. The comparison uses different objects of trust and should not be overstated.

Status:

VERIFIED / QUALIFY COMPARISON

## C05

Candidate claim:

Across the Reuters Institute's 2026 surveyed markets, follow-up questions are the most common reported AI-chatbot news use among AI-news users.

Evidence:

Across 45 markets, 42% of AI-chatbot news users reported asking follow-up questions. Other reported uses include getting latest news, summarisation, making news easier to understand, and source evaluation.

Source:

https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/emerging-uses-ai-chatbots-news-and-what-it-means-journalism

Tier:

A

Confidence:

HIGH

Caveat:

This describes the subset already using AI chatbots for news. It should not be generalized to all news consumers.

Status:

VERIFIED

## C06

Candidate claim:

Younger AI-news users are more likely than older users to report using chatbots to make news easier to understand.

Evidence:

Reuters Institute's 2026 young-audience synthesis reports that 48% of 18-24-year-old AI-news users used AI to make a news story easier to understand, compared with 27% of AI-news users aged 55+.

Source:

https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/understanding-young-news-audiences-time-rapid-change

Tier:

A/B

Confidence:

HIGH

Caveat:

The comparison is among AI-news users, not the full age groups. The behavior remains marginal at population level.

Status:

VERIFIED

## C07

Candidate claim:

A prior Philippine qualitative study already found that younger adults wanted news experiences that were easier to understand and more interactive.

Evidence:

Celine Isabelle Samson reports focus groups and one-on-one interviews with 20 Filipinos aged 18-34. She writes that a recurring request was for news to be more interesting and easier to understand, with more interaction.

Source:

https://verafiles.org/articles/how-to-encourage-millennials-and-gen-z-to-build-an-appetite-for-news

Tier:

B - original qualitative reporting

Confidence:

MEDIUM-HIGH

Caveat:

Small nonrepresentative sample; full research protocol is not published in the article. This evidence is primarily an originality constraint for C029, not a national prevalence claim.

Status:

VERIFIED AS PRIOR REPORTING / DO NOT DUPLICATE

## C08

Candidate claim:

Interview-only recall can differ from actual news behavior, so a stronger qualitative design combines retrospective and real-time observation.

Evidence:

Methodological scholarship on interview-based news-use research recommends careful follow-up and combining retrospective and real-time approaches to distinguish stated preferences from observed behavior.

Source:

https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1461670X.2020.1716829

Tier:

A - peer-reviewed methodology

Confidence:

HIGH

Caveat:

Methodological guidance, not a public substantive finding about Filipino readers.

Status:

VERIFIED / METHOD ONLY

## C09

Candidate claim:

Young news users can employ multiple verification and navigation tactics across platforms rather than following one linear source path.

Evidence:

Swart's qualitative research with 36 young people identifies tactics including checking other online sources, checking the source/author, consulting comments, relying on known others, and other forms of cross-verification.

Source:

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14614448211011447

Tier:

A - peer-reviewed qualitative research

Confidence:

HIGH for the study population

Caveat:

Netherlands sample and pre-generative-AI fieldwork. Use to inform C029 coding and method, not to claim Filipino behavior.

Status:

VERIFIED / METHOD + BACKGROUND ONLY

## Evidence gap before draft

No evidence currently establishes:

- the specific types of second questions younger Filipino business/technology readers ask;
- which next-step paths dominate in actual use;
- when readers choose AI over search, a second outlet, an official source, a creator, comments, or no action;
- what makes readers stop searching and feel adequately informed;
- whether the observed behavior is meaningfully different across story types or reader backgrounds.

Those questions require C029 fieldwork.
