# C029 Contradictions and Failure Cases

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

This file records evidence and interpretations that can weaken the emerging `second question after the headline` thesis.

## 1. AI-news use is still a minority behavior

Philippine weekly chatbot news use was reported at 9% in the 2026 Digital News Report coverage, including 11% among 18-24-year-olds.

Implication:

Do not write as though AI has become the normal second step for Filipino news consumers. If AI is central to the final story, the article must say clearly that it describes an emerging minority behavior.

## 2. AI-news users may be unusually interested in news

Reuters Institute describes AI-chatbot news users globally as relatively curious, experimental `power news consumers` with complex multi-platform news diets.

Implication:

Follow-up behavior may reflect high curiosity rather than confusion, poor journalism, or low literacy.

Fieldwork must not pre-classify a follow-up question as evidence that a story failed.

## 3. Using AI does not mean trusting AI

Philippine DNR coverage reports trust in AI-chatbot answers at 15%, even while a subset of users employ chatbots for summaries, follow-up questions, and source assessment.

Implication:

A participant may use AI as a convenience while still cross-checking it or distrusting it. The final story should distinguish tool use from epistemic trust.

## 4. The full article may already answer the question

A headline, TikTok clip, social post, or push notification is often only an entry point. Some unanswered questions after first exposure may disappear when the participant opens and reads the full article.

Implication:

C029 must record the exact first-exposure surface and whether the participant actually consumed the source article before treating a question as `missing context`.

## 5. Some readers may not want a second step

The existence of possible follow-up questions does not mean readers will pursue them. People may decide the story is not important enough, trust the first source, feel satisfied, feel overwhelmed, or simply move on.

Implication:

`No follow-up` and `question formed but no action` are essential negative cases, not unusable observations.

## 6. Creators can meet explanation needs without AI

The 2026 Philippine DNR coverage reports that creators are widely used and are perceived by some users as more entertaining, relatable, easy to understand, and up-to-date than traditional news brands.

Implication:

The second-step story may be about a broader information chain involving creators, search, official sources, communities, and other people, with AI as only one branch.

## 7. Prior Philippine research already found a clarity demand

Celine Isabelle Samson's 2024 interviews/focus groups with 20 Filipinos aged 18-34 found requests for news that was easier to understand and more interactive.

Implication:

If C029's fieldwork only produces the same preference statement, the story has not earned a new article. The contribution must be behavioral and more specific.

## 8. Controlled tasks can manufacture behavior

If Portfilo gives every participant a deliberately complex story and asks them to `find out more`, participants may perform more searching, cross-checking, or AI use than they normally would.

Implication:

Natural diary encounters must be analyzed separately from controlled walkthrough behavior. The interview should not direct participants to use AI or any specific source until after their natural path is observed.

## 9. Founder-network recruitment can bias the sample

Portfilo's easiest participants may be friends, founders, developers, students from similar schools, or people already interested in the publication.

Implication:

Recruitment must deliberately include non-technology/business backgrounds and people who are not Portfilo readers or supporters.

If this cannot be achieved, narrow the story's description of the sample rather than implying broad Filipino behavior.

## 10. Business and technology are sometimes genuinely complex

Some news requires prior knowledge because the underlying subject is complex. A question after reading about interest rates, data-center capacity, AI deployment, payment rails, or company finance is not automatically evidence of bad writing.

Implication:

C029 should study reader sensemaking, not grade journalists for failing to eliminate all complexity.

## 11. Self-report data have limits

Digital News Report findings and diary entries rely partly on participants' own recollection and classification of behavior.

Implication:

Use real-time walkthrough evidence to check how stated habits compare with observed behavior, while avoiding the opposite mistake of treating one artificial task as more `true` than a person's normal life.

## 12. A stronger alternative story may emerge

Possible reframes if fieldwork weakens the original thesis:

### Reframe A - The question people do not pursue

If participants regularly identify something they do not understand but rarely seek answers, the stronger story may be about friction, attention, perceived importance, or the cost of following up.

### Reframe B - Search still does most of the work

If conventional search, opening the full article, and checking another outlet dominate while AI is marginal, publish only if the observed search/sensemaking chain itself is sufficiently original.

### Reframe C - Trust and convenience pull in different directions

If participants use creators or AI despite explicitly trusting established or official sources more, the story may become about convenience versus trust rather than comprehension alone.

### Kill

Kill C029 if the fieldwork produces no recurring behavior beyond findings already established by the Digital News Report and Samson's 2024 Philippine youth-news research.
