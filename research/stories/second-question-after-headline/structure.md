## Structural problem

C029 has two tempting but weak structures to avoid:

1. `Here are statistics showing young people struggle with news, therefore Portfilo is needed.`
2. `AI is changing news, here are five ways people use chatbots.`

The first becomes promotional. The second duplicates Reuters Institute / VERA Files reporting.

The structure should instead be built around **observed reader behavior**.

## Recommended architecture - behavior first

### 1. One first encounter, then the next question

Open with a real participant encountering a business/technology story in the format they actually saw it: feed post, video, headline, article, creator clip, or other surface.

The opening should establish:

- what they saw;
- what they understood immediately;
- the first question that remained;
- what they did next.

Do not open with Portfilo, a mission statement, or a broad claim about `today's information overload`.

### 2. Establish that the behavior is real but not universal

Use the strongest current Philippine Digital News Report evidence briefly:

- platform-led news use;
- AI chatbots remain a minority news interface;
- among Philippine AI-news users, summaries/follow-up questions/simplification/source assessment are material uses;
- trust in AI answers remains low.

The purpose is to establish the environment around the participant behavior, not to turn the article into a DNR recap.

### 3. What are the second questions?

This should be the original reporting core.

Use the fieldwork to identify the recurring questions that appear after first exposure.

Possible categories to test, not pre-write:

- What does that mean?
- How does it work?
- Why does it matter?
- Compared with what?
- Where did that claim come from?
- Is this source credible?
- What happened before this?
- What changes for a company, worker, customer, or reader?

The final categories must come from observed evidence rather than the provisional coding sheet.

### 4. Where people go next

Reconstruct the actual information pathways:

- full article;
- search;
- official document/source;
- another news outlet;
- creator/video;
- comments/community;
- friend/colleague;
- AI chatbot;
- no action.

This section should show that `getting informed` can be a sequence rather than one source encounter.

Do not imply that longer sequences are automatically better or more literate.

### 5. AI's actual role, if fieldwork supports it

Only make AI a major section if participants use it spontaneously or describe recent real use.

Useful tension to test:

> A tool can be convenient enough to use without being trusted enough to end the search.

Compare observed behavior with the 2026 Philippine DNR findings on follow-up/summarisation and low trust.

If fieldwork shows AI is peripheral, shrink this section accordingly and keep the story under `Technology` rather than forcing `AI` as the primary topic.

### 6. What makes someone stop?

A second question does not create an infinite research loop.

Use the fieldwork to identify stopping rules:

- answer found;
- enough confidence;
- trusted source;
- time/effort cost;
- low personal relevance;
- information conflict;
- simple loss of attention.

This section can prevent the article from romanticizing exhaustive research.

### 7. End with the information behavior, not Portfilo

The conclusion should state only what the reporting earned.

A likely conceptual ending, if supported, is that first exposure and understanding are different stages for some readers, and modern news use can involve a chain of questions and sources.

Do not end with:

- `That is why we built Portfilo.`
- `Portfilo will answer the second question.`
- a subscription pitch disguised as a conclusion;
- advice that every reader must research every headline.

Portfilo's relevance should be visible through the reporting method, not explained in the article.

## Fallback architecture - if AI is weaker than expected

If fieldwork shows search, full articles, official sources, creators, or communities dominate and AI is marginal, preserve the core question but frame the story as a Technology/Reported piece about **how readers assemble understanding after first exposure**.

The DNR AI findings can then remain a small emerging-behavior section rather than the main frame.

## Reframe architecture - if questions form but go unanswered

If the dominant finding is that participants know what they do not understand but rarely pursue it, the story should not claim active second-step sensemaking.

A stronger structure would become:

1. the unresolved question;
2. why the participant does not follow it;
3. recurring friction across the sample;
4. what kinds of stories earn extra effort;
5. where convenience and trust affect the decision to continue.

That would be a different finding and should receive a revised headline/core before drafting.

## Evidence required for each structural section

| Section | Required evidence |
|---|---|
| Opening | one documented participant encounter and consented quote/observation |
| Environment | current DNR / Philippine data, accurately scoped |
| Second questions | recurring fieldwork pattern with negative cases |
| Next paths | observed or contemporaneously recorded actions |
| AI | spontaneous participant behavior plus DNR context; not interviewer prompting |
| Stopping rules | participant explanations linked to actual encounters |
| Conclusion | synthesis no stronger than the qualitative evidence |

## Headline direction

Do not lock a headline before fieldwork.

`The second question after the headline` is a useful internal working label because it describes the reporting question without making a factual conclusion.

The final headline must reflect what participants actually do. If AI is not central, it should not be inserted for topical heat.
