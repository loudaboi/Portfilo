# C029 Source Map

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Source strategy

This file records the documentary source base for C029 before fieldwork.

The central publishable contribution cannot come from these sources alone. They establish the environment, prior findings, and methodological constraints. The original story must still be carried by Portfilo's own Philippine reader research if that fieldwork produces a meaningful result.

## S01 - Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026: AI chatbots and news

Source:

https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/emerging-uses-ai-chatbots-news-and-what-it-means-journalism

Type / tier: `A - original survey research / research institution`

Date: 2026-06-16

Why it matters:

Across 45 markets, the report says asking follow-up questions was the most common news use among AI-chatbot news users at 42%. It also reports use for summarisation, making news easier to understand, source evaluation, and compiling information from different outlets.

Limitations:

- AI-news users are a minority of the overall population;
- survey responses are self-reported behavior;
- cross-market results do not establish Philippine-specific behavior;
- AI use does not establish that journalism caused the need for follow-up.

## S02 - Reuters Institute Digital News Report 2026: Philippines

Source:

https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2026/philippines

Type / tier: `A - original survey research / country report`

Date: 2026-06-16

Author: Yvonne T. Chua

Why it matters:

Establishes the current Philippine news environment: social media remain the dominant source of news, news avoidance is high, and direct access through traditional channels and publisher websites has weakened.

Limitations:

- online survey, weighted to population targets but not identical to a census;
- measures news use broadly, not Portfilo's intended business/technology audience;
- platform use does not itself prove comprehension problems.

## S03 - VERA Files: Filipino trust in news posts biggest fall - Digital News Report 2026

Source:

https://verafiles.org/articles/filipino-trust-in-news-posts-biggest-fall-digital-news-report-2026

Type / tier: `B - strong secondary reporting based on original DNR data`

Date: 2026-06-16

Author: Yvonne T. Chua, Philippine DNR country author

Why it matters:

Provides detailed Philippine DNR figures not surfaced clearly on the country landing page, including:

- social/video networks at 70% weekly news use versus 42% for news websites/apps;
- weekly AI-chatbot news use at 9%, including 11% among 18-24s and 3% among 55+;
- among weekly generative-AI news users, 52% used it for summaries, 48% for follow-up questions, 39% to find or assess a source, and 36% to simplify stories;
- trust in AI-chatbot answers at 15%;
- creator use and perceptions that creators can be easier to understand.

Limitations:

- the detailed figures should be traced back to the DNR dataset/report materials where practical before final publication;
- the AI-use percentages apply to AI-news users, not all Filipinos;
- low trust and AI use can coexist, so usage must not be presented as endorsement.

## S04 - Reuters Institute: Understanding young news audiences at a time of rapid change

Source:

https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/understanding-young-news-audiences-time-rapid-change

Type / tier: `A/B - research synthesis drawing on DNR data and prior studies`

Date: 2026-03

Why it matters:

Reports that younger AI-news users use chatbots more often for navigation and sensemaking, including making news easier to understand. It also explicitly cautions that this behavior is still marginal.

Limitations:

- multi-market, not Philippine-specific;
- younger users are not a homogeneous group;
- cannot establish why a specific reader sought clarification.

## S05 - VERA Files: How to encourage millennials and Gen Z to build an appetite for news

Source:

https://verafiles.org/articles/how-to-encourage-millennials-and-gen-z-to-build-an-appetite-for-news

Type / tier: `B - original qualitative reporting / fellowship research`

Date: 2024-09-03

Author: Celine Isabelle Samson

Why it matters:

Samson reports focus groups and one-on-one interviews with 20 Filipinos aged 18-34. A key finding was a request for news that is easier to understand and more interactive.

Originality implication:

Portfilo must not reproduce this study as `young people want clearer news`. C029 needs a narrower observed-behavior contribution: the specific questions and pathways after first exposure.

Limitations:

- small qualitative sample;
- published article does not provide a full academic methods appendix;
- findings are useful context, not prevalence estimates.

## S06 - Swart: Tactics of news literacy

Source:

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/14614448211011447

Type / tier: `A - peer-reviewed qualitative research`

Publication: New Media & Society

Why it matters:

Based on 36 in-depth interviews with young people aged 16-22, the study examines actual strategies used to access, evaluate, and engage with news on social media. It supports a user-practice approach rather than treating news literacy as an abstract skill.

Methodological value:

- start from everyday news practices;
- record cross-verification and source-evaluation behavior;
- avoid assuming that knowing a verification tactic means a person actually uses it.

Limitations:

- Netherlands sample;
- conducted before widespread generative-AI use;
- does not establish Philippine behavior.

## S07 - Hendrickx: How young adults encounter and experience news on Instagram and TikTok

Source:

https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/14614448241255955

Type / tier: `A - peer-reviewed qualitative research`

Why it matters:

Uses 25 in-depth interviews and a walkthrough method to study how young adults encounter news on Instagram and TikTok.

Methodological value:

Supports observing or reconstructing the platform encounter itself rather than relying only on abstract interview questions about news habits.

Limitations:

- Belgian sample;
- platform-specific;
- not a direct study of follow-up questions.

## S08 - Peters et al.: Seeing, Thinking, Feeling - interview-based methods for studying news use

Source:

https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1461670X.2020.1716829

Type / tier: `A - peer-reviewed methodological research`

Why it matters:

The paper argues that retrospective interviews can diverge from actual behavior and recommends combining retrospective and real-time approaches where possible.

Methodological value:

Directly supports C029's combined diary + walkthrough design.

Limitations:

Methodological guidance, not evidence for the story's substantive conclusion.

## S09 - Reuters Institute 2024 user-needs research

Source:

https://reutersinstitute.politics.ox.ac.uk/digital-news-report/2024/more-just-facts-how-news-audiences-think-about-user-needs

Type / tier: `A/B - original survey research / research synthesis`

Why it matters:

Shows that audiences value `educate me` and `give me perspective` alongside `update me`, supporting the distinction between first-level updates and deeper understanding.

Limitations:

- broad news needs, not C029's specific second-step behavior;
- not Philippine-business/technology specific;
- should remain background rather than the story's central evidence.

## Source gap

The decisive source gap is not another national survey.

It is direct evidence of the **sequence**:

`encounter -> unanswered question -> next action -> stopping point`

in actual business/technology news use by a small, clearly described Philippine sample.
