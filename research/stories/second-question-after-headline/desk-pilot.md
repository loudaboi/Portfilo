# C029 Desk-Research Pilot

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Status

**PILOT / PRESELECTED EVENTS / DISCUSSION SEARCH NOT YET USED FOR SELECTION**

This pilot tests whether C029 can be reframed into a rigorous desk-reported story without participant fieldwork.

The unit of analysis is the **publicly observable follow-up question**, not the private behavior, age, intent, or representativeness of the person asking it.

The pilot does not authorize population claims about Filipino readers and does not establish what people privately do after encountering news.

## Pilot question

> **What questions surface in public discussion around Philippine business and technology news after the initial update?**

Working reader-facing formulation:

> **What comes after `what happened?` in Philippine business and technology news?**

## Selection rule

Events are selected from recent credible Philippine business/technology coverage **before** reviewing related public discussion.

Once selected, an event remains in the pilot even if:

- no related public discussion can be located;
- the discussion contains no usable information-seeking questions;
- the questions are already answered in the source story;
- the discussion is dominated by opinion, rhetoric, jokes, abuse, or unrelated argument.

Do not replace a weak event with a more viral event after inspecting comments.

This rule exists to reduce cherry-picking and confirmation bias.

## Discussion-source hierarchy for the pilot

For each preselected event, search in this order where accessible:

1. direct public discussion attached to or clearly responding to the exact source event;
2. public Reddit threads clearly tied to the same event or underlying claim;
3. public YouTube discussion from credible news/publisher coverage of the same event;
4. other openly accessible discussion only when the event match is unambiguous.

Do not infer demographic identity from usernames or platform choice.

Do not use private groups, private messages, scraped personal profiles, or deleted content.

## Inclusion rule for a usable question

Include a question only when it genuinely seeks information, explanation, evidence, comparison, consequence, mechanism, responsibility, context, or next-step meaning.

Exclude:

- rhetorical questions whose main function is insult or advocacy;
- jokes and sarcasm;
- partisan slogans;
- questions unrelated to the event;
- duplicate paraphrases by the same account in the same thread;
- statements turned into question form without an information need.

## Provisional coding scheme

Question codes are hypotheses and may be revised after the pilot:

- `DEFINITION` — what does a term or claim mean?
- `MECHANISM` — how does it work?
- `BACKGROUND` — what happened before / what context is missing?
- `CONSEQUENCE` — what changes for workers, firms, users, communities or institutions?
- `COMPARISON` — compared with what / how large or unusual is this?
- `EVIDENCE` — what supports the claim or number?
- `CREDIBILITY` — should the source/claim be believed?
- `RESPONSIBILITY` — who decides, pays, regulates or bears responsibility?
- `LOCAL_RELEVANCE` — what does this mean for the Philippines or a particular locality?
- `NEXT` — what happens next?
- `ACTION` — what should a person, company or institution do?
- `OTHER` — substantive information-seeking question outside the above.

## Article-answer comparison

Each usable question receives one article-relation code:

- `ANSWERED` — source story clearly answers the question;
- `PARTIAL` — source story gives some relevant information but leaves a material part unresolved;
- `UNANSWERED` — source story does not answer it;
- `EXPANDS_FRAME` — question raises a materially different but relevant issue the update was not principally trying to answer;
- `UNCLEAR` — relationship cannot be coded confidently.

This comparison is necessary because a public question does not by itself prove deficient reporting. The person may have read only a headline, missed information already present, or introduced a new concern beyond the story's scope.

## Pilot success conditions

Proceed to a full desk-reported C029 only if the six-event pilot shows all of the following:

1. usable public questions can be recovered across multiple events rather than one viral case;
2. the coding scheme can be applied with limited ambiguity;
3. the article-answer comparison produces more than one relationship type, including negative/contradictory cases;
4. the result adds something beyond the already-known finding that audiences want news to be easier to understand;
5. the story can be written without demographic, prevalence or behavioral-path claims;
6. the conclusion does not require `Portfilo is the solution` framing.

## Reframe / stop conditions

Keep C029 held or reframe again if:

- only one or two events yield usable questions;
- public discussion is overwhelmingly rhetorical or unrelated;
- questions are mostly already answered by the source stories and the stronger finding cannot be established without knowing whether commenters read them;
- platform effects dominate so strongly that the corpus says more about one community than about news information needs;
- coding requires subjective inference about what commenters meant;
- the only publishable conclusion remains `people want more context`;
- the project would need to generalize from commenters to Filipino readers.
