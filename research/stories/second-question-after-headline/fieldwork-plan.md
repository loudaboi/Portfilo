# C029 Fieldwork Plan

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Objective

Observe the sequence between first exposure to a business/technology news item and the point where a younger Filipino reader either seeks more information or decides to stop.

The fieldwork is designed to answer:

> What is the reader's next question, what do they do with it, and where does AI fit if it appears naturally?

## Design principle

Use both:

1. **natural encounters** captured close to when they happen; and
2. **real-time walkthroughs** during an interview.

Do not rely only on abstract questions such as `Where do you usually get news?` or `Do you use AI to understand stories?`

Interview-method research on news use warns that stated habits and observed behavior can diverge. The combined design allows Portfilo to compare what participants remember, what they report in the moment, and what they actually do during a task.

## Target sample

Target: `12-15` completed participants.

Minimum publish-decision threshold: `10` complete records.

Age:

- approximately half `18-24` where practical;
- approximately half `25-34` where practical.

Deliberate variation:

- students and working adults;
- technology/business-adjacent and non-adjacent backgrounds;
- frequent and occasional business/technology news consumers;
- AI-information users and non-users;
- participants outside the founder's immediate friend/supporter circle;
- some geographic variation beyond Metro Manila if practical.

This is purposive qualitative recruitment, not probability sampling. Do not impose quotas that create false representativeness.

## Recruitment screener

Collect only what is necessary:

- age band: 18-24 / 25-34;
- broad location: NCR / Luzon outside NCR / Visayas / Mindanao;
- student / employed / self-employed / other;
- broad field of study/work;
- how often they encounter business/technology news: daily / several times weekly / weekly / less often;
- main discovery channels: publisher site/app, Facebook, YouTube, TikTok, X, Instagram, Reddit/forums, messaging, search, creators, other;
- whether they have used an AI chatbot for news or current-information questions in the past month: yes / no / unsure;
- whether they already know or regularly read Portfilo: yes / no.

Do not screen out non-AI users.

Do not recruit only high-news-interest participants.

## Part 1 - Natural news-encounter diary

Duration: `3 days`.

Ask each participant to record up to `3` naturally encountered business/technology news items during the period.

If they encounter none, record that rather than instructing them to manufacture one.

For each encounter, capture:

1. date/time;
2. where it appeared: platform/app/site/person;
3. link or screenshot where practical;
4. format: headline/post, short video, long video, full article, creator commentary, discussion thread, AI answer, other;
5. source/creator/outlet if identifiable;
6. in one sentence, what the participant thinks happened;
7. `What is the first thing, if anything, that you still want to know?`;
8. whether they did anything next;
9. if yes, the exact next action and where they went;
10. whether they found an answer;
11. what made them stop.

Avoid giving example follow-up questions before they answer; examples could prime the result.

## Part 2 - Semi-structured interview

Duration: approximately `40-50 minutes`.

### A. Reconstruct one natural encounter

Choose one diary entry with the participant.

Ask them to reconstruct the sequence:

- What made you notice it?
- What did you think it meant at first?
- What, if anything, felt unresolved?
- What did you do next?
- Why that source/tool?
- What would have made you choose another source?
- At what point did you feel you knew enough?
- Was there anything you still did not trust or understand?

Ask for concrete examples rather than general statements.

### B. Real-time walkthrough

Use one controlled current Philippine business/technology story that the participant says they are not already familiar with.

Prepare `2-3` possible stimuli across different beats so a participant can be assigned one they do not already know.

The stimulus should be a real, accurately sourced current story, not a fabricated fixture.

Procedure:

1. show the first-exposure surface only first, such as the headline/dek or the actual social post where appropriate;
2. ask the participant to say what they think happened;
3. ask what, if anything, they want to know next;
4. say: `You can use your phone/browser the way you normally would if you wanted to understand this better.`;
5. observe the path without suggesting search, AI, creators, or any particular outlet;
6. record the sequence of sources/tools opened and the queries/questions used;
7. ask the participant to say when they would normally stop.

Only after the natural path is complete may the interviewer ask whether AI, another outlet, an official source, a creator, comments, or another person would ever be part of their process.

### C. Reflection

Ask:

- Was that similar to how you normally handle a story you do not fully understand?
- What kinds of stories are worth the extra effort to you?
- What makes you abandon the search?
- Which sources or tools are fast but not fully trusted?
- Which are trusted but inconvenient?
- If you use AI, what do you trust it to do and what do you still check elsewhere?

## Data to retain

With participant consent:

- diary entries;
- interview notes or recordings;
- exact participant wording selected as candidate quotes;
- screen/path notes from the walkthrough;
- links/screenshots of the news surfaces used;
- coding sheet;
- consent preference: named / first-name only / anonymized / background-only.

Do not collect private account information, passwords, personal messages, or unrelated browsing history.

If screen-sharing is used, tell participants to close unrelated private content first and pause recording whenever needed.

## Provisional coding framework

Do not force every observation into these categories. Add or split codes when fieldwork requires it.

### Follow-up question type

- `DEFINITION` - What does this term mean?
- `BACKGROUND` - What happened before this?
- `MECHANISM` - How does this actually work?
- `CONSEQUENCE` - What changes because of this?
- `COMPARISON` - Is this large/small/good/bad relative to what?
- `CREDIBILITY` - Is this source/claim reliable?
- `EVIDENCE` - Where did this number/claim come from?
- `LOCAL_RELEVANCE` - What does this mean in the Philippines / for me / for my work?
- `ACTION` - What should someone do differently because of this?
- `OTHER`
- `NO_QUESTION`

### Next action

- open/read full original article;
- check another news outlet;
- use search engine;
- open official source/document;
- watch/search creator or video explanation;
- read comments/community/forum;
- use AI chatbot;
- ask friend/colleague/teacher;
- save for later;
- stop / no action;
- other.

### Stopping reason

- answer found;
- enough confidence without full answer;
- source trusted;
- too much effort/time;
- not important enough;
- too confusing;
- conflicting information;
- distrusted available sources;
- distracted/moved on;
- other.

## Analysis rules

- Do not calculate population prevalence from this sample.
- Counts inside the sample may be used descriptively in the research packet, but published wording should keep the denominator explicit.
- Preserve negative cases.
- Separate natural diary behavior from controlled-task behavior before combining patterns.
- Separate `question formed` from `question pursued`.
- Separate `tool used` from `tool trusted`.
- Separate `AI used spontaneously` from `AI mentioned after prompting`.
- Do not infer that a journalist should have answered every follow-up question in the original story.
- Do not infer low literacy from an unanswered question.

## Publish / reframe threshold

### PUBLISH candidate if

- at least 10 complete records exist;
- recurring question types appear across more than isolated anecdotes;
- actual next-step behavior can be documented;
- AI's role, if discussed, comes from spontaneous behavior or prior actual use rather than interviewer suggestion;
- the findings materially extend beyond `young people want clearer news`;
- the final story can survive without mentioning Portfilo as the solution.

### REFRAME if

- the strongest pattern is `questions form but people usually do not pursue them`;
- convenience versus trust becomes more explanatory than comprehension;
- creators/search/official sources matter substantially more than AI;
- the controlled task reveals behavior that natural diaries do not.

### KILL if

- the results merely restate existing DNR and VERA Files findings;
- recruitment remains too narrow to support even a useful qualitative comparison;
- the central story requires causal or prevalence claims the method cannot establish.

## Stimulus-selection rule

Do not lock the controlled stories until fieldwork begins.

At that point select current, factual, reasonably complex Philippine business/technology stories from strong sources. Avoid choosing stories Portfilo has already published, because familiarity with Portfilo's own explanatory style could bias the task.

Each stimulus should have a clear source trail so the interviewer can later evaluate whether participant follow-up sources were accurate without treating the participant's search path as a fact-checking test.
