# C030 Headline and Dek Review

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Status

**RECOMMENDATION READY / USER REVIEW REQUIRED**

The headline should describe the article's editorial instinct without becoming permanent Portfilo brand copy.

## Option A - recommended

### Headline

**What actually changed?**

### Dek

An update tells you something happened. Portfilo is interested in what changes when the announcement meets operating reality.

### Assessment

Strongest option.

Why:

- short and concrete;
- already earned by the argument;
- works across Business, Technology, Startups, AI, and Building;
- does not overstate a claim;
- leaves room for the article to explain the idea rather than summarizing it in the headline.

Risk:

It can become slogan-like if repeated elsewhere. Keep it as this story's headline/concept, not a permanent tagline.

## Option B

### Headline

**The story after the announcement**

### Dek

Business and technology often become more useful to understand once plans turn into operations, costs, constraints, and consequences.

### Assessment

Clearer as an article title, but slightly more media-about-media and less distinctive than Option A.

## Option C

### Headline

**When the update is only the beginning**

### Dek

Some developments are clear from the first report. Others change meaning once the announcement meets the way a company, market, product, or system actually works.

### Assessment

Accurate and reader-friendly, but longer and closer to generic explanatory-journalism language.

## Option D

### Headline

**Where plans meet reality**

### Dek

Why Portfilo covers business, technology, startups, AI, and building as parts of the same operating world.

### Assessment

Too close to brand-positioning language. Reject unless the article changes substantially.

## Recommendation

Use **Option A** for the next editorial gate.

Recommended metadata pair:

- title: `What actually changed?`
- description/dek: `An update tells you something happened. Portfilo is interested in what changes when the announcement meets operating reality.`

Do not promote either line into permanent homepage or masthead copy without a separate decision.
