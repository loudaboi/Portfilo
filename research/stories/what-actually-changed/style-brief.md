## Assignment

Story ID: `C030`

Working label: `What actually changed?`

Content type: `Perspective`

Primary topic: `Building` — provisional

Primary reader need: `Interpret`

## Writing job

Explain, through one bounded editorial argument, why Portfilo's Business, Technology, Startups, AI, and Building coverage can belong in the same publication: the useful story sometimes changes when an announcement, label, technology, investment, or rule is translated into what it changes in actual decisions and operations.

The piece must orient the reader, not advertise Portfilo.

## Audience decision

The intended reader can be assumed to understand ordinary business/technology developments such as a funding announcement, a company adopting a technology, a product launch, or a new rule.

The reader does **not** need to know Portfilo's internal planning language or editorial system.

Current institutional facts already established on `/about`:

- Portfilo is a business and technology publication;
- it covers Business, Technology, Startups, AI, and Building;
- it publishes multiple editorial forms;
- it is for people curious about business and technology.

Editorial hypothesis:

A reader may benefit from understanding why those coverage areas connect. Do not state that readers are confused, dissatisfied with existing media, or demanding this explanation.

## Evidence shape

The material contains:

- an authored editorial judgment;
- actual Portfilo coverage/product facts;
- one strong current example of Portfilo behavior in C009, where an industry phrase was translated into concrete roles and responsibilities;
- generic development patterns that can illustrate the argument without becoming empirical claims;
- a genuine limiting principle: an update is sometimes enough;
- no reported protagonist;
- no audience survey;
- no need for a current-event narrative;
- no basis for broad criticism of other journalism.

This is an argument plus limitation, not a reported feature or corporate launch note.

## Style-research findings used

Source:

`style-research.md`

### Finding 1 — editorial premise should be demonstrated before it is branded

Observed practice:

Comparable editorial-direction pieces become more credible when a concrete tension, experience, or actual piece of work appears before or alongside the institutional claim.

Portfilo interpretation:

C030 should not begin with `Portfilo believes`, `our mission`, or a five-topic coverage list.

Decision:

Open on one ordinary development pattern where the difference between the update and its practical consequence is easy to see. Move from that example to the thesis within the first 3-5 paragraphs.

### Finding 2 — explain connections through mechanisms, not category lists

Observed practice:

The strongest comparable pieces explain why subjects relate rather than merely enumerating beats.

Portfilo interpretation:

Business, Technology, Startups, AI, and Building should not each receive a symmetrical mini-paragraph.

Decision:

Use causal connections: technology enters workflows; workflows change costs, responsibilities and products; capital changes what can be built; building exposes pricing, distribution, hiring and operational constraints.

Use only the connections needed to make the argument.

### Finding 3 — institutional language needs restraint

Observed practice:

Founding pieces often rely heavily on `we`, promises, mission declarations, and future commitments.

Portfilo interpretation:

Those patterns are exactly where C030 can become corporate or AI-shaped.

Decision:

Most of the article should be ordinary argumentative prose. Use `Portfilo`, `we`, or `I` only where the publication/editor genuinely has to own a judgment.

### Finding 4 — actual work is stronger than adjectives

Observed practice:

404 Media and other launch pieces become most concrete when they show examples of the journalism rather than describing it with quality claims.

Portfilo interpretation:

One real Portfilo example is more useful than saying the publication is `practical`, `rigorous`, `curious`, or `different`.

Decision:

C009 is the preferred real example if one is needed. Do not turn C030 into a portfolio recap.

### Finding 5 — self-attack belongs in the argument

Observed practice:

Poynter's opinion guidance explicitly asks writers to attack their own premise.

Portfilo interpretation:

C030's strongest limitation is not a disclaimer; it is part of the editorial judgment.

Decision:

State before the conclusion that short updates can be exactly what a reader needs. A second layer is worth pursuing only when it materially changes understanding.

### Finding 6 — common launch endings are wrong for C030

Observed practice:

Several founding pieces end with promises, reader invitations, subscription asks, or elevated brand formulations.

Portfilo interpretation:

C030 does not need a launch flourish because the site already has About, Standards, and Subscribe surfaces.

Decision:

End on the bounded editorial distinction, then stop.

## Selected Portfilo recipe

Selected recipe: `Perspective`

Why it fits:

The central value is an authored editorial judgment about how Portfilo interprets developments across its coverage. The piece has a thesis, examples, and a meaningful limitation. It is not primarily reporting new facts, explaining a technical system, or giving the reader steps.

Default Perspective motion retained:

1. concrete tension belonging to the argument;
2. thesis early;
3. small number of examples/connections;
4. strongest limitation;
5. fair response/qualification;
6. bounded ending that advances the argument rather than restating it grandly.

## Deviations

### 1. No autobiographical founder opening

Reason:

Some comparators use personal history effectively because they have genuine lived/reported material that creates the publication premise. C030 does not need that material, and inventing or stretching autobiography would distort the assignment.

### 2. No subheads by default

Reason:

At this length, continuous essay form better distinguishes C030 from the About and Standards pages and avoids turning the editorial idea into a branded framework.

### 3. Prefer one real Portfilo example maximum

Reason:

The job is to explain the editorial connection, not prove the publication's catalog. One actual example can ground the argument; more can turn the piece into self-promotion.

### 4. Do not force the existing six-part structure literally

Reason:

The earlier structure remains useful as editorial history, but its repeated examples helped produce the repetition identified in the old adversarial review. The new draft should preserve the logical jobs while compressing them into a shorter continuous argument.

## Lead job

Start with **one** ordinary development pattern, not the previous four-item montage.

Preferred opening territory:

A company announces that it is adopting AI. The announcement establishes a real event; the editorial distinction appears when the story asks what changed in an actual workflow, cost, responsibility, or decision because of that adoption.

Why this lead fits:

- it is familiar without requiring a named current company;
- it connects AI, Technology, Business, and Building naturally;
- it demonstrates the argument rather than announcing the publication's mission;
- it does not require the reader to accept that the announcement was misleading.

Opening forms rejected:

- four-announcement list;
- `Portfilo believes...`;
- `Why we built Portfilo...`;
- founder autobiography;
- claim that media only reports announcements;
- `In a rapidly changing world...`;
- rhetorical-question stack;
- dramatic sequence of one-line paragraphs.

## Nut / thesis position

By paragraph 3-5, state the editorial distinction plainly:

**Internal specification, not final wording:** Portfilo's subjects connect because the meaning of a business or technology development often becomes clearer when the update is followed into the choices, operations, economics, constraints, or responsibilities it changes.

The thesis must also leave room for the later limitation that this second layer is not always necessary.

## Paragraph development

Normal paragraphs should be multi-sentence and perform one active job:

- establish the opening distinction;
- explain a causal connection between subjects;
- develop one example;
- qualify the premise;
- state the bounded implication.

Do not use a line break to manufacture emphasis after every sentence.

## Middle logic

No polished subheads are planned. These are paragraph-block jobs.

### Block 1 — establish the editorial distinction

Carry:

- one ordinary AI-adoption example;
- the difference between knowing an announcement occurred and knowing what it changed;
- early thesis.

### Block 2 — explain why Portfilo's topics connect

Carry:

A short causal chain rather than a five-beat list. Technology enters companies through products and workflows; companies make decisions about cost, people and markets; startups face capital, distribution and operating constraints; building turns those abstractions into choices.

Do not claim every story involves every topic.

### Block 3 — show the distinction in another form

Preferred real example:

C009. `Higher-value work` was an industry phrase; the published Explained story examined what concrete jobs and responsibilities sat underneath it and also established what the available evidence could not prove.

Job:

Show that `what changed / what does this mean concretely` can apply to language and categories, not only announcements.

Constraint:

One concise reference, not a summary of C009.

Optional alternative if the paragraph becomes self-referential:

Use a generic startup funding example instead. Do not force C009 merely because it is available.

### Block 4 — state the limiting principle

Carry:

- an update can be useful and complete on its own;
- speed and narrowness can be editorial virtues;
- extra depth is justified only when the next question changes understanding.

This is the main self-attack on the premise.

### Block 5 — bounded conclusion

Carry:

The implication for Portfilo's editorial judgment without promises or slogans.

End after the distinction is clear.

## Evidence and example handling

- Generic AI/funding/product examples must remain hypothetical/illustrative and must not imply measured industry behavior.
- C009 can be referenced as actual Portfilo editorial behavior because it is published and its deployed review has passed.
- Do not introduce new factual claims from C001 or C021.
- Do not use named external companies/policies solely to make the essay feel reported.
- No statistics are needed unless the assignment materially changes and returns to research.

## Transition behavior

Transitions should follow causal or argumentative logic rather than editorial-signpost formulas.

Avoid repeated:

- `That is why...`;
- `What matters is...`;
- `The real question is...`;
- `In practice...`;
- `This is where...`;
- `The point is...`;
- `At the same time...` when no actual contrast exists;
- `not X, but Y` constructions.

The exact title phrase `What actually changed?` should not become a repeated refrain.

## Person and rhetorical devices

First-person singular:

None required. At most 1-2 uses if the draft needs Louda to explicitly own an editorial judgment. No autobiographical narrative is authorized.

First-person plural / `Portfilo`:

Restrained. Use only for an institutional statement whose provenance is documented below.

Second person:

Avoid by default.

Rhetorical questions:

Zero in the lead by default. At most one in the full body if a real argumentative need appears during drafting.

Other devices:

- no recurring metaphor;
- no named framework;
- no three-part slogan;
- no `intersection of X and Y` positioning;
- no motivational escalation.

## Counterevidence / limitation

Strongest limitation:

A short update is not an inferior form. Sometimes the useful fact is simply that something happened, and adding more layers can delay or clutter the answer rather than improve it.

Placement:

After the article has demonstrated why a second question can matter, before the conclusion.

Response:

The editorial decision is conditional: pursue the additional layer when it materially changes what the reader would understand about the company, product, market, technology, rule, or decision.

Do not claim that Portfilo always goes deeper or that depth itself is quality.

## Ending job

End with the bounded implication of the limitation: the publication should distinguish between developments where the update is sufficient and developments where implementation, economics, constraints, or consequences change the meaning.

Prohibited endings:

- `That is why we built Portfilo.`;
- `This is where Portfilo lives.`;
- `We will keep asking...` as a slogan;
- subscription invitation;
- list of promises;
- restatement of all five topics;
- grand claim about the future of journalism;
- motivational statement;
- final rhetorical question.

## Voice and vocabulary constraints

Use concrete editorial/business/technology language only where it clarifies the example:

- workflow;
- cost;
- capacity;
- implementation;
- pricing;
- distribution;
- responsibility;
- operation;
- constraint;
- decision.

Avoid strategy-deck or publication-marketing vocabulary:

- `editorial lens` in public prose;
- `content ecosystem`;
- `at the intersection of`;
- `operating reality` as a recurring catchphrase;
- `mission`;
- `value proposition`;
- `connect the dots`;
- `our approach`;
- `our philosophy`;
- `future-facing`;
- `practical intelligence`;
- `second-order effects` unless technically required;
- `deeper story` as an automatic quality claim.
