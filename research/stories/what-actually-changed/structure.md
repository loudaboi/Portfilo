# C030 Structure Research

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Status

**PROPOSED / USER REVIEW REQUIRED BEFORE DRAFTING**

This structure is designed for a concise founding Perspective, not a manifesto and not a reported investigation.

Target length: `800-1,200` words.

## Structural objective

The article should reveal Portfilo's mission through one editorial instinct:

> When an update says something happened, ask what actually changed underneath it.

The piece should explain why that instinct naturally connects Business, Technology, Startups, AI, and Building.

It should not spend the article defending Portfilo's existence.

## Recommended architecture

### 1. Start with ordinary updates

Open with a short sequence of familiar business/technology developments:

- a company says it is adopting AI;
- a startup raises capital;
- a data center is announced;
- a new rule takes effect.

Do not over-explain them or turn them into reported case studies.

The purpose is to establish a simple distinction:

An update can accurately tell you that something happened. The next useful question may be what changed in practice.

Possible transition concept:

> The update tells you something happened. The rest of the story can begin with what changed.

This is structural guidance, not locked prose.

### 2. Name the editorial instinct

Introduce the working question:

> **What actually changed?**

Explain that this can mean different things depending on the subject:

- what moved from announcement to operation;
- what changed for a customer, worker, company, market, or product;
- what a broad phrase means in concrete terms;
- what mechanism produces the claimed result;
- what tradeoff or constraint appears once the idea meets reality.

Do not present this as a universal formula or numbered framework in the public article.

### 3. Show why the subjects connect

This is the core mission section.

Move naturally across Portfilo's coverage rather than listing the five topics one by one.

Possible chain:

Technology changes how companies operate. That changes jobs, costs, products, and markets. Startups are shaped by capital, distribution, and the same infrastructure and regulation. AI enters through products and workflows rather than existing in a separate universe. Building something forces all of those questions to meet.

The reader should understand why Portfilo can move from a payment rule to an AI deployment to a startup decision without the publication feeling incoherent.

Avoid `at the intersection of...` language.

### 4. Explain what following the story means in practice

Use a few clean contrasts, but avoid slogan symmetry.

Examples of the editorial motion:

- announcement -> operating reality;
- label -> concrete meaning;
- investment -> what gets built;
- technology -> who uses it and how;
- policy -> actual implementation;
- growth claim -> measurable underlying change.

The article may use one or two verified Portfilo examples if they improve clarity, but it should not become a portfolio recap.

C009 is the safest published example if one is needed: instead of accepting `higher-value work` as a complete explanation, Portfilo examined what job families and responsibilities the phrase actually described.

Do not cite or publish unresolved conclusions from C001 or C021.

### 5. Add the limiting principle

Prevent the piece from sounding like Portfilo thinks every news update is shallow.

State plainly that an update has value on its own and not every development needs a long explanation.

The distinction is editorial judgment:

Sometimes the important fact is simply that something happened. Sometimes the announcement creates a more useful question about implementation, economics, consequence, or operating reality.

Portfilo should follow the second path when the second question materially changes understanding.

This paragraph is important because it keeps the article curious rather than self-righteous.

### 6. End on what Portfilo will keep following

The conclusion should be short.

Return to the question rather than summarizing every section.

Possible ending territory:

The most interesting part of a business or technology story is often the point where the announcement meets reality. Portfilo wants to keep looking there.

Do not end with:

- `That is why we built Portfilo.`;
- `Subscribe to Portfilo.`;
- a list of promises;
- a grand statement about changing journalism;
- a tagline.

Let the normal site UI handle subscription and navigation.

## Voice and pacing

Use:

- short concrete opening;
- mostly paragraph prose rather than headings in the final article;
- a few well-chosen examples;
- restrained first-person plural only where Portfilo itself is the subject;
- no autobiographical founder setup unless one sentence becomes genuinely necessary;
- no research-summary language;
- no `in today's rapidly changing...` framing;
- no three-part mission slogan.

The piece should feel closer to an editorial note with an argument than a corporate launch announcement.
