## Image necessity gate

Decision:

`GENERATED ILLUSTRATION`

Why does this story need an image?

C030 has a genuinely visualizable conceptual mechanism: a business or technology development can acquire a different practical meaning once it changes decisions, responsibilities, workflows, or constraints.

What useful job would the image perform that the headline/dek/body/data/source material does not already perform?

The image gives the abstract mechanism a memorable visual entry point by showing a changed condition that alters what an anonymous person has to do or decide.

Why is a generated illustration appropriate rather than a real photograph, document, chart, or no image?

C030 is not about one documented company, rollout, funding round, person, workplace, or event. A real photograph would arbitrarily imply that one specific case is the subject. The story contains no quantitative mechanism that requires a chart. A generated illustration can visualize the editorial mechanism while remaining clearly non-documentary.

## Visual problem

Central story idea:

What happened and what that development changes are related but distinct questions.

Central tension/change/mechanism:

A development enters an existing operating situation and changes available choices, movement, responsibilities, or constraints.

One-sentence visual problem:

Show a changed condition requiring a practical choice without depicting a specific real event, implying deception, or turning the idea into a universal formula.

What must the reader **not** infer from the image?

- that the image documents a real company, workplace, person, or event;
- that a specific company adopted AI or changed a workflow;
- that one route or decision is objectively correct;
- that the changed condition represents danger, failure, or crisis;
- that every business or technology update contains a hidden deeper truth;
- that Portfilo uses `What actually changed?` as a universal branded framework.

### Concept B

Visual subject/scene:

A realistic grayscale generic work/building environment seen from a slightly elevated or high angle. One spatial or structural change alters the available route or work position. An anonymous partial or distant figure reaches the changed condition and has to choose how to proceed.

Why it represents the story:

Directly visualizes the approved story's shift from a development existing in the abstract to changing what someone has to do or decide.

What it deliberately leaves out:

The cause of the change, the correct decision, company identity, screens, products, money, AI symbols, rules, headlines, and any final outcome.

Factual/misreading risk:

Could be mistaken for a real workplace, safety/emergency scene, generic maze/life-choice metaphor, or image with one implicitly correct route.

Crop behavior:

Strong in 16:9 and 1200x630; viable in 4:3 and 1:1 only if the figure and decision point are kept close to the center.

Accent justified? Why/why not?

No chromatic accent. A blue route could imply a preferred answer; red could imply danger or failure. Geometry, light, and shadow should carry the concept.

Decision:

`SELECT`

## Scene / subject specification

Primary subject:

A generic realistic work/building environment whose spatial arrangement has changed, creating a practical route or work-position decision.

Environment:

- ordinary and plausible;
- work/building oriented;
- generic rather than branded;
- may resemble a workshop, studio, utility workspace, or neutral operating environment without becoming identifiable as any real company or sector;
- strong structural lines, partitions, work surfaces, openings, or passages;
- restrained environmental detail rather than polished commercial set dressing.

Human presence:

`partial anonymous figure` or `distant anonymous figure`

The person should:

- be non-identifiable;
- be visually secondary to the environment;
- appear calm, observant, and engaged in an ordinary practical decision;
- not appear trapped, endangered, panicked, heroic, celebratory, or defeated.

Objects that may appear:

- generic work surfaces;
- neutral partitions or structural barriers;
- shelves or plain storage elements;
- generic tools or material traces only if they remain unreadable and non-sector-specific;
- ordinary architectural openings, paths, or floor geometry;
- restrained furniture only if it does not make the scene look like a named office.

Objects/details that must **not** appear:

- branded products;
- company or product logos;
- readable screens;
- recognizable software interfaces;
- dashboards or charts;
- readable documents or signs;
- headlines or newspapers;
- money or funding paperwork;
- AI symbols, robots, glowing brains, circuitry, floating UI, or cyberpunk cues;
- server racks used as generic technology shorthand;
- safety/emergency signs or evacuation cues;
- arrows showing a correct route;
- maze-like walls or puzzle motifs;
- maps or flowcharts;
- generated typography of any kind.

## Factual implication boundary

The image may imply:

- a generic operating/work environment;
- that something about the environment or conditions has changed;
- that the changed condition creates a practical choice or constraint;
- that an anonymous person must adjust what they do or decide.

The image may **not** imply:

- a real reported workplace or event;
- a specific AI deployment, funding round, regulatory change, startup, or product rollout;
- that one decision is the correct answer;
- danger, crisis, failure, success, or moral judgment;
- that the article documents the depicted scene;
- that the image depicts Louda Tuppal or another real person.

Specific risks:

- fake real event: controlled by generic editorialized environment;
- fake workplace/location: controlled by removing branding, distinctive architecture, and sector-specific detail;
- fake screenshot/UI: prohibited;
- fake document: prohibited;
- fake logo/brand: prohibited;
- fake public figure/quoted subject: prohibited;
- unsupported scale/success/failure/danger: avoid dramatic obstruction, damage, emergency lighting, or triumphant route resolution;
- cultural/national cliché: no decorative Filipino symbols or token signaling;
- other: avoid generic existential-maze or `life choices` symbolism.

## Visual treatment

Base:

`MONOCHROME / GRAYSCALE`

Style:

Realistic editorial illustration. The image may have photographic realism in materials, light, perspective, and anatomy, but must remain an explicitly synthetic editorial illustration rather than a purported documentary photograph.

Do not use cartoon, anime, comic-book, cel-shaded, caricature, fantasy, surrealist, or glossy advertising treatment.

Lighting:

Directional or low-key lighting with clear structural shadows. The figure and decision point must remain readable rather than disappearing into black areas.

Texture/grain:

Visible restrained grain/texture. Avoid plastic smoothness and heavy cinematic noise.

Contrast:

High but readable contrast with large neutral/dark structural areas and preserved detail around the central decision point.

Geometry/composition:

Strong architectural lines and negative space. The figure and changed junction must remain near the crop-safe center. The spatial change should be legible without arrows or diagrammatic annotation.

Mood in concrete visual terms:

Quiet, observational, practical, and slightly tense through geometry rather than drama. The environment should feel used and plausible, not luxurious, dystopian, abandoned, futuristic, or polished like an ad campaign.
