## Headline job from approved style brief

The headline must:

- name the editorial tension or question;
- fit a bylined Perspective rather than a corporate launch note;
- avoid implying that every update hides a deeper truth;
- avoid becoming a permanent Portfilo slogan or named framework;
- remain accurate to a body that applies the argument both to announcements and to broad industry language.

### Poynter — `10 questions for your headline-writing checklist`

https://www.poynter.org/educators-students/2017/10-questions-for-your-headline-writing-checklist/

Relevant principles:

- each word must be accurate;
- do not oversell or undersell;
- make the right point;
- make the headline easy to understand;
- match the story's tone.

### Poynter — `10 questions to help you write better headlines`

https://www.poynter.org/reporting-editing/2011/10-questions-to-help-you-write-better-headlines/

Relevant principles:

- headlines should normally work out of context;
- question headlines are legitimate when the article answers the question or frames it in a compelling way;
- an interrogative should not promise an answer the story does not deliver.

Limitation:

These are professional editing heuristics, not evidence that one headline form is universally superior.

## Re-audit of current headline

> **What actually changed?**

### Strengths

- It names the exact editorial question that motivated C030 without stating a universal rule.
- The interrogative form preserves uncertainty and conditionality better than a slogan-like declarative claim.
- The approved body genuinely frames when this question becomes useful: after an AI-adoption announcement, a funding round, a broad industry phrase, or another development where implementation/economics/constraints change meaning.
- It applies to both announcements and language/categories, unlike alternatives centered only on `announcement`.
- It is short and does not sound like an About-page heading or launch manifesto.
- The body does not repeat the phrase, so it does not become a branded framework.
- It already has explicit editorial history with Louda, but the current recommendation does not rely on that history alone.

### Weakness

The headline is context-light when seen entirely alone. A reader encountering only `What actually changed?` cannot know that the piece concerns Portfilo's editorial treatment of business and technology developments.

This is a real tradeoff under Poynter's `works out of context` heuristic.

### Why the weakness is not decisive here

C030 is a founding/editorial Perspective, not a breaking update or search-led explainer. The question itself is the essay's editorial tension, and the surrounding product surfaces supply content type/topic plus description. The later dek/description must therefore do real contextual work.

This rationale would not justify using equally vague headlines on ordinary reported stories.

## Alternatives tested

### `When the update is only the beginning`

Source:

Earlier C030 pre-standard alternative, re-audited now rather than treated as precedent.

Strengths:

- carries context better when seen alone;
- preserves conditionality through `when`;
- communicates that the piece concerns updates.

Weaknesses:

- `only the beginning` is generic editorial language;
- narrows the essay toward chronological `what happens next` framing;
- fits the announcement examples better than the C009 language/category example;
- risks implying that Portfilo's value is simply going further/longer, which the approved body explicitly rejects.

Decision: **REJECT AS PRIMARY.**

### `The story after the announcement`

Source:

Earlier C030 pre-standard alternative, re-audited now.

Strengths:

- concise;
- clearer out of context than the question title.

Weaknesses:

- makes the piece more media-about-media;
- excludes the broad-label/C009 case;
- can imply that the later story is inherently more valuable than the announcement itself.

Decision: **REJECT.**

### `What changes after the announcement?`

New test wording generated only for audit comparison; not recommended merely because it is new.

Strengths:

- clearer scope than the current title;
- interrogative preserves uncertainty.

Weaknesses:

- again over-centers announcements;
- implies the essay is mainly about consequences after a discrete event;
- does not fit the language/category example as cleanly as the current question.

Decision: **REJECT.**

> **What actually changed?**
