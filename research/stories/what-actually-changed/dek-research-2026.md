## Purpose

Research the dek beneath the approved C030 headline:

> **What actually changed?**

The headline is intentionally context-light. The dek therefore has to supply enough subject and interpretive context for the pair to work on the story page and when encountered outside the full body.

The old pre-standard dek is not protected:

> `An update tells you something happened. Portfilo is interested in what changes when the announcement meets operating reality.`

Its institutional phrasing and `operating reality` abstraction were specifically reopened under the current writing system.

Description/card/metadata remains a separate later gate.

## Human-edited dek behavior beneath question headlines

The examples were chosen because the headline itself asks a question and therefore leaves a meaningful contextual job for the dek.

### 1. WIRED — `Why Does Wikipedia Think I’m Evan Spiegel?`

https://www.wired.com/story/why-does-wikipedia-think-im-evan-spiegel/

Observed dek:

> `For the past week, a photo of me has been plastered on the CEO of Snap’s Wikipedia page. No one, not even Spiegel, seems to care.`

Observed job:

The headline contains the curiosity; the dek supplies the concrete situation that makes the question intelligible.

Transferable lesson:

A short question headline does not need the dek to restate the question. The dek can ground it in the specific kind of problem the article examines.

Do not imitate:

- first-person anecdotal tone;
- comic cadence;
- WIRED voice.

### 2. WIRED — `What Comes After OnlyFans?`

https://www.wired.com/story/onlyfans-artificial-intelligence-sex-work-relationships/

Observed dek:

> `Fandom revolutionized sex work and remapped the parameters of connection across social media. The question now is, how real are you willing to get?`

Observed job:

The dek supplies the historical/cultural change underneath a broad question and tells the reader what dimension of `after` the article cares about.

Transferable lesson:

A broad interrogative headline can be made specific by naming the mechanism or dimension being examined rather than by summarizing every section.

Do not imitate:

- rhetorical flourish;
- second-person challenge;
- elevated cultural phrasing.

### 3. The Atlantic Ideas / David Brooks — `Why Do So Many People Think That Trump Is Good?`

Original Atlantic URL recorded by external index:

https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/07/trump-administration-supporters-good/683441/

Human-edited title/dek also visible in Süddeutsche Zeitung's licensed presentation:

https://www.sueddeutsche.de/projekte/artikel/politik/why-do-so-many-people-think-that-trump-is-good-e213853/

Observed dek:

> `The work of the moral philosopher Alasdair MacIntyre helps illuminate some central questions of our time.`

Observed job:

The headline asks the substantive question; the dek supplies the interpretive lens used to answer it.

Transferable lesson:

A question headline can pair with a dek that identifies *how* the essay will think about the question rather than giving away the entire conclusion.

Do not imitate:

- named-philosopher device;
- Atlantic Ideas voice;
- broad `questions of our time` framing.

### 4. The Atlantic Ideas / Arthur C. Brooks — `Why Are Young People Everywhere So Unhappy?`

https://www.theatlantic.com/ideas/archive/2025/05/young-people-global-unhappiness/682632/

Observed deck in The Atlantic's own distribution and licensed presentation:

> `Here’s the answer to that—and what we can do about it.`

Observed job:

The deck promises both explanation and action.

Usefulness as a counterexample for C030:

This is professionally edited but **not** a pattern C030 should transfer. C030 is not a Guide, has no reader action to prescribe, and should not use generic promise language merely to make a short headline feel complete.

## Cross-example findings

### Finding 1 — the dek should solve the headline's missing context

The question headline already supplies curiosity/tension.

The dek's strongest job is therefore one of:

- concrete situation;
- mechanism;
- interpretive lens;
- stakes/scope.

It does not need to repeat the question in declarative form.

### Finding 2 — one grounding layer is enough

The strongest examples do not attempt to encode the entire article architecture in the dek.

For C030, the dek should not simultaneously explain:

- AI adoption;
- startup funding;
- Building;
- broad industry labels;
- the `sometimes an update is enough` limitation;
- Portfilo's complete coverage model.

Those belong in the body.

### Finding 3 — C030 needs subject context more than institutional voice

`What actually changed?` alone does not tell a reader that this is about business/technology developments and their practical consequences.

The dek should supply that context directly.

It does **not** need:

- `Portfilo believes`;
- `Portfilo is interested in`;
- `our approach`;
- `our mission`;
- `operating reality`;
- a claim that Portfilo always goes deeper.

### Finding 4 — ordinary consequence language fits better than editorial jargon

The approved body repeatedly reduces developments to what they change in choices, work, responsibilities, costs and constraints.

For the dek, ordinary language about what someone has to **do or decide** is more readable than an abstract list such as `implementation, economics and constraints`.

## Candidate development

### Candidate A — recommended

> **A business or technology development can mean something different once it changes what someone has to do or decide.**

Why it fits:

- gives the context-light headline a clear business/technology domain;
- one sentence;
- expresses conditionality through `can` rather than claiming every development needs a second layer;
- translates the approved body's `choices`, `responsibility`, workflows and constraints into ordinary language;
- applies to AI adoption, funding, products and rules without listing them;
- avoids institutional self-description;
- avoids `operating reality`, `deeper`, `second-order effects`, or other strategy language;
- no rhetorical question, transition scaffold or framework term;
- does not restate the headline word for word.

Risk:

The sentence emphasizes practical decisions more than the C009 language/category example. That is acceptable because a dek should add one useful layer rather than represent every example in the body.

### Candidate B — hold

> **A business or technology development is worth following further when implementation, economics or constraints change what it means.**

Strengths:

- very close to the approved argument;
- preserves the article's limiting principle;
- context-rich.

Weaknesses:

- `implementation, economics or constraints` reads more like the approved style brief than publication copy;
- `worth following further` makes the sentence more explicitly about newsroom process;
- more abstract than Candidate A.

Decision: `HOLD / NOT RECOMMENDED.`

### Candidate C — reject

> **Sometimes an update is enough. Sometimes the more useful story starts after it.**

Reason:

- symmetrical two-sentence construction;
- turns `more useful story` into a generic quality claim;
- recreates the old draft's polished contrast rhythm;
- over-centers announcements;
- does not solve the headline's business/technology context problem.

Decision: `REJECT.`

### Candidate D — reject old dek

> **An update tells you something happened. Portfilo is interested in what changes when the announcement meets operating reality.**

Reason:

- proposition A -> proposition B scaffold;
- unnecessary institutional self-reference;
- `is interested in` says very little;
- `operating reality` is abstract strategy language;
- over-centers announcement framing and underrepresents the approved body's language/category example.

Decision: `REJECT / SUPERSEDED.`

## Anti-imitation boundary

The recommended wording is not adapted from any exemplar sentence.

The exemplars authorize only the editorial function: under a short question headline, the dek can provide the missing situation, mechanism or interpretive scope.

Do not copy WIRED's anecdotal/cultural cadence or The Atlantic's institutional Ideas voice.
