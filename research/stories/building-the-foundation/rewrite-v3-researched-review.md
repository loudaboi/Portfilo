## Findings requiring revision

### F01 — rhetorical question appears outside the approved application allowance

V3:

> The additional question is narrower: after the outcome is known, did the work itself change a later decision, capability, or piece of evidence in a way that can actually be used?

Why revise:

The approved mechanics default to zero rhetorical questions except possibly one genuine application question near the end. This question appears during thesis development and is not necessary.

Action:

Convert it to a declarative statement.

### F02 — unsupported prevalence-like wording

V3:

> early work is often judged in all-or-nothing terms

Why revise:

The story has no evidence establishing how frequently people judge early work this way. The argument does not need a prevalence claim.

Action:

Describe the evaluative frame directly without `often` or a population-level implication.

### F03 — neat three-item application list recreates AI-shaped balance

V3 near ending:

> a finished piece of work that can be inspected, evidence that rules out or sharpens a problem, or a relationship created through actual delivery

Why revise:

All three examples are plausible, but the three-part list functions more as completeness/rhythm than as necessary argument. The style brief specifically warns against artificial three-part balance and exhaustive benefit lists.

Action:

Use one or two examples tied back to the developed body rather than reopening a catalogue.

### F04 — small amount of binary contrast language remains

Examples include:

- `The project has not become successful; it has changed...`
- `Those possibilities do not rescue... but...`

Why revise:

Neither is independently severe, but the assignment has a known tendency toward `not X, but Y` scaffolding. Reduce where a simpler declarative construction works.
