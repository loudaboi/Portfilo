## S001 - NBER: Does Generative AI Narrow Education-Based Productivity Gaps?

URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w34851

Type / tier: Tier A research source; randomized experiment / NBER working paper

Date: February 2026; revised May 2026

Why it matters:

A randomized online experiment with 1,174 adults aged 25-45 tested a workplace-style business problem-solving task with and without a generative-AI assistant. AI improved performance for all participants, with substantially larger gains for lower-education participants. The education-linked performance gap fell from 0.548 SD without AI to 0.139 SD with AI, closing about three-quarters of the initial gap in the assisted task.

Known limitations:

- One experimental task, not entrepreneurship or general life outcomes.
- Education is not interchangeable with technical experience, money, network, or opportunity.
- A sizable performance gap re-emerged when AI was removed.
- Higher-education participants used the assistant somewhat more effectively on several dimensions.

## S002 - NBER: Generative AI at Work

URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161

Type / tier: Tier A research source; workplace study / NBER working paper

Date: April 2023; revised November 2023

Why it matters:

Studied the staggered introduction of a generative-AI conversational assistant across 5,179 customer-support agents. Access increased productivity by 14% on average, with a reported 34% improvement for novice and lower-skilled workers and little impact on experienced, highly skilled workers.

Known limitations:

- One company and occupation.
- The system and model generation predate current 2026 tools.
- Productivity in customer support does not prove broad capability equalization.
- Evidence on durable learning was suggestive rather than definitive.

## S003 - METR: Measuring the Impact of Early-2025 AI on Experienced Open-Source Developer Productivity

URL: https://metr.org/blog/2025-07-10-early-2025-ai-experienced-os-dev-study/

Paper: https://metr.org/Early_2025_AI_Experienced_OS_Devs_Study-paper.pdf

Type / tier: Tier A/B research source; randomized controlled trial by research nonprofit

Date: July 10, 2025

Why it matters:

Sixteen experienced open-source developers completed 246 tasks in mature repositories they knew well. Tasks were randomized to allow or disallow AI tools. In this setting, allowing early-2025 AI tools increased completion time by 19%, even though developers expected to be faster.

Known limitations:

- Small developer sample.
- Mature codebases and highly experienced developers are a specific setting.
- Tools were from February-June 2025 and capabilities have changed.
- METR later said its follow-up experiment had selection problems that made the newer estimate unreliable.

## S004 - Meta-analysis: Generative AI, programming productivity and learning

URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04779

Type / tier: Tier B research source; 2026 preprint meta-analysis

Date: May 6, 2026

Why it matters:

Meta-analysis of 23 studies / 27 effect sizes found a moderate positive effect of GenAI coding assistants on productivity (Hedges' g = 0.33) with substantial heterogeneity, but no statistically significant effect on measured programming learning outcomes (g = 0.14, 95% CI crossing zero).

Known limitations:

- Preprint, not treated as settled peer-reviewed consensus.
- Included studies vary materially in design and context.
- Programming is only one form of building.
- Learning measures were narrower than long-term professional capability.

## S005 - Competitive framing scan: `taste is the bottleneck`

Representative examples located in 2026 include:

- The Compounding Founder, `The Taste Bottleneck`
- Buttondown essay, `Taste Is the New Bottleneck`
- Matt Senter, `When Everyone Can Build, Taste Is the Bottleneck`
- Vikas Malpani, `What to Build When Building Is Free`

Type / tier: Tier D / competitive and language research only

Why it matters:

The `AI made production cheap, therefore taste/judgment is the new bottleneck` thesis is already common in current builder commentary. Portfilo should not publish a weaker rewrite of that idea.

Known limitations:

These are not evidence for universal productivity or economic claims. Their use is limited to originality/coverage checking and identifying crowded language.
