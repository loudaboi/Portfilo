## Purpose

Research the specific editorial job of the dek under the approved C027 headline:

> **Build things that make the next thing easier**

The headline is approved by Louda Tuppal. The approved C027 body remains locked.

This pass does not reopen the headline or body. It asks only:

> What should the dek add that the headline does not?

The previous proposed dek was rejected because its `The result still comes first. Beyond that...` construction compressed the editorial logic into a visibly synthetic proposition-transition-proposition structure.

## Professional framing principle

A dek does not need to restate every caveat or summarize the whole argument. Its job is to add a useful second layer beneath the headline: scope, consequence, mechanism, tension, or a concrete angle that makes the promise clearer.

The C027 dek should therefore avoid acting like a miniature abstract of the style brief.

## Cross-example findings

### Finding 1 — the dek should do one extra job

The strongest pairings do not force the dek to restate the article's entire logic.

For C027, the headline already carries the positive proposition:

> Build things that make the next thing easier.

The dek therefore does not need to repeat:

- direct outcomes matter;
- later usefulness is secondary;
- later usefulness must be concrete;
- not every failure is valuable;
- structural constraints still exist.

Those conditions belong in the article.

### Finding 2 — the previous dek failed because it exposed the editorial scaffolding

Rejected wording:

> `The result still comes first. Beyond that, the work should only count if it changes how the next attempt begins in a concrete way.`

Problems:

- proposition A -> transition -> proposition B;
- reads like a compressed internal memo;
- `Beyond that` is generic essay glue;
- `should only count` sounds like a rubric;
- `changes how the next attempt begins in a concrete way` is accurate but abstract;
- it explains the strategy behind the article rather than adding a fresh reader-facing tension.

Decision:

**REJECT / DO NOT REUSE.**

### Finding 3 — C027's strongest missing layer is the anti-rationalization tension

The headline is optimistic and forward-looking. On its own, it can be read as motivational advice.

The approved body becomes credible when it attacks the easy excuse:

> after a project ends, `I learned a lot` can be claimed almost regardless of what actually changed.

That tension is specific to this article and is more useful beneath the headline than an abstract restatement of the two-part evaluation rule.

### Finding 4 — one sentence is enough

The sample provides no reason to force a two-sentence dek.

For C027, one sentence reduces the temptation to build an artificial thesis/qualification pair.

## Dek pattern selected for C027

**Headline proposition + counterpressure.**

Headline:

> **Build things that make the next thing easier**

Dek job:

Introduce the standard that prevents the headline from becoming generic `every project teaches you something` advice.

The dek should sound like a sentence from the same intellectual problem as the article, not like the abstract of the style brief.

## Candidate development

### Candidate A — recommended

> **An early project should leave you with more than a story about what you learned.**

Why it fits:

- one clear sentence;
- adds tension rather than restating the headline;
- directly anticipates the article's strongest counterargument;
- uses ordinary language;
- no framework term;
- no transition scaffolding;
- no list;
- no rhetorical question;
- does not center failure as the entire subject;
- does not try to encode every caveat.

Risk:

It is deliberately incomplete. It does not tell the reader exactly what qualifies as `more`; the body supplies that answer through the software and client-work examples.

Assessment:

That incompleteness is acceptable and preferable to turning the dek into a mini-summary.

### Candidate B — hold

> **A project that falls short can still leave better evidence for the next decision.**

Strengths:

- concrete mechanism;
- closely matches the software example;
- less abstract than the rejected dek.

Weaknesses:

- still shifts the framing toward projects that `fall short`;
- `better evidence` is somewhat formal;
- underrepresents the finished-work / usable-output side of the body.

Decision:

`HOLD`, not recommended.

### Candidate C — reject

> **Learning from a project only matters when it changes what you do next.**

Strength:

- concise and clear.

Weaknesses:

- makes `learning` the entire standard even though the body also values inspectable finished work;
- `only matters when` reads like another rule formulation;
- sounds closer to an aphorism than to an editorial dek.

Decision:

`REJECT`.
