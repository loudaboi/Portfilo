# C027 Draft v3 Adversarial Review

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

_Status: COMPLETE / MATERIAL FINDINGS RESOLVED IN DRAFT V4_

## Review question

Assume Draft v3 is wrong, vague, or too flattering to its own thesis. Identify where it could drift into motivational writing, rationalize failure, overgeneralize early-builder experience, or become more complicated than the approved message.

Approved core idea:

> **Build things that make the next thing easier.**

Internal theme statement:

> Some work matters because it changes the quality of the next starting point.

## Material findings

### 1. Draft v3 over-indexes on failed projects

The approved idea is prospective: choose and judge work partly by what it leaves usable for the next attempt.

Draft v3 repeatedly frames the argument through projects that fail, stop, or never become businesses. That risks turning the article into an essay about why failure is secretly good.

Resolution for Draft v4:

- keep the opening contrast;
- reduce repeated failure language;
- make the central subject what carries forward, not retrospective consolation;
- state plainly that the visible outcome remains the primary outcome.

### 2. `Carryover` is useful but can become invented jargon

Draft v3 uses `carryover` to distinguish transferable value from vague `learning`. The distinction is useful, but repeated use risks sounding like a framework term Portfilo invented for branding.

Resolution:

- use the word once to name the distinction;
- return immediately to plain language: what remains usable, what becomes easier next, what changed in the starting position.

### 3. Some abstractions weaken the copy

Phrases such as `accumulated leverage`, `durable advantage`, `residual value`, and `the quality of the next starting point` are analytically precise but not all belong in public prose.

They make the Perspective sound more like a strategy memo and conflict with the copy rule to prefer concrete nouns and verbs.

Resolution:

- remove `accumulated leverage`, `durable advantage`, and `residual value` from the body;
- keep `starting point` because it is the core metaphor;
- use concrete examples instead of asset-language where possible.

### 4. The draft can accidentally imply structural constraints are mainly a matter of project choice

The article is aimed partly at people who may lack money, connections, experience, or distribution. It must not imply that a smart project choice erases unequal starting conditions.

Resolution:

- preserve one narrow caveat: choosing useful work does not remove constraints such as money, access, or opportunity;
- do not turn that caveat into a separate socioeconomic essay;
- frame the thesis only around choices a person actually has.

### 5. The hypothetical examples are valid but can become a hidden checklist

Product, service, newsletter, research, money, distribution, relationships, skill, proof, customers, and reputation all appear in Draft v3.

The approved structure allows examples, but too many nouns reconstruct the same multi-theme problem that broke Draft v1.

Resolution:

- use fewer examples;
- make every example demonstrate the same transformation: the next attempt begins with something specific that was absent before;
- do not enumerate possible forms of carryover in one long sentence.

### 6. The counterargument is necessary but slightly too long

Draft v3 correctly challenges `I learned a lot` rationalization, but the section repeats the same point through several paragraphs.

Resolution:

- keep the hard counterargument;
- shorten it;
- give it one concrete standard: if nothing specific became usable afterward, the story about `learning` may be rationalization.

### 7. Several statements read like universal factual claims

Examples include broad statements about how people with experience, savings, audiences, or professional relationships start projects, and about how early work `often` begins in a vacuum.

For a Perspective, these can be framed as reasonable observations, but they should not masquerade as measured population-level facts.

Resolution:

- replace universal formulations with narrower language such as `when you have little previous work to draw on`;
- avoid unsupported prevalence language;
- no external statistic is required for the central argument.

## Claim audit

Draft v3 contains no consequential numbers, quotations, legal claims, company claims, rankings, historical claims, or current-event assertions.

The examples are hypothetical and should remain clearly hypothetical.

The central argument is opinion / interpretation, appropriate for `Perspective`.

Result:

**NO EXTERNAL FACTUAL CLAIM REQUIRES A READER-FACING CITATION IN DRAFT V4, provided the revision does not introduce new empirical claims.**

The research packet still documents the broader audience and craft research that led to topic selection. That research should not be inserted into the public story merely to make the Perspective look researched.
