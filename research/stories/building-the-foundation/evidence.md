# C027 Evidence Ledger

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## E01

Candidate claim:

Generative AI can narrow some task-performance gaps between people with different levels of prior human capital.

Evidence:

In a 2026 randomized experiment with 1,174 adults completing a workplace-style business problem-solving task, AI improved performance for all participants and produced substantially larger gains for lower-education participants. The high-versus-low education performance gap fell from 0.548 SD without AI to 0.139 SD with AI.

Source:

S001 - NBER Working Paper 34851

Tier:

A

Confidence:

HIGH for the specific experiment; MEDIUM for broader generalization

Caveat:

Education is not equivalent to technical background, capital, social network, or entrepreneurship. The result supports `AI can compress some gaps`, not `AI eliminates starting-position differences`.

Status:

VERIFIED / QUALIFY

## E02

Candidate claim:

Less-experienced workers can receive larger productivity gains from generative AI in some workplace settings.

Evidence:

The 2023 NBER customer-support study found a 14% average productivity increase from AI assistance and a 34% improvement among novice and lower-skilled workers, with little impact on experienced/high-skilled workers.

Source:

S002 - NBER Working Paper 31161

Tier:

A

Confidence:

HIGH for the studied workplace

Caveat:

One occupation and company. Do not generalize the 34% figure to builders, founders, programmers, or all knowledge work.

Status:

VERIFIED / QUALIFY

## E03

Candidate claim:

AI does not universally make experienced people faster, and its productivity effect depends on context.

Evidence:

METR's 2025 randomized study found 16 experienced open-source developers took 19% longer when AI tools were allowed while working on mature repositories they knew well.

Source:

S003 - METR RCT

Tier:

A/B

Confidence:

HIGH for the studied setting; LOW for universal developer productivity

Caveat:

Small sample, early-2025 tools, specific mature-codebase context.

Status:

VERIFIED / QUALIFY

## E04

Candidate claim:

Faster AI-assisted execution is not the same thing as durable learning.

Evidence:

A 2026 meta-analysis of 23 programming studies found a moderate positive productivity effect but no statistically significant effect on measured learning outcomes. S001 also found that a sizable education-linked performance gap re-emerged on a later non-AI-assisted task.

Source:

S004 and S001

Tier:

B + A

Confidence:

MEDIUM-HIGH

Caveat:

Programming-learning measures are narrower than long-term capability, and S004 is a preprint. The story should say the evidence does not support assuming that faster AI-assisted output automatically produces equivalent skill development.

Status:

VERIFIED / QUALIFY

## E05

Candidate claim:

`Building is free now` is too strong to use as a factual premise.

Evidence:

The research shows heterogeneous effects. AI can improve task performance and lower some execution barriers, but it can also slow experienced developers in specific contexts, and learning effects are not consistently positive.

Source:

S001-S004

Tier:

Synthesis

Confidence:

HIGH

Caveat:

This does not deny that generation and prototyping costs have fallen in many workflows. It rejects a universal or zero-cost formulation.

Status:

VERIFIED

## E07

Candidate argument:

A useful way to think about an early weak starting position is as a sequence of changing bottlenecks rather than one permanent deficiency.

Evidence:

This is the author's Perspective and analytical model, informed by the evidence that AI assistance can change which constraints bind in a task while underlying skill differences can remain.

Source:

Authorial synthesis; S001-S004 for the AI component

Tier:

INTERPRETATION

Confidence:

N/A as a normative/analytical frame

Caveat:

Do not present `knowledge -> skill -> network -> reputation` as a scientifically established universal sequence. Different people face different constraints, including structural constraints that effort alone may not remove.

Status:

QUALIFY AS PERSPECTIVE
