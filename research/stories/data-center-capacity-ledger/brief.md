## Assignment

ID: `C021`

Working question:

> **How much Philippine data-center capacity is operating, under construction, committed, and merely announced?**

Primary topic: `Technology`

Likely content type: `Analysis / Reported`

Primary reader need: `Update`

Priority: `HIGH`

Planning decision: `PASS`

## Reader payoff

Give readers a defensible picture of how much Philippine data-center capacity exists now versus how much belongs to projects that are still being built, committed, planned, or only announced.

The story should make it possible to distinguish a live megawatt from a future megawatt.

## Working hypothesis

Public discussion of Philippine data-center growth frequently places unlike numbers beside one another:

- current operating IT load;
- utility power secured or energized;
- full-campus design capacity;
- capacity under construction;
- future phases;
- investment commitments and government targets.

The resulting headline totals can imply more immediately usable computing capacity than the underlying project status supports.

This is a hypothesis to test, not a conclusion.

## Measurement rules

### Preferred unit

Use **IT load MW** whenever available.

Do not silently equate:

- IT load;
- utility power;
- facility electrical capacity;
- powered shell capacity;
- rack count;
- cabinet count.

If a source reports only utility power or an unspecified MW figure, preserve the number but exclude it from strict IT-load subtotals until the basis is verified.

### Status definitions

`OPERATING`
: A facility or phase is in service and the cited capacity is explicitly live, available, or operational.

`UNDER CONSTRUCTION`
: Physical development is currently underway and the capacity is not yet operating.

`COMMITTED`
: A specific project has an identified site and capacity with material execution evidence such as an operator schedule, approval, financing, power commitment, or equivalent, but Portfilo cannot yet verify operating service or active construction for the capacity counted.

`ANNOUNCED`
: A future target, full-buildout ambition, memorandum, investment intention, or project concept without enough current execution evidence to place it in a stronger category.

When evidence conflicts, use `STATUS TO RECONCILE` and exclude the row from status subtotals until resolved.

### Phase rule

Count only the capacity supported for the current phase.

Example: if a 36 MW campus has a 6 MW first phase live, record 6 MW as operating and the remaining 30 MW separately according to the evidence for later phases.

## Original contribution

The contribution is not another market-growth forecast.

Portfilo should build a project-level status ledger that:

1. identifies the operator, facility/campus and location;
2. records the MW figure exactly as the source defines it;
3. records whether that MW is IT load, utility power or another metric;
4. assigns a status using explicit rules;
5. records the evidence date and source;
6. keeps disputed or incomparable numbers out of clean subtotals;
7. explains why widely quoted national totals differ.

## Initial source-recon finding

The story has already cleared the basic contribution test because current public figures are difficult to reconcile without a project ledger.

Examples located in the first pass include:

- DICT statements putting current Philippine capacity around or below 200 MW while setting much larger future targets;
- reporting that STT GDC Philippines alone operates seven sites with nearly 150 MW of IT load, while the operator's own location pages distinguish live, launching-soon and under-development facilities;
- operating facilities whose published MW may refer to full design capacity rather than the currently live phase;
- projects describing utility power or energized powered-shell capacity rather than IT load.

These differences may reflect scope, timing and metric definitions rather than factual error. The ledger must resolve them before Portfilo publishes a national total.

## Disproof / reframe conditions

Reframe or kill the original total-ledger thesis if:

- project-level capacity cannot be sourced consistently enough to produce defensible subtotals;
- too many important facilities disclose only rack counts or unspecified power;
- operator status labels remain irreconcilable after source follow-up;
- the strongest finding becomes the measurement problem itself rather than a reliable national capacity estimate.

If that happens, reframe to a narrower story explaining **why Philippine data-center capacity numbers conflict and what each number actually measures**.

## Evidence threshold before drafting

Do not draft until:

- the major identifiable operators are covered;
- every included MW has a metric definition or explicit qualification;
- current versus future phases are separated;
- the most consequential contradictions have been reconciled or transparently left unresolved;
- at least one national/government baseline is compared against the bottom-up ledger;
- a contradiction search tests for missing operators, double counting and stale project status.
