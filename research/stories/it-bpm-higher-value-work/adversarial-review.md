# C009 Adversarial Review - Higher-Value Work in Philippine IT-BPM

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Review instruction

Assume the draft is wrong or overstated. Challenge sourcing, framing, representativeness, terminology, causality, currentness, headline strength and the boundary between advertised work and actual employment outcomes.

Draft reviewed:

`research/stories/it-bpm-higher-value-work/draft.md`

## Overall result

**Draft survives, with revisions required.**

The central finding remains supported, but several sentences need tighter attribution or less interpretive language before the claim audit.

## A01 - Opening implies labor-market conditions more strongly than the packet establishes

Draft wording:

> The phrase has become more important as the sector adjusts to AI, slower headcount growth and pressure to compete on more than labor scale.

Problem:

The packet verifies IBPAP's stated strategic shift from capacity/headcount toward value per worker. It does not independently establish a causal relationship between AI and slower headcount growth across the sector.

Resolution:

Attribute the strategic framing directly to IBPAP and avoid causal shorthand. Suggested replacement:

> In July, IBPAP said future growth should depend less on adding headcount and more on the value created by each worker, as the sector adapts to AI-enabled services.

Status: **REVISE**

## A02 - GCCs and outsourced BPOs risk being blurred together

The draft introduces global capability centers alongside contact centers but does not immediately explain that GCCs are in-house service hubs rather than outsourced BPO providers.

This distinction matters because the operating and employment models differ even though both sit inside the broader IT-BPM discussion.

Resolution:

Add a short clarification when GCCs first appear.

Status: **REVISE**

## A03 - `Harder to standardize` is plausible but stronger than the direct evidence

Draft wording:

> ...the work depends on knowledge and judgment that are harder to standardize into a generic service script.

Problem:

The sample supports deeper domain knowledge and judgment relative to frontline baseline postings. It does not directly test automability or standardizability.

Resolution:

Replace with a direct comparative claim about specialized knowledge, judgment and consequences.

Status: **REVISE**

## A04 - `Designing and controlling the operating system` is metaphorical and potentially inflated

Draft wording about workforce management:

> ...designing and controlling the operating system behind that service...

Problem:

The Concentrix posting supports forecasting, staffing models, scheduling, tooling and planning. `Operating system` is rhetorical rather than factual.

Resolution:

Use literal wording: planning and coordinating the operation behind frontline service.

Status: **REVISE**

## A05 - Frontline-work comparison must not become a value judgment

The draft generally handles this well, but any contrast between `specialized` and `routine` can be misread as ranking the worth of workers.

Resolution:

Retain the explicit paragraph rejecting `low-value work` as a normative label. Keep descriptions tied to advertised process structure: high-volume, routine, standardized, procedure-constrained.

Status: **KEEP WITH CARE**

## A06 - Current employer postings establish advertisements, not lived work

The draft sometimes says a role `involves` certain tasks. Employer postings support what the employer advertises, not direct observation of day-to-day work.

Resolution:

Where the distinction is material, use formulations such as `the posting describes`, `the role is advertised as`, or `the posting covers`. General synthesis can remain if the method note clearly states the limitation.

Status: **MINOR REVISE**

## A07 - The sample could be mistaken for a market study

The draft states the sample is structured and illustrative, but this is the most important representativeness risk in the article.

Resolution:

Keep the limitation near the first mention of the sample and repeat it once in the limitations section. Methodology should state:

- 15 purposefully selected roles;
- employer-owned career pages;
- current/posting dates concentrated May-September 2026;
- selected to cover contrasting job families;
- not random or weighted;
- not usable for prevalence, growth or wage estimates.

Status: **REQUIRED IN FINAL METHODOLOGY**

## A08 - `Higher-value` cannot be equated with higher wages or worker welfare

The draft correctly says this later, but the headline/dek should not imply `better jobs`.

Resolution:

Avoid `better`, `better-paying`, `upgraded jobs`, or a salary promise in the headline/dek. Keep compensation outside the central claim.

Status: **PASS / GUARDRAIL**

## A09 - AI risks dominating a story whose evidence says AI is not the definition

The draft mentions AI in the opening and the data section. That is appropriate as sector context, but the story's finding is that many specialized roles are not defined by AI.

Resolution:

Do not add AI examples merely for topicality. Keep the AI discussion short and subordinate to role characteristics.

Status: **PASS WITH LIMIT**

## A10 - `The industry's language tends to skip` is unnecessarily accusatory

Draft wording:

> Those are the next questions the industry's language tends to skip.

Problem:

The packet establishes that current evidence does not answer wage, prevalence or transition questions. It does not establish that industry actors systematically avoid them.

Resolution:

Replace with:

> Those remain separate empirical questions.

Status: **REVISE**

## A11 - The final paragraph risks turning the five dimensions into an official definition

The dimensions are Portfilo's analytical synthesis, not an IBPAP or government taxonomy.

Resolution:

Retain explicit language that they are an analytical framework derived from recurring characteristics in the frameworks and sampled postings.

Status: **KEEP QUALIFICATION**

## A12 - Headline audit

Working headline:

> The jobs behind Philippine IT-BPM's `higher-value work` push

Assessment:

- concrete subject: PASS;
- preserves scope: PASS;
- no wage/growth claim: PASS;
- `push` is supportable from current IBPAP strategy but slightly campaign-like;
- could be clearer that the story explains the phrase rather than measures a transition.

Preferred headline candidate:

> **What `higher-value work` means in Philippine IT-BPM**

Alternative:

> **The jobs behind Philippine IT-BPM's `higher-value work` shift**

Recommendation:

Use the first unless editorial review finds it too generic. It states exactly what the article answers and does not imply measured growth.

## A13 - Dek audit

Working dek:

> Official skills frameworks and a structured sample of 15 current job postings point to specialized work in finance, healthcare, data, process control and global capability centers. The phrase does not tell us how common those jobs are or whether they pay more.

Assessment:

Strong and appropriately limited. `Process control` could be misread as industrial process-control engineering.

Suggested revision:

> Official skills frameworks and a structured sample of 15 current job postings point to specialized work in finance, healthcare, data, operations, risk and global capability centers. The phrase does not tell us how common those jobs are or whether they pay more.
