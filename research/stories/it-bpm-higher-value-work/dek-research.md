## C009-specific conclusion

The headline already promises a definition.

The strongest missing layer is not another abstract definition and not a caveat list. It is the **range of actual work** hidden by one industry phrase.

The approved body demonstrates that range through roles that are materially different from one another.

A useful dek should therefore make the phrase concrete with a small number of contrasting examples.

It should not try to also explain:

- the 15-role sample method;
- wage limitations;
- prevalence limitations;
- transition limitations;
- AI/non-voice caveats;
all in the same line.

Those belong in the body and description/methodology surfaces where appropriate.

## Candidate development

### Candidate A — recommended

> **The same industry phrase can point to credit analysis, clinical informatics or workforce planning.**

Why it fits:

- one sentence;
- adds concrete examples rather than restating the headline;
- makes the term's breadth visible immediately;
- uses examples already verified and present in the approved body;
- does not imply that the three roles form an official category;
- does not claim pay, growth, seniority or transition access;
- no transition glue, two-part thesis scaffold or framework terminology;
- no AI-opportunity language.

Risk:

`can point to` remains an editorial synthesis rather than an official IBPAP classification. That is acceptable because the body explicitly explains the phrase as industry shorthand and grounds the examples in official frameworks/current postings.

### Candidate B — hold

> **Credit analysis, clinical informatics and workforce planning are three very different kinds of work caught by the same label.**

Strength:

Concrete and clear.

Weakness:

`caught by the same label` is more rhetorical and can imply a stronger formal labeling relationship than the evidence supports.

Decision: `HOLD / NOT RECOMMENDED.`

### Candidate C — reject

> **Official frameworks and current job postings point to specialized work across finance, healthcare, data, operations and risk, but the phrase does not show how common those jobs are or whether they pay more.**

Decision: `REJECT.`

Reason:

This recreates the old mechanically structured dek: evidence list + limitation clause. It is accurate but overloaded, abstract and visibly assembled from the research memo.

### Candidate D — reject

> **It is not just AI or non-voice work.**

Decision: `REJECT.`

Reason:

Too thin and defined by negation. It gives the reader less information than the approved body can support.
