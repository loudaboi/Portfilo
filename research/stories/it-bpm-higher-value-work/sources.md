# C009 Source Inventory - Higher-Value Work in Philippine IT-BPM

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Source policy

This inventory supports the C009 question:

> What does "higher-value work" actually mean inside Philippine IT-BPM?

The first full pass prioritizes official sector frameworks and current employer career pages. Job postings are evidence that a role is being advertised and of the tasks/requirements stated by the employer. They are not evidence of sector-wide prevalence, realized compensation, actual day-to-day work, or future employment growth.

Access date for current webpages below: 2026-09-03 unless otherwise stated.

## S001 - OECD Economic Surveys: Philippines 2026

**Source:** OECD  
**URL:** https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-philippines-2026_f0e0c581-en/full-report/sustaining-growth-and-stability-amid-headwinds_a8f57a2c.html  
**Type / tier:** Multilateral economic analysis using IBPAP and other data / Tier B  
**Publication:** 2026

**Why it matters:** Describes the Philippine IT-BPM sector at roughly 1.8 million workers and says employment remains concentrated in contact centres and back-office operations while the sector has diversified into higher-value segments including global capability centres, healthcare information management, and IT/software.

**Limitations:** The `higher-value` label is descriptive, not a standardized occupational classification. The sector composition cited by OECD relies partly on IBPAP data.

## S002 - IBPAP Investors page

**Source:** IT & Business Process Association of the Philippines  
**URL:** https://www.ibpap.org/investors  
**Type / tier:** Industry association primary statement / Tier A for IBPAP's current positioning  
**Current page:** 2026

**Why it matters:** Shows the industry's own current vertical framing: contact center/business process services, GCC/shared services, healthcare IT/information management, IT/software, animation and game development. It describes examples including analytics, digital support, finance, cloud, cybersecurity, medical coding, telehealth, and life-sciences research.

**Limitations:** Promotional/investor-facing industry material. It establishes how IBPAP describes opportunity, not the size, wages, or growth rate of individual job families.

## S004 - Philippine Skills Framework for Contact Center and Business Process Management

**Source:** DICT/CCAP framework hosted by Department of Education  
**URL:** https://www.deped.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/Philippine-Skills-Framework-for-Contact-Center-and-Business-Process-Management.pdf  
**Type / tier:** Government/industry skills framework / Tier A  
**Version:** current framework hosted in 2026

**Why it matters:** Defines six tracks inside contact center/BPM: Customer Service and Support, Quality Assurance, Workforce Management, Business Transition and Transformation, Security, and Learning and Development. This is important because specialized or higher-complexity work can exist inside the traditional contact-center/BPM vertical rather than only in separate technology industries.

**Limitations:** A competency framework maps roles and skills; it does not establish current vacancies, headcount, wages, or actual promotion rates.

## Immediate evidence gaps

The current sources are strong enough to map concrete role types and complexity dimensions, but they do not establish:

- the share of Philippine IT-BPM employment represented by each role family in 2026;
- whether those role families are growing fast enough to offset slower hiring in routine work;
- a representative wage premium for `higher-value` work;
- the probability that an existing frontline worker can transition into the sampled roles;
- realized job quality, workload, or compensation from job advertisements alone.

Those questions require separate datasets or reporting and must not be smuggled into C009's conclusion.
