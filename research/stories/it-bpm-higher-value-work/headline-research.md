## Professional guidance

### Poynter — `9 tips for writing stronger headlines`

https://www.poynter.org/educators-students/2017/9-tips-for-writing-stronger-headlines/

Relevant principles:

- be specific rather than vague;
- make the story's actual point;
- do not mislead or overpromise;
- write for reader understanding rather than cleverness.

### Poynter — `10 questions for your headline-writing checklist`

https://www.poynter.org/educators-students/2017/10-questions-for-your-headline-writing-checklist/

Relevant principles:

- every word must be accurate;
- do not undersell or oversell;
- make the right point rather than a side issue;
- keep the headline easy to understand;
- match the story's tone and publication personality.

## Human-edited explainer sample

The examples below were selected for their framing problem: defining an unfamiliar or ambiguous term/entity for a general reader.

### 1. The Guardian — `What is neijuan, and why is China worried about it?`

https://www.theguardian.com/world/2024/dec/24/what-is-neijuan-china-viral-buzzword-laptop-bicycle

Pattern:

`What is X + why it matters?`

Lesson:

A direct definitional headline is appropriate when the reader's first problem is understanding a term. The second clause is useful only because the article also has a substantial significance question.

### 2. The Guardian — `Explainer: what is Volt Typhoon and why is it the 'defining threat of our generation'?`

https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2024/feb/13/volt-typhoon-what-is-it-how-does-it-work-chinese-cyber-operation-china-hackers-explainer

Pattern:

`What is X + why is it significant?`

Lesson:

The headline names the thing and the reader's consequential follow-up. It does not attempt to list every section or caveat.

### 3. The Guardian — `Explainer: what is the filibuster and why do some Democrats want to get rid of it?`

https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2021/jan/30/what-is-filibuster-meaning-republicans-blocking-biden-agenda

Pattern:

`What is X + why is it contested?`

Lesson:

Definition can be the headline's entire organizing logic when the story answers a concrete terminology problem.

### 4. Associated Press — `EXPLAINER: What is ChatGPT and why are schools blocking it?`

https://apnews.com/article/ac4967a4fb41fda31c4d27f015e32660

Pattern:

`What is X + why is an institution reacting?`

Lesson:

A plain `What is...` construction is not weak merely because it is literal. The second clause earns its place by naming a major practical consequence.

### 5. Reuters — `What is visual verification and why does Reuters practice it?`

https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/what-is-visual-verification-why-does-reuters-practice-it-2026-08-28/

Pattern:

`What is X + why is it used?`

Lesson:

Reuters uses a direct definition question when the article's job is procedural explanation rather than argument or narrative.

### 6. Reuters — `What is ISIS-K, the group that attacked a Moscow concert hall?`

https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/why-did-isis-k-attack-moscow-theater-2024-03-23/

Pattern:

`What is X + identifying context`

Lesson:

The headline can use contextual scope rather than a `why` clause when that is the most useful way to orient readers.

## Cross-example findings

### Finding 1 — direct definition is a professional explainer pattern

There is no need to replace a literal `What ... means` headline simply to make it sound more authored.

### Finding 2 — a second `why` clause must be earned

C009's approved body primarily explains meaning, boundaries and examples. It does not establish one singular consequence strong enough to dominate the headline.

Adding `why it matters`, `why it is growing`, `why workers should care`, or similar language would either become generic or overstate what the evidence can prove.

### Finding 3 — scope matters more than novelty

C009 needs both:

- the contested industry phrase;
- the Philippine IT-BPM context.

A shorter headline such as `What is higher-value work?` loses necessary scope.

### Finding 4 — quotation marks perform a useful editorial job

Keeping `higher-value work` in quotation marks signals that the story is unpacking an industry phrase rather than endorsing it as a formal occupation class.

## Re-audit of current headline

> **What 'higher-value work' means in Philippine IT-BPM**

Strengths:

- says exactly what the article answers;
- contains the important geographic/sector scope;
- quotation marks flag the phrase as terminology under examination;
- does not imply measured growth, wage advantage, better job quality or transition access;
- fits the human-edited definitional-explainer pattern;
- easy to parse;
- already has editorial history and does not need novelty for novelty's sake.

Risk:

It is deliberately literal rather than high-concept. For an Explained story whose central job is definition, that is a feature rather than a defect.

## Alternatives considered

### `What counts as 'higher-value work' in Philippine IT-BPM?`

Decision: reject.

Reason:

`counts as` implies a cleaner classification rule than the article can establish. The body explicitly says there is no formal occupational line.

### `The jobs behind Philippine IT-BPM's 'higher-value work' shift`

Decision: reject.

Reason:

`shift` can imply measured labor-market transition or growth. The current evidence cannot establish prevalence or trend magnitude.

### `What 'higher-value work' actually means in Philippine IT-BPM`

Decision: reject.

Reason:

`actually` adds adversarial/click-oriented emphasis without adding information.

### `How Philippine IT-BPM is moving into higher-value work`

Decision: reject.

Reason:

This converts an explainer into a trend story and implies evidence of movement at scale that the 15-role illustrative sample cannot provide.
