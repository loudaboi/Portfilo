# C009 Structured Role Sample - Higher-Value Work in Philippine IT-BPM

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Purpose

This is a deliberately structured **illustrative role sample**, not a prevalence sample of Philippine IT-BPM employment.

It exists to test a narrower question:

> When the industry talks about `higher-value work`, what kinds of work are visible in current employer hiring, and what distinguishes them from more routine frontline work?

The sample must not be used to calculate the percentage of IT-BPM jobs in any category, sector-wide growth, or average compensation.

## Collection method

### Primary evidence universe

Employer-owned career pages for organizations with substantial Philippine service, shared-service, technology, healthcare, banking, or contact-center operations.

### Collection window

Priority was given to postings current or posted from May through September 2026, with the strongest concentration in August-September 2026. Public-story examples were rechecked on 2026-09-03 before the pre-publication gate.

### Selection rule

Roles were purposefully selected to cover multiple operating contexts rather than randomly sampled. The sample includes:

- frontline/routine service baselines;
- finance and healthcare domain-intensive work;
- data/technology work;
- process, workforce, risk, and transformation work;
- GCC/shared-services capability roles.

### Classification rule

Do **not** classify a role as `higher-value` because of its title, employer, seniority, salary, or use of the word `AI`.

Instead, record whether the advertised work shows one or more of these observable dimensions:

1. **Domain depth** - specialized finance, clinical, legal/regulatory, industry, or other subject knowledge;
2. **Technical/data depth** - analytics, engineering, data, software, cybersecurity, systems, or specialized tools;
3. **Judgment/control responsibility** - decisions, risk, compliance, exception handling, or consequential review;
4. **Process/system ownership** - design, transformation, forecasting, governance, improvement, architecture, or operating-model responsibility;
5. **Stakeholder scope** - cross-functional, client, regional/global, product, engineering, or leadership coordination.

A role can score strongly on one dimension and weakly on another. This is not a numeric score and not an official industry taxonomy.

## What the sample supports

### 1. `Higher-value work` is not one occupation

The current sample spans at least four practical clusters:

- **domain-intensive services**, such as credit/risk, capital-markets operations and clinical work;
- **data and technology work**, such as analytics, informatics and systems operations;
- **process/control work**, such as workforce planning, business-process analysis and risk/control functions;
- **GCC/shared-services capability ownership**, such as finance data, governance and enterprise transformation.

These clusters overlap. They should not be presented as mutually exclusive official categories.

### 2. The clearest dividing line is not voice versus non-voice

A non-voice role can still be routine and procedural. A contact-center organization can contain specialized workforce-management, quality, transformation or security roles. The more useful distinction is the mix of domain expertise, technical depth, judgment, process ownership and stakeholder scope.

### 3. `Technical` is not automatically synonymous with `higher-value`

The Cognizant Systems Engineer example contains genuine troubleshooting and technical responsibilities while still involving standardized changes, routine service operations and escalation. Technical content exists on a continuum of discretion and complexity.

### 4. Specialized work is not necessarily senior-only

Johnson & Johnson's Global Finance Masterdata Analyst accepts 0-2 years of relevant experience while requiring finance-master-data knowledge, controls, risk handling and process-improvement responsibilities. This complicates any framing that equates `higher-value` solely with management seniority.

### 5. Job titles are an unreliable classifier

Cognizant's `PE-CX-Multi Channel Helpdesk` title masks capital-markets operations involving trade lifecycle, collateral, reconciliations and claims. Classification should use tasks and requirements, not titles alone.

## What the sample does not support

Do not infer from these 15 selected postings:

- how common each family is in Philippine IT-BPM;
- whether specialist roles are growing faster than frontline roles inside the sector;
- whether enough specialist positions exist to absorb workers displaced from routine roles;
- average or median pay;
- an automatic wage premium;
- worker mobility or probability of transitioning from J01-J03 into J04-J15;
- that every role in J04-J15 is intrinsically `better` employment.

Those are separate empirical questions.

## Publication-use rule

If C009 proceeds to drafting, describe this as a **structured illustrative sample of current employer postings**, state the selection purpose, and preserve the non-prevalence limitation.

Do not write `Portfilo analyzed the Philippine IT-BPM job market` without immediately defining the sample. A more accurate formulation is that Portfilo reviewed official skills frameworks and a structured sample of current employer postings to translate an industry phrase into concrete role characteristics.
