# C009 Working Notes - Higher-Value Work in Philippine IT-BPM

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Research checkpoint

**Public-data/source pass:** COMPLETE, first pass  
**Original question:** viable but too broad if it promises wage, prevalence or worker-transition conclusions  
**Narrowed publishable question:**

> What jobs are Philippine IT-BPM employers actually talking about when they say `higher-value work`?

**Recommended research outcome:** `PUBLISH`

This recommendation means the packet is strong enough to move into drafting **for the narrowed question only**. It does not authorize claims about sector-wide job shares, wage premiums, displacement, or reskilling outcomes.

## Emerging answer

The phrase `higher-value work` is not one occupation and does not have a single operational definition in the sources reviewed.

The most defensible way to explain it from current Philippine frameworks and hiring is as work that moves away from highly standardized volume execution by adding one or more of the following:

- specialized domain knowledge;
- technical or data depth;
- judgment, risk or control responsibility;
- process, system or capability ownership;
- wider stakeholder, regional or global scope.

These characteristics appear in four overlapping practical clusters:

### 1. Domain-intensive services

Examples:

- credit analysis and underwriting;
- markets risk/control and capital-markets operations;
- clinical appeals;
- clinical informatics;
- specialized healthcare-information-management work.

The value claim here comes primarily from deeper subject expertise, higher consequences of error, and more judgment or professional responsibility.

### 2. Data and technology work

Examples:

- banking/risk analytics;
- business/data analytics;
- software/infrastructure;
- cybersecurity;
- clinical informatics.

But `technical` should not be treated as a binary higher-value label. Some technical support remains heavily procedural and escalation-based.

### 3. Process, control and operational-leverage work

Examples:

- workforce forecasting and planning;
- quality assurance;
- business transition/transformation;
- business process analysis;
- risk and security functions.

This matters because higher-complexity work can exist inside contact-center/BPM organizations themselves. The story should not imply that the only path `up the value chain` is leaving contact-center operations for software engineering.

### 4. GCC/shared-services capability ownership

Examples:

- financial shared services and finance master data;
- data governance;
- HR operations/technology;
- supply-chain functions;
- service-center governance;
- enterprise transformation.

At the senior end, these roles can own global processes, controls, platforms, KPIs and operating models. At the analyst level, some specialized finance/shared-service work is accessible with relatively limited experience.

## Strongest original contribution

Existing coverage repeatedly says the Philippine sector is moving toward `higher-value services`, usually naming broad areas such as AI, analytics, healthcare, GCCs or IT.

The Portfilo contribution is more concrete:

1. distinguish the phrase from an official occupational category;
2. use official Philippine skills frameworks to identify the underlying role families;
3. test those families against a structured sample of current employer postings;
4. identify the observable characteristics that make the roles materially different from high-volume standardized work;
5. state what the public evidence still cannot establish.

That is a useful synthesis not found comprehensively in the existing-coverage pass.

## Method note for eventual article

Use the official skills frameworks as the structural backbone, then current job postings as concrete examples.

Do not organize the article as a list of 15 jobs. The sample is evidence for a broader explanation.

A likely structure is:

1. industry keeps saying `higher-value`; define the problem with that phrase;
2. explain that it is not one job category;
3. show the practical dimensions using current roles;
4. explain that specialized work also exists inside contact-center/BPM operations;
5. clarify what this does **not** prove about wages, job growth or worker transitions;
6. end with the actual labor-market questions that remain open.

## Compensation decision

Do not make compensation a central part of this version.

The current primary employer sample lacks sufficiently consistent salary disclosure. Aggregated official wage data do not map neatly onto the role families. Unsampled Jobstreet ranges would introduce false precision.

If compensation becomes its own story, create a separate structured protocol covering:

- role family;
- seniority;
- location;
- base versus incentives;
- salary range availability;
- employer/agency duplication;
- monthly versus annual pay;
- remote/offshore contractor differences;
- advertised versus realized compensation.

## Access and interview assessment

Interviews are `NOT REQUIRED` for this explained/analysis version.

The central claim can be established through official frameworks and employer-primary postings.

Interviews would become more important if Portfilo attempts downstream questions about:

- actual role transitions from frontline work;
- realized job tasks versus advertisements;
- worker experience/job quality;
- compensation;
- employer hiring strategy;
- reskilling outcomes.

Given Portfilo's current stage, do not delay this publishable documentary story solely to obtain executive commentary that is not necessary to answer the narrowed question.

## Visual/data possibilities

Potential useful visual after drafting:

- a simple matrix of the five complexity dimensions versus selected role examples;
- a four-cluster role map showing overlap;
- a `what the phrase does / does not tell you` evidence graphic.

Avoid a chart implying prevalence because the sample is not designed for that.

## Verification-disclosure judgment

The story should not use generic `Portfilo could not verify` language for the missing salary/prevalence questions.

Instead state the limitation directly where needed, for example:

- the reviewed job postings do not provide a representative salary comparison;
- the sample was selected to illustrate role types, not estimate their share of industry hiring;
- industry strategy does not by itself establish worker transition outcomes.

## Candidate derivatives

The current pass strengthens several future questions without answering them:

- C010-style compensation comparison: do specialized IT-BPM roles actually advertise/pay more?
- C011: which AI/data/technical skills appear in a defined sample of IT-BPM postings?
- reskilling outcome question: can frontline workers realistically move into these roles, and on what timeline?
- employment composition: are specialist roles growing fast enough to change the sector's job mix?

Do not automatically activate these while the three-story WIP limit is occupied.

## Story decision

`PUBLISH`

### Scope of that decision

Publishable finding:

> In practice, `higher-value work` in Philippine IT-BPM is an umbrella for several kinds of specialized work, distinguished less by voice/non-voice or AI branding than by domain expertise, technical/data depth, judgment/control responsibility, process ownership and stakeholder scope.

Required limitations:

- not a prevalence study;
- not a wage study;
- not proof of displacement or absorption;
- not proof of worker mobility;
- not a claim that every specialist role is a better job.
