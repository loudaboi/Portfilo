# Portfilo Editorial Lens + Four-Issue Pilot v1

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

_Status: RECOMMENDATION / AWAITING FOUNDER APPROVAL_

## Context

Founder-approved product direction:

- Portfilo v0 follows the **focused business/technology briefing + original reporting** model.
- Portfilo makes **no geographic product promise**.
- A two-issue-per-week rhythm is an **internal pilot target only**, not a public cadence promise.
- Portfilo remains free in v0.
- Homepage copy remains closed until the product itself is better defined.

This document defines the next gate only:

1. the internal editorial selection lens;
2. the structure of a four-issue dry-run pilot;
3. the evidence Portfilo should collect before making any public newsletter promise.

It does not authorize public copy, live sends, site changes, taxonomy migration, or newsletter-template changes.

## Research basis

The product-model research showed several different ways successful newsletter-led products become focused without relying on geography:

- BackScoop uses a narrow startup/deals subject model and publishes Tuesday/Friday.
- Semafor uses specialist briefings with explicit beats such as Business and Technology, generally 2x/week.
- Platformer organizes around a clearly bounded technology beat and combines original reporting, analysis, commentary, and curated links, with at least one free edition weekly.
- Stratechery is organized around the business, strategy, and impact of technology and publishes on a defined recurring schedule.

Useful source pages reviewed for this gate:

- https://backscoop.com/search
- https://backscoop.com/work-with-us
- https://www.semafor.com/newsletters
- https://www.semafor.com/newsletters/tech
- https://www.platformer.news/about/
- https://stratechery.com/stratechery-plus/schedule/

The common pattern is not one universal issue format. The common pattern is that each product has a **clear editorial reason for selecting one development and ignoring another**.

## Recommended internal editorial lens

Portfilo should cover **business and technology developments that materially change how companies, products, markets, infrastructure, or work operate**.

This is an internal selection rule, not homepage copy.

A story does not need to involve both business and technology equally. It belongs when at least one of those domains is central and the development has a concrete effect on how an organization, market, product, system, or working process functions.

Examples of subjects that can fit:

- company strategy, funding, acquisitions, pricing, distribution, business models, and operations;
- software, AI, platforms, products, cloud, payments, data centers, cybersecurity, and connectivity;
- how a technology changes cost, workflow, competition, labor, customer behavior, or company structure;
- how regulation or infrastructure changes what a business or technology product can do;
- original tests, calculations, reporting, or explanatory work that clarify a consequential business/technology question.

This lens is intentionally geography-neutral.

A Philippine company, US AI lab, Japanese manufacturer, European regulation, Singapore startup, or global infrastructure shift may all belong if the editorial question passes the normal Portfilo planning and evidence gates.

No geographic relevance sentence is required.

## Selection test

A candidate for the newsletter should survive four questions:

### 1. What actually changed?

There must be a concrete development, finding, decision, release, transaction, policy, result, or reported question.

Reject broad trend language without an identifiable event or evidence base.

### 2. What does it change in practice?

The development should materially affect at least one of:

- a company;
- a product;
- a market;
- a business model;
- a technology system;
- infrastructure;
- a workflow or job;
- the process of building or operating a business.

This does not mean every brief must contain a long consequences section. It means the item should have a reason to be in Portfilo beyond novelty.

### 3. Can Portfilo establish it responsibly?

A newsletter item still needs a credible source path. Fast format does not relax verification.

Use primary sources, strong secondary reporting, direct observation, Portfilo reporting, or clearly attributed claims.

Do not turn rumor aggregation or social-media chatter into a briefing item merely because it is current.

### 4. Is this worth one of a small number of slots?

The pilot should behave as if attention is scarce.

A development can be real and verified but still not deserve inclusion.

If removing an item does not make the issue meaningfully worse, it is a deletion candidate.

## What the lens is not

It is not:

- `everything important in business and technology`;
- a promise to summarize the whole news cycle;
- a Philippines-first or Southeast Asia-first product;
- an AI-only product;
- a startup-deals newsletter;
- a general finance newsletter;
- a gadget/product-launch feed;
- a required mix of Business / Technology / Startup / AI every issue.

The four public coverage labels may help navigation later, but they do not become issue quotas.

## Four-issue dry-run pilot

The pilot is an internal production test, not a public launch.

Target:

- four complete issues;
- approximately two issues per week for two weeks;
- no public cadence claim;
- no live send required for the pilot;
- use real, verifiable developments only;
- do not fabricate items to fill the structure.

The goal is to learn whether Portfilo can repeatedly produce a useful briefing at the desired quality with current editorial capacity.

## Fixed pilot skeleton

Keep the structure stable across all four issues so the test measures editorial feasibility rather than constant format changes.

### 1. Lead item

One development that deserves the most attention in that issue.

Working target:

- one concise headline;
- approximately 120-180 words of newsletter copy;
- enough sourcing and explanation to establish what happened and the key material detail;
- link to a Portfilo story when one exists, otherwise link to the strongest appropriate source only if the final newsletter policy permits external-source linking.

The word target is provisional and should be measured during the pilot, not advertised publicly.

### 2. Briefs

Target:

- 3-5 additional items;
- approximately 60-100 words each;
- each item must survive the same verification and selection lens;
- no requirement to fill all five slots.

A three-brief issue that is strong is preferable to a five-brief issue padded with weak material.

### 3. Original Portfilo work, when available

If a substantial Portfilo Feature, Test, reported story, explainer, or analysis is genuinely ready, include one as an additional destination.

Do not require one in every pilot issue.

Do not rewrite a weak or unfinished story just to satisfy the newsletter format.

### 4. End

Keep the pilot ending minimal.

No lifestyle links, recommendations, memes, quotes, jobs, events, tools, or recurring filler modules unless later research establishes a real reader need for them.

The pilot should test the core information product first.

## Why the pilot uses one stable skeleton

BackScoop, Semafor, Platformer, and Stratechery use different editorial forms, but each has a stable reader expectation.

Portfilo does not yet know which details of its own product should become promises.

Changing the number of sections, voice, cadence, and format in every pilot issue would make it difficult to determine whether the core model is sustainable.

The first pilot therefore tests **selection + verification + concise briefing production**, not format creativity.

## Pilot workflow

For each of the four issues:

1. collect a candidate pool from real developments;
2. apply the internal lens and existing Portfilo planning/evidence standards;
3. select one lead and up to five additional briefs;
4. source and verify each selected item;
5. draft the issue using the fixed skeleton;
6. edit for duplication, filler, unsupported interpretation, and AI-shaped prose;
7. record production metrics;
8. perform a post-issue review before starting the next issue.

Do not send a pilot issue publicly merely because it is complete.

## Required pilot measurements

After each issue record:

### Supply

- number of candidate developments considered;
- number that survived the selection lens;
- number actually used;
- reasons strong candidates were rejected.

### Time

- sourcing/research time;
- drafting time;
- editing/verification time;
- total issue-production time.

### Evidence quality

For every item, record whether the evidence base is primarily:

- primary source;
- Portfilo original reporting/testing;
- strong secondary reporting;
- mixed.

Flag any item that required weak sourcing pressure to make the issue feel full.

### Issue quality

Review:

- whether the lead clearly deserved lead position;
- whether any brief was filler;
- whether items repeated the same underlying development;
- whether the issue felt too broad or too narrow;
- whether the issue had enough differentiation from simply opening a general news site;
- whether the issue contained unsupported interpretation;
- whether the writing stayed concise without becoming shallow.

### Product observations

Measure actual, not promised:

- final word count;
- approximate reading time;
- number of items;
- subject distribution;
- geography distribution;
- original-Portfilo vs externally sourced material;
- links clicked during internal QA if useful.

Geography is measured only to observe what the selection lens naturally produces. It is not a quota.

## Four issue sequence

### Issue 1 - Baseline

Use the fixed skeleton with no attempt to optimize for a particular topic mix.

Question:

> What does Portfilo naturally select when it applies the lens to a real news cycle?

### Issue 2 - Repeatability

Use the same skeleton again.

Question:

> Can Portfilo produce the same level of usefulness without relying on the exact kinds of stories that happened to be available for Issue 1?

### Issue 3 - Scarcity test

Apply a stricter deletion standard.

Do not add an item merely to reach the target range.

Question:

> Does the product remain useful when Portfilo refuses to fill weak slots?

### Issue 4 - Sustainability test

Produce the issue using what has been learned from the first three, while keeping the same public-facing skeleton.

Question:

> Is there enough evidence to commit to a recurring v0 format and cadence?

## Pilot success criteria

The pilot can support a recurring newsletter decision only if all of the following are true:

- four issues can be completed without fabricated or obviously weak filler;
- sourcing quality remains within Portfilo standards;
- the selection lens produces a recognizable business/technology product without forcing geography or category quotas;
- issue production is feasible alongside deeper Portfilo reporting;
- concise format does not routinely require unsupported interpretation;
- the strongest items feel meaningfully selected rather than mechanically summarized;
- no recurring section exists only because the template has space for it.

## Possible post-pilot outcomes

### A. Model 2 confirmed

Evidence supports a recurring focused briefing plus original reporting.

Then decide:

- public cadence;
- final issue structure;
- reading-time claim, if supported;
- newsletter copy;
- template changes;
- website/newsletter relationship.

### B. Reduce frequency

The product works but two issues per week is not sustainable.

Move toward a weekly or event-driven recurring briefing rather than lowering quality.

### C. Move toward analysis-led Model 3

If high-quality current briefs are consistently difficult to sustain but deeper Portfilo work is strong, reduce the briefing role and make the newsletter more closely tied to original analysis/reporting.

### D. Reopen the lens

If the pilot naturally produces a product that is too broad, incoherent, or indistinguishable from general news aggregation, narrow the editorial lens before writing public copy.

## Copy boundary

No homepage/newsletter marketing copy should be drafted from this document alone.

After the pilot defines what Portfilo can actually sustain, public copy must be derived from:

1. observed pilot facts;
2. the approved newsletter-copy reference corpus;
3. explicitly approved product decisions.

Do not invent a cadence, reading-time promise, geographic identity, information benefit, or audience claim before the pilot supports it.
