## Purpose

Define the v0 visual system for the black featured-story cover panel on the Portfilo homepage.

This is a topic-level editorial/brand surface, not documentary story evidence and not yet a per-story hero-image system.

System logic:

`featured story -> topic -> canonical topic cover asset`

The featured story continues to be selected by the existing homepage logic. Its canonical `topic` determines which cover asset is shown.

## Research basis

This direction is based on researched professional editorial practice, not model taste alone.

Relevant references and transferable findings:

- Fast Company, `Inside the redesign of Fast Company magazine`
  - https://www.fastcompany.com/90490678/inside-the-redesign-of-fast-company-magazine
  - transferable principle: strong editorial systems balance consistency and variety; stable brand rules can support more expressive content-specific layouts and imagery.
- Fast Company, `How to redesign a magazine, Fast Company-style`
  - https://www.fastcompany.com/90946499/how-to-redesign-a-magazine-fast-company-style
  - transferable principle: analytical editorial content benefits from disciplined systems combined with high-impact visual moments rather than repetitive generic business imagery.
- Rest of World, `New year, new look`
  - https://restofworld.org/inside/homepage-redesign/
  - transferable principle: avoid both rigid one-layout systems and over-engineered systems with too many choices; use a small flexible visual system that supports editorial variation.
- Rest of World visual identity/style guidance
  - https://restofworld.org/style-guide/
  - transferable principle: publication-wide image and design rules should remain consistent across contexts while supporting subject variation.
- The Washington Post, `How The Washington Post design team works with freelance illustrators`
  - https://www.washingtonpost.com/pr/2020/12/18/washington-post-design-team-is-looking-illustrators/
  - transferable principle: concept review precedes execution; multiple rough directions are evaluated before final illustration work.
- Society of Publication Designers medal finalist records
  - https://www.spd.org/spd-61-medal-finalists
  - https://www.spd.org/spd-58-medal-finalists
  - used only as a professional-quality filter showing that publications such as Fast Company, WIRED, MIT Technology Review, Bloomberg Businessweek, and other references belong to established award-recognized editorial-design practice.

These sources do not authorize copying any publication's style, composition, or distinctive visual identity. They support transferable system decisions only.

`Proven` in this context means established and repeatedly used professional editorial-design practice. It does not mean there is objective evidence that one aesthetic direction will maximize audience performance for Portfilo.

## Why this direction was selected

The original three directions were:

- `A — Working Worlds`: environment-led realistic editorial imagery;
- `B — Material Evidence of Work`: object/process-led imagery;
- `C — Human Work`: human-action-led imagery.

The selected direction combines A with B's restraint.

Reasons:

- environment-led imagery creates the strongest differentiation among Business, Technology, Startups, AI, and Building;
- a material/process anchor prevents the environment from becoming an over-detailed generic workplace scene;
- humans remain available where useful without becoming a mandatory stock-photo-like motif;
- the system preserves one Portfilo visual grammar while allowing subject/context to change;
- the approach is appropriate for the relatively compact black homepage panel because one dominant anchor can remain readable at small sizes.

## V0 scope

Create five canonical topic-cover assets:

1. `Business`
2. `Technology`
3. `Startups`
4. `AI`
5. `Building`

Do not create a separate asset for every story in this stage.

A later phase may evaluate topic variants or story-specific overrides after these five assets have been tested together.

## Shared visual grammar

All five topic covers must share:

- realistic editorial illustration rather than cartoon/anime/comic treatment;
- monochrome / grayscale base;
- near-black / off-white tonal compatibility with the homepage feature panel;
- high but readable contrast;
- strong geometry and negative space;
- directional or low-key lighting;
- restrained visible grain / texture;
- quiet, serious, observational editorial atmosphere;
- ordinary work/building/process environments rather than glossy advertising sets;
- restrained information density;
- no generated typography;
- no fake logos;
- no readable screens or interfaces;
- no readable documents, signs, dashboards, charts, or headlines;
- no named-person likenesses;
- no unrelated hues;
- no imitation of a named artist, photographer, publication, or supplied reference.

The five topics must feel like one publication, not five separate art directions.

## Core composition rule

Each cover should contain:

- **one environment**;
- **one dominant material/process anchor**;
- **zero or one anonymous human presence**.

The environment establishes the topic world.

The material/process anchor gives the image focus and small-size readability.

Human presence is optional and must remain secondary to the environment and anchor.

Avoid compositions where meaning depends on many small objects or multiple simultaneous actions.

## Topic expressions

### Business

Visual territory:

`coordination / exchange / operating decisions`

Scene direction:

A realistic generic operational environment where work, materials, or resources move through an organized system.

Possible material/process anchors:

- a shared operating surface;
- routing or sorting elements;
- a handoff point;
- organized physical units or inventory;
- an operational checkpoint;
- a physical exchange or coordination mechanism.

Avoid:

- handshakes;
- smiling executives;
- skyscraper shorthand;
- money piles;
- boardroom cliché;
- laptop-and-coffee business stock imagery;
- finance dashboards.

Target reading:

Business as real-world coordination and operating systems, not corporate symbolism.

### Technology

Visual territory:

`tools / systems / infrastructure / technical process`

Scene direction:

A realistic generic technical environment where a physical system is being built, inspected, maintained, or operated.

Possible material/process anchors:

- connectors;
- cabling;
- instrument surfaces;
- machine housings;
- non-branded equipment;
- physical technical modules;
- structured tool systems.

Avoid:

- neon circuitry;
- floating UI;
- hacker/cyberpunk treatment;
- server racks as universal shorthand;
- sci-fi glow;
- recognizable branded hardware.

Target reading:

Technology as practical systems and tools that are physically built and operated.

### Startups

Visual territory:

`prototyping / iteration / constrained early-stage making`

Scene direction:

An unfinished or provisional working environment where something is actively being tested, revised, or assembled.

Possible material/process anchors:

- an unfinished prototype;
- taped or temporary assembly;
- test pieces;
- mock components;
- a trial setup;
- visible physical revision without readable text.

Avoid:

- celebratory founders;
- pitch decks;
- rocket/growth metaphors;
- generic coworking scenes;
- startup-equals-laptop-and-coffee shorthand.

Target reading:

Startups as incomplete but active creation under constraint.

### AI

Visual territory:

`human-machine process / automation entering work`

Scene direction:

A restrained work environment where a machine-assisted or semi-automated process is physically present without fantasy AI symbolism.

Possible material/process anchors:

- an input/output station;
- repeated processed units;
- an inspection or review station;
- a generic automated apparatus;
- a physical handoff between human work and machine process;
- compute-linked physical process without branded hardware or readable UI.

Avoid:

- robot heads;
- humanoid androids;
- glowing brains;
- neon neural networks;
- magical screens;
- floating UI;
- sci-fi spectacle.

Target reading:

AI as an operational process entering work, not an abstract mythology.

### Building

Visual territory:

`making / assembly / structural or operational construction`

Scene direction:

A realistic generic making environment with clear evidence of assembly or construction in progress.

Possible material/process anchors:

- joined parts;
- fitted materials;
- measured assembly;
- structural surfaces;
- a tool beside active work;
- an unfinished constructed object.

Avoid:

- hard-hat category cliché;
- construction-site shorthand unless a later specific assignment requires it;
- blueprints;
- inspirational maker imagery.

Target reading:

Building as tactile, grounded, in-progress creation.

## Human presence

Humans are optional.

If used, they must be:

- anonymous;
- partial, distant, silhouetted, or visually secondary;
- non-identifiable;
- engaged in ordinary work rather than expressive acting;
- free of branded clothing/uniforms that imply a real organization.

Do not use:

- portrait emphasis;
- smiling corporate-team treatment;
- handshake gestures;
- heroic or celebratory posing;
- staged concentration as the main visual idea;
- any likeness of a real identifiable person.

## Color decision

First-pass decision:

`GRAYSCALE / NO CHROMATIC ACCENT`

Reason:

The homepage panel already has a strong black/blue interface identity. The pilot should first test whether subject, geometry, light, and texture carry the visual system without relying on topic colors.

Portfilo blue or red may be reconsidered only after the grayscale system is evaluated in the actual homepage context.

## Small-panel readability rule

The image should remain compositionally legible when reduced.

A useful evaluation heuristic:

If the image is blurred or viewed from a distance, its dominant geometry, major light/dark relationship, and one primary anchor should remain visible.

The image does **not** need to identify the topic by itself because the homepage panel already presents the topic text deterministically in HTML.

Do not turn the cover into a category icon.

## Main risks and controls

### Generic stock feel

Control:

Use environment and material/process logic rather than staged people, generic desks, or smiling teams.

### Fake documentary workplace

Control:

Keep environments generic/editorialized. Prohibit branding, readable interfaces/documents, distinctive company architecture, real-person likenesses, and named products.

### AI / Technology collapse

Control:

Technology focuses on technical systems/tools/infrastructure. AI focuses on automation or human-machine process entering work. Neither uses sci-fi iconography.

### Business abstraction

Control:

Represent Business through physical coordination, exchange, routing, handoff, and operating systems rather than symbolic money/corporate objects.

### Excessive detail

Control:

One environment + one dominant anchor + zero/one secondary person.

## Pilot production strategy

Do not generate all five immediately.

Representative test pair:

1. `Building`
2. `AI`

Reason:

They are meaningfully different topic territories. If one shared visual grammar can produce both without making them look like the same generic AI-generated universe, the system has evidence of useful range.

After the representative pair is reviewed in homepage context, either:

- approve the grammar and continue with Technology, Startups, and Business; or
- revise the grammar before generating the remaining three.
