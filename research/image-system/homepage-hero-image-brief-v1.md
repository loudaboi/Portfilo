# Portfilo Homepage Hero Image Brief v1

_Status: PROPOSED / AWAITING CONCEPT SELECTION_

_Date: 2026-09-07_

## Purpose

Define the visual source of truth for the new Portfilo newsletter-first homepage hero image before any candidate generation.

This is a non-story editorial/brand surface. The image supports Portfilo as a business and technology news brand; it does not illustrate one specific article or claim.

This brief follows `docs/IMAGE_SYSTEM.md`. AI-generated imagery remains editorial illustration, not documentary evidence. Brief approval does not authorize candidate generation. Generation requires a separate explicit human authorization after the exact generator instruction is presented.

## Homepage context

The approved homepage redesign uses a compact two-column desktop hero:

- left: newsletter label, primary promise, short supporting copy, email signup, microcopy;
- right: one universal editorial hero image;
- below: Latest strip and editorial content.

The hero is intentionally compact rather than full-screen.

Public coverage language for the redesign:

`Business / Technology / Startup / AI`

The current runtime taxonomy is not changed by this brief.

## Image job

Primary job: **brand / editorial**.

The image should give Portfilo a contemporary business-and-technology world without depicting or implying a specific reported event.

It should:

- make the homepage feel premium, editorial, current, and serious;
- balance the newsletter copy without overpowering it;
- suggest companies, technology, infrastructure, systems, work, and change through a real-world physical environment;
- remain visually useful for a long period rather than age like a news photograph;
- work as a universal Portfilo homepage image rather than a topic-specific cover;
- carry forward Portfilo's approved grainy editorial texture.

It should not:

- summarize Portfilo literally;
- illustrate a specific company, product, funding round, AI model, policy event, or headline;
- imply that a generated building, workplace, or scene is a real reported location;
- compete with the newsletter promise for attention;
- contain readable text, logos, interfaces, charts, headlines, signage, or brand marks.

## Visual treatment

Carry forward the existing approved Portfilo image character:

- monochrome / grayscale or very nearly neutral base;
- visible **print-like grain** as a signature texture;
- high but readable contrast;
- strong geometry;
- directional natural-looking light and shadow;
- restrained visual information density;
- editorial still rather than glossy advertising imagery;
- no gradient treatment;
- no glow;
- no cyberpunk/neon technology language;
- no decorative blur or artificial depth-of-field effect as the main aesthetic.

### Grain rule

The grain should feel like a controlled editorial print/film texture:

- clearly perceptible at normal desktop viewing size;
- fine enough that architectural edges and important forms remain legible;
- distributed naturally rather than as a uniform digital-noise overlay;
- not distressed, dirty, scratched, retro, sepia, or faux-vintage;
- not so strong that it harms image compression or mobile clarity.

The grain belongs to selected editorial imagery, not to the surrounding UI, page background, forms, or typography.

## Color

Preferred hero treatment: **neutral / grayscale with zero chromatic accent inside the image**.

Reason: the adjacent live interface already carries Portfilo blue through the newsletter CTA and links. Keeping the hero neutral prevents color competition and gives the blue UI more authority.

If later review shows one image accent is genuinely useful, it must use only one approved Portfilo accent and be controlled after generation. Blue and red should not both appear by default.

No unrelated hues.

## Composition contract

### Desktop

Target visual proportion: approximately **8:5 landscape**.

Expected displayed height: roughly **320-380 px** at normal large-desktop widths, subject to the final homepage grid.

The image and the left newsletter-copy column form one shared rectangular hero module.

Hard alignment rule:

- top edge of the left content column aligns with the top edge of the image;
- bottom edge of the left content column, including signup microcopy, aligns with the bottom edge of the image;
- neither side may visibly run taller than the other in the approved desktop composition.

The image must therefore have a stable crop with no important subject matter dependent on the extreme edges.

The visual should have one clear structural anchor and enough calmer surrounding area that it does not fight the headline.

### Mobile

The hero stacks vertically. Equal-height alignment no longer applies.

Mobile crop should:

- remain understandable as a landscape editorial image;
- preserve the main structural anchor;
- avoid depending on ultra-wide peripheral information;
- tolerate modest top/bottom or side cropping without losing the image's logic.

No embedded text means the same asset can be cropped responsively without losing information.

## Concept candidates

### Concept A — Architectural Structure

**Visual subject**

A generic contemporary architectural structure or facade dominated by concrete, glass, steel, shadow, and strong linear geometry. The scene is not identifiable as a specific real building or company property.

**Composition**

One primary architectural mass occupies roughly two-thirds of the frame. A secondary plane, open sky, reflected light, or deep shadow creates negative space and directional contrast. Viewpoint is slightly oblique rather than a centered postcard facade.

**Why it works**

Architecture gives physical form to companies, infrastructure, technology, capital, cities, and systems without becoming literal business stock imagery. It is long-lived and crops cleanly.

**Deliberately omitted**

People as focal subjects, readable signage, logos, company interiors, devices, screens, vehicles, flags, landmark-specific details.

**Risk**

Could become generic real-estate advertising or look like documentary photography of a real named building. The final rendering should feel like an editorial structural study, not property marketing.

**Crop behavior**

Strong. Geometry can survive 8:5 desktop and tighter mobile crops if the primary mass is kept away from extreme edges.

**Current recommendation**

**STRONGEST CANDIDATE.** Best match to the approved homepage mockup and Portfilo's existing visual language.

---

### Concept B — Infrastructure / Systems

**Visual subject**

A generic infrastructure structure such as an elevated transit support, bridge geometry, service corridor, industrial-commercial framework, or layered structural system. The emphasis is on how parts connect rather than on a named place.

**Composition**

One primary structural relationship: for example, repeating beams crossing one main support, or a strong diagonal/vertical system creating depth. No complex mechanical assemblies whose physical logic the generator must invent.

**Why it works**

Feels closer to systems, networks, movement, and enabling infrastructure than a conventional office-building image. It can broaden Portfilo beyond startup/office clichés.

**Deliberately omitted**

Traffic as a focal subject, logos, branded infrastructure, readable signs, complicated machinery, data-center/server-rack clichés, cyber imagery.

**Risk**

Can become cold, overly industrial, or visually associated with construction/infrastructure reporting rather than Portfilo generally.

**Crop behavior**

Good if the composition uses one obvious structural relationship and avoids many small elements.

**Current recommendation**

**BACKUP CANDIDATE.** Use if Concept A feels too conventional or real-estate-like.

---

### Concept C — Human Scale in the Built Environment

**Visual subject**

One anonymous, distant or partial human figure moving through or standing within a contemporary commercial/urban architectural environment. Architecture remains dominant; the person establishes scale only.

**Composition**

One figure at small scale, placed away from the visual center, with one dominant architectural plane or corridor. No face detail and no narrative action beyond ordinary movement/presence.

**Why it works**

Adds human scale and reminds the viewer that business and technology ultimately affect people and work.

**Deliberately omitted**

Teams, meetings, laptops, phones, staged office work, smiles-to-camera, identifiable people, business attire as a corporate cliché.

**Risk**

Highest risk of becoming stock-business imagery or creating an unintended documentary/narrative implication.

**Crop behavior**

More fragile because the anonymous figure must remain visible enough to perform its job without becoming the subject.

**Current recommendation**

**NOT PREFERRED FOR THE FIRST HERO.** Keep only as a fallback if the human presence materially improves a later concept.

## Selection criteria

A concept should be selected only if it can satisfy all of these:

1. **Homepage fit** — strengthens the approved compact newsletter-first composition.
2. **Alignment compatibility** — works in a stable 8:5-ish rectangular cell beside the text stack.
3. **Longevity** — does not depend on a current event or trend.
4. **Editorial character** — feels like an editorial still, not an ad campaign, stock image, or SaaS hero.
5. **Portfilo continuity** — supports monochrome, print-like grain, strong geometry, and restrained contrast.
6. **Non-documentary clarity** — does not imply evidence of a real named place/event/company.
7. **Crop resilience** — remains coherent on desktop and mobile.
8. **Low cliché load** — no robot/AI-brain/handshake/meeting/laptop/neon/stock-office shorthand.
9. **Text independence** — needs no embedded typography or labels.
10. **Visual subordination** — supports rather than overwhelms the newsletter promise and email CTA.

## Hard rejection rules

Reject a candidate if it contains or strongly implies:

- readable text or signage;
- company/product logos;
- fake UI, charts, documents, or screens;
- a recognizable real landmark presented as if documentary;
- impossible architecture, supports, joints, stairs, reflections, perspective, or structural relationships;
- glossy property-marketing aesthetics;
- generic smiling corporate-team imagery;
- laptops/phones as the primary technology shorthand;
- robots, glowing brains, circuitry, neon tech motifs, or cyberpunk styling;
- heavy blur, glow, gradients, lens-flare spectacle, or unrelated colors;
- grain so aggressive that forms become muddy;
- composition whose important content is lost in the required desktop/mobile crops;
- embedded Portfilo wording or any other generated typography.

## Relationship to the old homepage image

Its approved characteristics — monochrome presentation, visible print-like grain, strong geometry, directional contrast, and absence of generated text/logos/screens — remain useful visual continuity.

This brief does **not** delete, invalidate, or automatically reuse that image. The new homepage hero is a different visual job and requires a new explicit selection decision.

The old asset may remain useful for Feature presentation or another later editorial surface if separately justified.

## Approval boundary

Approval of this brief or selection of Concept A authorizes preparation of one exact, decision-complete generator instruction.

It does **not** authorize image generation.

After concept selection:

1. prepare the exact generator instruction from the selected concept;
2. present it for explicit human generation authorization;
3. generate candidate(s) only after that authorization;
4. review geometry, grain, crop, factual implication, and visual fit;
5. human selects or rejects the candidate;
6. implementation remains a later separate step.
