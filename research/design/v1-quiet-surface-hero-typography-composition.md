# Composition thesis

The resting hero should feel complete without pretending the static frame is the entire brand experience.

## First viewport structure

### Header

Top-left:

- Sharp Ornamental 01;
- PORTFILO wordmark in a compact lockup relationship.

Top-right:

- `Work / Services / Hustle / Journal / Contact`.

The header should read as one usable interface band, not as decorative perimeter typography.

### Primary hero group

Lower-left / lower-middle region.

Headline:

**Software built around  
the problem.**

Use two lines.

The headline should be:

- large enough to lead;
- clearly smaller than the rejected giant-poster treatments;
- left aligned;
- compact in measure;
- visually calm.

The line break is semantic rather than decorative.

Do not stagger individual lines horizontally.

Do not distort width per line.

### Support copy

Place the approved support sentence directly in the same compositional family as the headline.

Preferred structure:

- beneath the headline;
- smaller but clearly readable;
- stronger contrast than the previous muted-gray treatment;
- controlled line measure;
- enough separation to read as explanation, not caption.

Do **not** detach it into a distant corner simply to create asymmetry.

### Background

The grainy dark surface supports the foreground.

It should not contain a hero object.

It may later respond subtly to pointer movement, but the static composition is judged with the material at rest.

### Motion allowance

The composition should leave clean clipping boundaries around:

- header band;
- headline block;
- support block.

This gives the later motion prototype meaningful places to mask/reveal without inventing extra wrappers or graphics.

# Candidate A — Schibsted Grotesk

Source:

https://github.com/schibsted/schibsted-grotesk

License:

SIL Open Font License 1.1.

## Why it fits

Schibsted Grotesk is explicitly digital-first and designed for user interfaces while taking cues from Schibsted's print/media history.

That combination maps unusually well to Portfilo:

- software studio;
- editorial Journal;
- founder-focused Hustle;
- one identity spanning product and reading surfaces.

## Proposed use

Hero:

- medium or medium-semi-bold territory;
- normal width;
- restrained tracking;
- no dramatic compression.

Support:

- regular;
- same family.

Navigation:

- medium;
- same family.

## Character

- clean;
- contemporary;
- slightly editorial without becoming publication-first;
- less startup-coded than Geist/Inter;
- less playful than Bricolage.

## Risk

It may lean slightly media/editorial if the hero composition becomes too text-led.

The dark software surface and interaction language must keep the overall read software-first.

## Assessment

**Recommended.**

# Candidate B — Instrument Sans

Source:

https://github.com/Instrument/instrument-sans

License:

SIL Open Font License 1.1.

## Why it remains viable

Instrument Sans is a neo-grotesque variable family with width control and stylistic alternates.

It is already present in Portfilo's current runtime, but the rejected homepage failed as a complete visual system rather than proving the typeface itself was unusable.

## Proposed use

Hero:

- near-normal width;
- medium;
- no aggressive width experimentation.

Support:

- regular.

Navigation:

- medium.

Avoid using stylistic alternates unless a specific glyph improvement earns them.

## Character

- precise;
- polished;
- flexible;
- slightly more design-studio coded.

## Risk

The visual memory of the rejected runtime may keep pulling Portfilo toward the previous creative-tech look.

It is also easier to make Instrument Sans feel like a familiar contemporary studio website.

## Assessment

**Best control candidate.**

# Candidate C — Hanken Grotesk

Source:

https://github.com/marcologous/hanken-grotesk

License:

SIL Open Font License 1.1.

## Why it fits

Hanken Grotesk is designed across signage, books/magazines, interface, web, and mobile use.

That gives it a broad workhorse quality without requiring a display/body pairing.

## Proposed use

Hero:

- medium;
- normal width;
- slightly tighter tracking than body.

Support:

- regular.

Navigation:

- medium.

## Character

- classic grotesque;
- calm;
- straightforward;
- less fashionable than many current startup fonts.

## Risk

It may become too neutral.

If the motion and identity are not strong enough, the hero could feel competent but anonymous.

## Assessment

**Useful restrained alternate.**

# Why not the obvious alternatives

## Mona Sans

Still reopened.

The founder did not like the previous hero treatment.

There is no reason to force another Mona round before a stronger candidate proves necessary.

## Geist

Technically excellent but too strongly associated with contemporary developer/product interfaces for the current Portfilo identity problem.

## Inter / Inter Tight

Excellent workhorse families, but they create the highest risk that the hero's static frame reads as category-standard product UI rather than authored Portfilo.

## Bricolage Grotesque

Founder-rejected for the hero.

Do not revive.

# Recommended system

**Schibsted Grotesk**

Reason:

It does not need to become the visual idea.

It can stay quiet while still giving the headline more drawing character than a pure product-UI default.

It also makes sense across both sides of Portfilo:

- software;
- publishing/editorial.

The approved Noto Serif Display wordmark remains the refined serif identity voice.

Schibsted becomes the website/interface voice.

# Recommended first-viewport composition

Reference viewport:

**1440 × 900**

This is structural intent, not final pixel lock.

## Header zone

Approximate top margin:

48–56 px.

Left identity lockup:

- emblem approximately 28–32 px;
- wordmark approximately 155–180 px wide;
- compact horizontal relationship.

Right navigation:

- one line;
- approximately 14–15 px;
- enough separation for scanning without becoming a decorative tracking exercise.

## Hero zone

The primary group sits in the lower half rather than dead center.

Approximate headline width:

780–900 px.

Headline:

- two lines;
- approximate size territory: 82–96 px at 1440 desktop;
- medium / 500-ish visual weight;
- line height near 0.95–1.0 depending on rendered face;
- no extreme negative tracking.

Exact line break:

**Software built around**  
**the problem.**

Support:

- directly beneath;
- approximately 16–18 px;
- 50–62ch measure;
- normal readable weight;
- high enough contrast to feel intentional;
- no distant detached placement.

## Visual balance

The right side is intentionally quieter.

Do not fill it with:

- an abstract object;
- a card;
- a fake product screen;
- a decorative caption;
- extra labels.

The background material and eventual pointer response are enough.

## Interaction allowance

The hero group can later reveal through one vertical or horizontal clip.

The first-scroll transition can use the lower edge of the viewport as the continuity boundary.

Nothing in the static layout exists only to support animation.

# Anti-slop review

## Category interchangeability

Risk remains if this becomes only:

`dark background + clean sans + headline`.

The static frame is intentionally restrained, so the authored character must ultimately come from:

- Portfilo identity;
- exact copy;
- grain/material quality;
- interaction;
- transition continuity.

This is acceptable only because **Quiet Surface / Active Interface** explicitly locates the strongest authorship in state change.

## Empty-frame compensation

No extra object is added.

The quiet right side is intentional.

## Specificity

Portfilo specificity comes from:

- approved emblem + wordmark;
- problem-led proposition;
- studio/editorial duality in typography;
- later stateful interaction and Work continuity.

Not from decorative material.

## Reviewability

Founder is deciding two things together:

1. whether Schibsted Grotesk is the correct web/interface voice;
2. whether the two-line lower-half composition is the correct resting structure.
