_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

## Scope

This record covers only the approved newsletter-first homepage copy gate:

- small hero label;
- main hero headline;
- one supporting sentence;
- signup button and microcopy;
- final newsletter CTA.

It does not authorize homepage implementation, taxonomy migration, newsletter naming migration outside the approved homepage copy, article changes, or release.

## Governing constraints

The canonical `docs/COPY_STYLE.md` remains authoritative.

Relevant rules:

- say the thing, then stop;
- product clarity before positioning;
- do not manufacture a permanent tagline;
- avoid self-praise such as `clear`, `smart`, `essential`, `practical`, or similar qualities unless a specific supported use exists;
- avoid vague future language such as `what matters`, `what comes next`, or similar copy without a concrete referent;
- lead with what the reader gets;
- newsletter copy should state what arrives and operational facts plainly;
- do not promise a cadence that Portfilo has not operationally established.

The approved homepage design contract requires a compact newsletter-first hero, one supporting sentence, one embedded email form, and a second/final signup only after editorial content.

Public coverage labels remain:

`Business / Technology / Startup / AI`

## Comparative copy research

Current newsletter/news products consistently use concrete acquisition language rather than institutional manifestos.

Observed patterns:

- BackScoop: direct promise to keep readers current on Southeast Asian tech, followed by a literal description of the newsletter's tech/business coverage;
- TLDR: subject + time cost + what stories arrive;
- Morning Brew: what business news arrives and the established daily cadence;
- The Peak: what the reader needs in a morning email and its established weekday cadence;
- The Information: newsletter-specific subject matter and cadence rather than a universal abstract brand slogan;
- Semafor: newsletter-specific information promise plus actual frequency.

Transferable principle for Portfilo:

> State the information job first. Add only operational claims Portfilo can currently support.

Portfilo currently has no approved fixed newsletter cadence and no measured reading-time promise. Do not invent either.

## Rejected mockup headline

`A clearer picture of what's next.`

Verdict: **REJECT AS FINAL COPY / RETAIN ONLY AS HISTORICAL MOCKUP TEXT**.

Reasons:

- `clearer` is an unsupported quality claim;
- `what's next` is vague future-positioning language;
- the line functions like a permanent slogan rather than literal product copy;
- it says less about what the reader will actually receive than the available plain alternatives.

The visual mockup remains approved for composition. Its generated copy has no editorial authority.

## Candidate directions

### Candidate A — recommended

Small label:

`NEWSLETTER`

Headline:

> **Keep up with business and technology.**

Supporting sentence:

> **News and deeper stories from Portfilo, sent by email.**

Button:

`Subscribe free`

Microcopy:

> **Free. Unsubscribe anytime.**

Why it works:

- literal reader job;
- accurately describes the current product direction;
- short enough to support the approved hero composition;
- does not claim speed, cadence, superiority, exclusivity, or geography;
- leaves the surrounding coverage labels and journalism to establish personality.

Risk:

- deliberately plain. The design, journalism, and selected hero image must carry the premium character rather than the sentence trying to sound premium.

## Recommendation

Approve **Candidate A** as the homepage hero package:

> **NEWSLETTER**
>
> **Keep up with business and technology.**
>
> **News and deeper stories from Portfilo, sent by email.**
>
> `[Email address] [Subscribe free]`
>
> **Free. Unsubscribe anytime.**

The input placeholder should remain functional and familiar:

`you@email.com`

Do not add audience flattery, `stay ahead`, `insights`, `what's next`, a reading-time claim, or an unproven delivery cadence.

## Final newsletter CTA

Recommended final CTA after editorial content:

Headline:

> **Get Portfilo in your inbox.**

Supporting sentence:

> **News and Features from Portfilo, by email.**

Button:

`Subscribe free`

Microcopy:

> **Free. Unsubscribe anytime.**

This repeats the product action intentionally after substantial editorial content but does not repeat the hero headline.

If the final page feels over-explained during static composition, the supporting sentence is the first deletion candidate. The final CTA may reduce to:

> **Get Portfilo in your inbox.**
>
> `[Email address] [Subscribe free]`
>
> **Free. Unsubscribe anytime.**

## Operational note

Portfilo currently uses double opt-in. The hero does not need to explain the confirmation step before submission unless usability testing shows confusion. The submission success state should continue to say that the reader must check their inbox and confirm the email.

Do not claim a fixed newsletter schedule until the actual operating cadence is approved and sustained.
