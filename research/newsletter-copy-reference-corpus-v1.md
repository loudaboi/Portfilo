# Newsletter Copy Reference Corpus v1

_Last updated: 2026-09-07_

_Status: RESEARCH ONLY / NO PORTFILO COPY RECOMMENDATION_

## Purpose

This corpus collects current reader-facing copy from established newsletter and news products before Portfilo drafts new homepage newsletter copy.

This file does **not** propose Portfilo copy, positioning, cadence, geography, benefit language, or product promises.

The goal is to separate:

1. real reference language;
2. observable packaging patterns;
3. Portfilo facts and decisions, which belong in a later gate.

No phrase below becomes Portfilo authority merely because another publication uses it.

## Research method

For each reference, capture:

- the current primary proposition or newsletter descriptor;
- the operational promise when visible, such as cadence or reading time;
- the concrete subject framing;
- the structural pattern used to communicate the product.

Short quoted fragments are included only to preserve the actual reader-facing language. Supporting descriptions are summarized rather than copied at length.

## Neutral pattern map

The corpus shows several recurring structures. These are observations, not Portfilo recommendations.

### Pattern A — functional catch-up promise

Examples:

- TLDR;
- Axios AM;
- Bloomberg Morning Briefing;
- Techmeme.

Common structure:

`what the reader can do -> what is covered -> time/cadence/format`

Typical verbs:

- keep up;
- catch up;
- start your day;
- stay on top;
- gather;
- follow.

### Pattern B — concrete editorial output

Examples:

- Bloomberg Tech In Depth;
- Platformer;
- Stratechery;
- Newcomer.

Common structure:

`reporting/analysis/scoops -> precise subject -> author/editor authority`

These products often rely less on slogan-like hero language because the type of journalism itself is specific.

### Pattern C — habit + operating constraint

Examples:

- Morning Brew;
- The Peak;
- Semafor Flagship;
- TLDR.

Common structure:

`delivery habit -> explicit cadence -> reading-time or format constraint -> subject`

The product is easier to describe because the operating promise is concrete.

### Pattern D — personality-led proposition

Examples:

- The Hustle;
- BackScoop to a lesser extent.

Common structure:

`short branded line -> immediate concrete explanation -> content demonstrates voice`

The branded line is not expected to carry the full product definition by itself.

### Pattern E — proof-led acquisition

Examples:

- Morning Brew;
- 1440;
- The Peak;
- Semafor;
- The Information;
- Newcomer.

Common proof types:

- subscriber count;
- professional/executive readership;
- retention/unsubscribe data;
- named journalist/editor;
- established brand reputation;
- methodology/source-count claims.

### Pattern F — narrow subject/lens definition

Examples:

- Bloomberg Tech In Depth: business of technology;
- Stratechery: strategy/business side of technology and media;
- Newcomer: technology + venture capital/startup inner workings;
- Semafor Technology: AI through people/money/ideas;
- Platformer: AI + social platforms + people shaping technology.

These products make the subject itself do much of the positioning work.

## What established products tend to make concrete

Across the corpus, the strongest recurring factual anchors are:

- subject;
- cadence;
- reading time;
- number of stories/items;
- delivery moment;
- reporting mode;
- named author/editor;
- source/method;
- subscriber proof;
- free vs paid status.

Not every product uses every anchor.

## Terms that appear because the underlying product supports them

The corpus includes words such as:

- important;
- smart;
- unbiased;
- trust;
- analysis;
- scoops;
- informed;
- deeper;
- independent.

Their presence in a successful reference is **not** evidence that Portfilo should use them. In the references, these words are usually attached to an operating model, reputation, methodology, author, proof point, or established editorial product.

## Research boundary

This corpus intentionally does **not** answer:

- what Portfilo's newsletter cadence should be;
- whether Portfilo should promise a reading time;
- whether Portfilo should emphasize any geography;
- whether Portfilo should use a slogan;
- which reference pattern Portfilo should adopt;
- what the Portfilo hero headline or support line should say.

Those require a separate Portfilo product-facts and direction gate.
