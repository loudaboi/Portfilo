# Portfilo Editorial Launch Research - Stage 6 Evaluation

_Last updated: 2026-09-02_

## Purpose

Apply the six editorial gates from `docs/CONTENT_PLANNING.md` to the 28 Stage 5 candidate stories and reduce the pool before any launch package is selected.

This is editorial selection, not story research. A `PASS` means a candidate is strong enough to remain in launch consideration. It does not mean the story is assigned, proven, or ready to draft.

The six gates are:

1. Question
2. Importance
3. Timing
4. Contribution
5. Evidence
6. Feasibility

Evaluation shorthand:

- `YES` - currently strong enough for the gate;
- `PARTIAL` - plausible but materially conditional;
- `NO` - insufficient as a standalone launch candidate.

Priority is assigned only to `PASS` candidates.

## PASS - HIGH

### C001 - AI pilot to production

**Decision:** PASS / HIGH

Why it survives:

- the question is specific and consequential;
- the timing is supported by current evidence of broad experimentation but shallow production deployment;
- current coverage describes barriers but rarely reconstructs successful and failed implementations at operating-detail level;
- Portfilo can contribute comparative reporting rather than another adoption survey summary.

Main dependency:

Access to real projects, including at least one stalled or failed case if possible. Success-only vendor case studies are insufficient.

### C002 - All-in cost of one AI customer-support workflow

**Decision:** PASS / HIGH

Why it survives:

- strong decision value for companies considering deployment;
- materially different from product-price comparisons;
- clear path to original calculation and transparent cost modeling;
- directly addresses the difference between buying AI software and operating an AI-enabled process.

Main dependency:

A credible real-world cost base. If private company data is unavailable, the story must explicitly become a transparent modeled scenario rather than imply observed ROI.

### C009 - What "higher-value work" means in IT-BPM

**Decision:** PASS / HIGH

Why it survives:

- the phrase is central to current industry strategy but remains abstract;
- evidence can be built from job postings, employer materials, recruiters, workers, compensation data, and official/industry sources;
- the result can help readers understand the labor-market transition without repeating generic "AI will change BPO" framing.

Main dependency:

Define job-family inclusion criteria before collecting data so unlike roles are not compared casually.

### C017 - Who pays for zero-fee small-merchant digital payments

**Decision:** PASS / HIGH

Why it survives:

- the rule change is current and already well documented at the announcement level;
- the unresolved layer is economic incidence, which is a distinct Portfilo contribution;
- the question can be answered through BSP rules, provider economics, fee schedules, interviews, and explicit calculations.

Main dependency:

Separate regulated fee treatment from broader commercial pricing and avoid assuming providers recover costs in one particular way without evidence.

### C018 - What Direct Debit PH changes for a recurring-payment business

**Decision:** PASS / HIGH

Why it survives:

- newly launched infrastructure creates a clear timing trigger;
- existing coverage explains what the service is but not the full operating implications for a business using it;
- the reader payoff is concrete: setup, collection flow, failed payments, reconciliation, fees, settlement, and alternatives.

Main dependency:

Operational documentation and at least one realistic business-use case. Avoid presenting early availability as evidence of broad adoption.

### C021 - Operating vs under-construction vs announced data-center capacity

**Decision:** PASS / HIGH

Why it survives:

- public discussion frequently mixes different capacity states;
- the distinction is measurable and consequential for understanding actual infrastructure supply;
- Portfilo can add a verified project-status dataset rather than another expansion-announcement story.

Main dependency:

Use explicit status definitions and source each project's state. Announced MW must not be treated as operating capacity.

## PASS - MEDIUM

### C007 - Buy, integrate, or build an AI workflow

**Decision:** PASS / MEDIUM

Strong decision value and feasible source paths, but it should be scoped to one defined workflow. A generic "build vs buy" article would become consultancy-style advice and lose Portfilo specificity.

### C011 - AI-related skill requirements in IT-BPM job postings

**Decision:** PASS / MEDIUM

Strong evidence path and measurable contribution. It ranks below C009 because it is narrower and may work better as a supporting dataset or follow-up unless the sample reveals a strong independent finding.

### C020 - Direct Debit PH vs cards vs transfer-based collection

**Decision:** PASS / MEDIUM

Useful decision journalism with an auditable comparison path. It should not duplicate C018. C018 asks what the new rail changes operationally; C020 asks how alternative collection methods compare for a defined small-business use case.

### C023 - What makes a data center genuinely AI-ready

**Decision:** PASS / MEDIUM

The phrase is increasingly used in infrastructure marketing, so a technical explanation grounded in actual facility requirements could be useful. It ranks below C021 because "AI-ready" can become vendor-defined unless Portfilo establishes independent technical criteria.

### C026 - Financing structures used when equity is harder to obtain

**Decision:** PASS / MEDIUM

This is more differentiated than routine funding-round coverage and can help readers understand debt, venture debt, revenue-based structures, convertibles, strategic capital, and other financing forms. It requires a clearly defined Philippine sample rather than extrapolating from a few deals.

## REFRAME

### C003 - How companies decide whether AI is paying off

Reframe into the economics/measurement layer of **C002** unless source reconnaissance shows enough comparable ROI frameworks for a standalone story.

Reason: strong reader value, but too much overlap with implementation cost and insufficient independent evidence path at launch.

### C005 - Data work before production AI

Reframe as a major reporting dimension inside **C001** first.

Reason: the mechanism is important and evidence is plausible, but on its own it risks becoming a technical checklist rather than a strong launch story.

### C006 - Why AI pilots are abandoned

Reframe as the failure-side reporting requirement for **C001**.

Reason: potentially excellent journalism, but a standalone failure taxonomy currently has too much source-access risk. It should be pursued during C001 reporting and spun out only if enough independent cases emerge.

### C010 - Are higher-value IT-BPM roles paid more?

Reframe as one measurable dimension of **C009**.

Reason: compensation is important, but the definition of "higher-value" must be established before a salary comparison can be interpreted correctly.

### C016 - One transition from routine BPO work to an AI-enabled role

Reframe as a case study or profile supporting **C009** or, later, **C012**.

Reason: one transition can be vivid and useful but cannot establish a broader labor-market pattern by itself.

### C022 - Power secured for data-center projects

Reframe as a verification dimension of **C021**.

Reason: power status is central to distinguishing credible projects from speculative pipeline, but it is strongest when tied to the broader project-status dataset.

## HOLD

### C012 - Do reskilling programs produce role transitions?

Hold until a program, employer, or cohort provides usable completion and employment-transition data.

Revisit trigger: access to participant-level or cohort-level outcomes that go beyond enrollment counts.

### C013 - Are BPO contracts moving away from per-seat/per-hour pricing?

Hold because the question is strong but contract evidence is currently too private and vendor claims are not sufficient.

Revisit trigger: multiple buyers/providers willing to discuss contract structure, or verifiable procurement/contract evidence.

### C014 - How AI is changing staffing mix in customer-service operations

Hold because existing reporting already establishes broad workforce change, while company-level staffing data may be difficult to verify.

Revisit trigger: access to comparable staffing data or a defined operation willing to document changes before and after deployment.

### C015 - Geographic accessibility of higher-skill IT-BPM roles

Hold. The question is consequential but requires a defensible job-location and role-classification dataset before it becomes a strong launch assignment.

Revisit trigger: sufficient job-posting sample with reliable location, role, and compensation fields.

### C024 - How much announced data-center capacity has identifiable customer demand?

Hold because demand commitments are often commercially confidential and public statements may not distinguish reservations, contracts, and speculative pipeline.

Revisit trigger: project-level customer or contracted-demand evidence from multiple facilities.

### C025 - What happens 18-24 months after a startup raises seed/Series A

Hold because the contribution could be strong, but private-company operating data and survivorship bias create a high research burden for launch.

Revisit trigger: a sufficiently complete cohort with verifiable follow-up outcomes, including companies that stalled or shut down.

### C028 - What capital efficiency looks like after the funding reset

Hold because "capital efficiency" needs a precise metric and comparable private operating data is unlikely to be available across a useful sample at launch.

Revisit trigger: a definable metric plus reliable financial/operating data for a non-cherry-picked cohort.

## Editorial interpretation

The strongest candidates share a pattern without needing to become a public slogan or series thesis:

- move from announcements to operating reality;
- replace broad adoption claims with implementation detail;
- turn industry language into measurable definitions;
- expose underlying economics and tradeoffs;
- distinguish announced infrastructure from verified status;
- help readers make decisions through evidence rather than generic advice.

This pattern is useful for launch-package selection, but Stage 7 must still test whether any candidates form a coherent series. Portfilo should not create a series merely because several candidates share a broad technology theme.
