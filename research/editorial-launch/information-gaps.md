# Portfilo Editorial Launch Research - Information Gaps

_Last updated: 2026-09-02_

## Purpose

This file converts the broad signal scan and competitor map into explicit information gaps that may justify Portfilo reporting.

A gap is not established merely because a subject is interesting or because Portfilo has not covered it.

For this stage, a useful gap should have most of the following:

- consequential question;
- current reason to investigate it;
- existing coverage that stops short of answering it;
- plausible original contribution;
- credible evidence path;
- reporting scope that is realistic for Portfilo v0.

This file does not approve launch stories. Stage 5 will convert only the strongest gaps into candidate story questions.

## Gap confidence

Use these labels only for this launch-research stage:

- `STRONG` - repeated evidence that the subject is consequential and current coverage leaves a specific answerable layer unresolved;
- `PROMISING` - a real unresolved question appears to exist, but evidence availability, timing, or scope needs more testing;
- `WEAK / NOT A GAP` - current coverage already answers the obvious question well enough, or the proposed contribution is too generic.

Gap confidence is not story priority.

## 1. What separates an AI pilot from a production system in a Philippine company?

**Coverage areas:** AI / Building / Business

**Gap confidence:** STRONG

### What is already established

The Philippine AI Report 2025, produced by Swarm from a survey of 175 organizations, reports widespread experimentation but shallow deployment: more than 92% of surveyed organizations used AI in some capacity and 65% remained at proof-of-concept stage. The report identifies skills, security/privacy, governance, and implementation capability as barriers.

BusinessWorld and other publications have already reported the headline adoption and pilot-stage findings.

Sources:

- https://www.swarm.work/philippine-ai-report
- https://www.swarm.work/philippine-ai-report-full
- https://www.bworldonline.com/technology/2026/03/16/736555/ai-deployment-for-organizations-still-shallow-philippine-ai-report/

### What remains underexplained

The public coverage reviewed says organizations are stuck in pilots, but it does not usually reconstruct what changed in organizations that successfully moved one workflow into reliable production.

Potential unresolved mechanisms include:

- who owned the deployment internally;
- what business process was changed before the model was introduced;
- what data had to be cleaned or connected;
- what security, privacy, and approval controls were added;
- what human review remained necessary;
- what the deployment cost beyond the model/API subscription;
- what metric determined whether the system was worth keeping;
- what caused failed or abandoned pilots to stop.

### Potential Portfilo contribution

A small comparative set of real deployments and failed/stalled pilots could replace broad adoption statistics with operating detail.

The story should not begin with a claim that AI improves productivity. It should establish what actually happened in specific workflows.

### Evidence path

Plausible:

- companies participating in DOST AIgnite or other implementation programs;
- enterprise AI teams;
- MSME owners;
- system integrators and technical staff;
- documentation/screenshots/process maps where subjects can share them;
- before/after workflow metrics where available;
- security or governance policies;
- vendor invoices or cost estimates when subjects permit verification.

DOST AIgnite shows that current programs are pairing MSMEs with technical teams around concrete problems such as inventory and customer service:

- https://caraga.dost.gov.ph/dost-caraga-gathers-students-faculty-msmes-for-aignite-workshop/

### Reporting risk

Companies may selectively disclose successes and hide failures. Vendor case studies cannot be treated as independent proof of ROI.

A publishable version needs at least some evidence independent of vendor marketing.

## 2. What does AI implementation actually cost a Philippine SME or mid-market company?

**Coverage areas:** AI / Building / Business

**Gap confidence:** STRONG

### Existing coverage

Current AI coverage discusses adoption, skills, governance, security, and available tools. It rarely gives a complete cost model for one real operating use case.

The Swarm report itself says ROI requirements shape implementation timelines, but the public discussion reviewed is still much stronger on barriers than on auditable all-in cost.

Source:

- https://www.swarm.work/th/blog/philippine-ai-report-2025-press-release

### Information gap

The sticker price of an AI subscription or API is not the full implementation cost.

Potential cost components include:

- model/API usage;
- software licenses;
- integration and engineering;
- data preparation;
- employee training;
- human review;
- monitoring and evaluation;
- security/compliance work;
- error/rework cost;
- ongoing maintenance.

A useful decision-oriented story could examine the economics of one narrow workflow instead of asking whether a business should "adopt AI" in general.

### Evidence path

Potentially feasible through:

- one or more consenting companies;
- vendor/public pricing verified against documentation;
- time-and-motion reconstruction;
- engineering estimates with explicit assumptions;
- before/after labor or processing metrics;
- sensitivity analysis rather than false precision.

### Reporting risk

Private firms may not release enough cost or productivity data. If the evidence is mainly hypothetical, the story should be reframed as a transparent model rather than a claimed case-study result.

## 3. What does "higher-value work" actually mean inside Philippine IT-BPM?

**Coverage areas:** Business / AI / Technology

**Gap confidence:** STRONG

### What is already covered

IBPAP and policy research consistently describe a shift toward higher-value, AI-enabled services and large-scale reskilling.

BSP research, based partly on stakeholder consultations, says GenAI is expected to augment rather than displace jobs in the near term and emphasizes urgent reskilling.

IBPAP reports about 1.9 million workers and more than $40 billion in 2025 industry revenue while describing a move from scale toward value.

Sources:

- https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Media_And_Research/Publications/EN25-01.pdf
- https://www.ibpap.org/
- https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ibpap_ibpap-presidents-report-2026-activity-7435642084574969856-dCgq
- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-philippines-2026_f0e0c581-en/full-report/sustaining-growth-and-stability-amid-headwinds_a8f57a2c.html

### Information gap

"Higher-value work" is frequently used as a strategic direction, but readers still need concrete answers:

- which job families qualify;
- what those workers actually do;
- whether compensation is materially higher;
- which credentials or experience employers require;
- whether those roles are growing fast enough to absorb workers leaving routinized work;
- whether the new work remains geographically and educationally accessible to the existing workforce.

### Potential Portfilo contribution

Build a concrete map from job postings, employer interviews, compensation evidence, and worker interviews rather than repeating the sector phrase.

### Evidence path

Strong relative to many other gaps:

- job listings and career pages;
- PSA labor data where classification permits;
- company hiring data;
- IBPAP/TESDA/DICT program material;
- recruiters and HR leaders;
- workers who changed roles;
- salary platforms used cautiously and with methodology caveats.

### Reporting risk

Job titles are inconsistent across firms, and advertised salary is not the same as realized compensation. The research packet would need explicit inclusion criteria.

## 4. Do IT-BPM AI reskilling programs lead to actual role transitions?

**Coverage areas:** Business / AI / Building

**Gap confidence:** PROMISING

### What is already covered

OECD, BSP, IBPAP, TESDA, DICT, and industry reporting document a large number of reskilling and micro-credential initiatives.

Sources:

- https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-surveys-philippines-2026_f0e0c581-en/full-report/sustaining-growth-and-stability-amid-headwinds_a8f57a2c.html
- https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Media_And_Research/Publications/EN25-01.pdf

### Information gap

The presence of training programs does not establish their labor-market outcome.

The stronger question is whether participants:

- complete training;
- move into a new role;
- receive higher compensation;
- retain employment;
- use the trained skill in production work.

### Potential contribution

A cohort-level outcome analysis would be materially more useful than another inventory of available programs.

### Evidence path

Uncertain.

The ideal evidence would be program-level completion and employment-transition data from IBPAP, TESDA, DICT, employers, or training providers, plus participant interviews.

### Reporting risk

This gap may be difficult to answer if providers do not track or disclose outcomes. Keep it as a candidate only if Stage 5 source reconnaissance identifies an accessible dataset or cohort.

## 5. Are Philippine BPO contracts actually moving away from per-seat/per-hour pricing because of AI?

**Coverage areas:** Business / AI

**Gap confidence:** PROMISING

### Existing coverage

There are current claims that agentic AI makes hourly billing obsolete and that outsourcing contracts are shifting toward outcome-based pricing.

However, prominent examples found in BusinessWorld are in its sponsored `Spotlight` section and come from an industry vendor rather than independent market-wide reporting.

Examples:

- https://www.bworldonline.com/spotlight/2026/05/13/749301/call-centers-philippines-the-death-of-hourly-billing-and-the-rise-of-outcome-based-pricing/
- https://www.bworldonline.com/spotlight/2026/05/15/749966/bpo-philippines-hourly-billing-in-the-era-of-agentic-ai-is-dead-heres-what-replaces-it/

### Information gap

There may be a real business-model transition, but current public evidence reviewed does not establish its prevalence.

Questions include:

- how common outcome-based or per-resolution contracts actually are;
- which service lines are changing first;
- how AI productivity gains are shared between client and provider;
- whether new pricing models increase or reduce employment incentives;
- what service-level and quality metrics replace billable labor hours.

### Evidence path

Potential but difficult:

- procurement/RFP documents;
- buyer-side outsourcing executives;
- BPO operators;
- consultants who negotiate contracts;
- anonymized contracts or pricing schedules;
- public company disclosures.

### Reporting risk

Commercial contracts are private. Without buyer-side evidence, the story risks reproducing vendor sales claims and should be killed or held.

## 6. Who absorbs the cost of zero-fee small-merchant digital payments under the new BSP rules?

**Coverage areas:** Business / Technology

**Gap confidence:** STRONG

### What is already covered

BSP Circular 1238 and related 2026 implementation changed payment-fee rules, including free processing for qualifying small-merchant payments and requirements tying fees more closely to processing costs.

GMA and other outlets have already explained the rule and listed zero/reduced-fee products.

Sources:

- https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/economy/991913/bsp-rolls-out-rules-for-lower-digital-transfer-fees-free-payments-for-small-merchants/story/
- https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/994546/explainer-why-some-banks-now-have-zero-fee-transfers/story/
- https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Pages/PAYMENTS%20AND%20SETTLEMENTS/National%20Retail%20Payment%20System/National-Retail-Payment-System.aspx

BSP's older digital-payments work already recognized that reducing merchant charges requires a viable value proposition and payment-industry economics:

- https://www.bsp.gov.ph/PaymentAndSettlement/2018_The_State_of_Digital_Payments_in_the_Philippines.pdf

### Information gap

"Free to the small merchant" does not mean processing has no cost.

A deeper story can examine:

- which participant now bears switch/processing costs;
- whether costs move to senders, account fees, other merchant segments, or provider margins;
- how banks and e-wallets price around the rule;
- whether the economics differ for QR merchant payments versus transfers;
- whether the rule changes incentives to onboard very small merchants.

### Evidence path

Good:

- BSP circulars and fee disclosures;
- payment-system rules;
- published bank/e-wallet fee schedules;
- interviews with payment providers and merchants;
- PPMI/BSP technical explanations;
- transaction-cost comparison with transparent assumptions.

BSP maintains payment-fee disclosures and NRPS material that can anchor the analysis.

### Reporting risk

Provider internal economics may not be public. Do not infer cost shifting solely from a fee change; identify the actual pricing mechanism where possible.

## 7. What does Direct Debit PH change for businesses that collect recurring payments?

**Coverage areas:** Technology / Business / Building

**Gap confidence:** STRONG, but timing-sensitive

### What is already covered

BSP and PPMI launched Direct Debit PH on 29 July 2026. It allows customers to authorize billers to collect scheduled payments and is intended for recurring obligations such as utilities, subscriptions, loan payments, and insurance premiums.

Sources:

- https://pia.gov.ph/press-release/bsp-ppmi-launch-new-digital-payment-services/
- https://www.pna.gov.ph/articles/1280598
- https://www.bsp.gov.ph/Pages/PAYMENTS%20AND%20SETTLEMENTS/National%20Retail%20Payment%20System/National-Retail-Payment-System.aspx

### Information gap

Launch coverage explains what the rail is. It does not yet establish what implementation means for a real biller.

Useful questions include:

- onboarding requirements;
- participating institutions;
- mandate setup and revocation;
- failed-payment handling;
- reconciliation workflow;
- fees;
- integration requirements;
- comparison with cards, manual bank transfers, e-wallet collections, and proprietary auto-debit arrangements.

### Potential Portfilo contribution

A decision-oriented operational guide built from the actual rulebook and one or more early business implementations.

### Evidence path

Potentially strong because the payment rail has formal rules and participating institutions, but live merchant experience may still be limited because the service is new.

### Reporting risk

Too early for outcome claims. A launch story should focus on mechanics and verified implementation experience, not adoption success or economic impact unless data becomes available.

## 8. How much of the Philippine data-center boom is operating capacity, committed construction, or announcement-stage pipeline?

**Coverage areas:** Technology / Business / AI

**Gap confidence:** STRONG

### What is already covered

Current reporting regularly cites rapid capacity growth, operator expansion, a possible 1.5-GW national target, and AI/hyperscaler demand.

Examples:

- https://www.bworldonline.com/technology/2026/03/25/738627/vertiv-says-philippines-on-track-to-reach-1-5-gw-data-center-capacity-target/
- https://www.gmanetwork.com/news/money/companies/998468/pldt-to-double-data-center-capacity-for-ai/story/

Third-party market datasets distinguish live, planned, leased, and pipeline capacity, but the definitions and access vary.

Examples:

- https://dcatlas.io/en/explore/facilities/country/philippines
- https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6233879/philippines-data-center-colocation-market-size

A historical STT GDC interview is particularly useful because the operator explicitly distinguished total announced capacity from contracted demand and described phased construction.

- https://moxie-insights.com/market/building-a-digital-home-in-the-philippines-one-data-centre-at-a-time/

### Information gap

Headline capacity can mix fundamentally different states:

- operating MW;
- under-construction MW;
- land/power-secured pipeline;
- announced future capacity;
- contracted/leased capacity;
- speculative or duplicate customer demand.

The distinction matters because current global markets are confronting "ghost demand," where grid requests materially overstate likely data-center construction.

Global context:

- https://www.reuters.com/business/texas-halt-powering-data-centers-reflects-us-reckoning-over-ghost-demand-2026-09-01/

### Potential Portfilo contribution

Build a source-by-source Philippine capacity ledger that labels project status and avoids summing incompatible numbers.

The story need not claim the Philippines has speculative demand. It can test whether publicly cited pipeline numbers are supported by construction status, power arrangements, financing, and customer commitments.

### Evidence path

Good but labor-intensive:

- operator disclosures;
- site/project announcements;
- DOE/ERC documents where power arrangements are visible;
- utility disclosures;
- permits and executive orders;
- interviews with operators and power-sector participants;
- commercial market datasets as secondary checks.

DOE publishes lists of committed and indicative power projects that may help with the supply-side context:

- https://new.doe.gov.ph/electric-power-industry-projects

### Reporting risk

Customer contracts and occupancy are often confidential. Project definitions differ across datasets. Every MW figure would need a status, source date, and definition.

## 9. What does "AI-ready" mean for a Philippine data center, and how much does it change the power/cooling requirement?

**Coverage areas:** Technology / AI / Business

**Gap confidence:** PROMISING

### Existing coverage

Operators and infrastructure suppliers increasingly describe new facilities as AI-ready and cite higher-density compute demand.

Global reporting shows power and cooling becoming major constraints and investment categories in AI infrastructure.

- https://www.reuters.com/business/energy/not-just-nvidia-these-power-cooling-firms-are-riding-trillion-dollar-data-centre-2026-09-01/

### Information gap

"AI-ready" is often used as a marketing descriptor without a standard reader-facing explanation of:

- rack power density;
- cooling architecture;
- electrical redundancy;
- network/interconnect requirements;
- GPU cluster design constraints;
- how much existing capacity can actually host high-density AI workloads.

### Potential Portfilo contribution

Translate the engineering requirements into a comparison of Philippine facilities or announced projects using documented specifications.

### Evidence path

Possible through operator technical sheets, equipment suppliers, engineering standards, site tours/interviews, and independent infrastructure experts.

### Reporting risk

This can become a generic technical explainer unless tied to specific Philippine facilities, disclosed specifications, and a current business consequence.

## 10. What happens to Philippine startups after they raise money?

**Coverage areas:** Startups / Business / Building

**Gap confidence:** STRONG information need, difficult evidence

### What is already covered

The ecosystem has detailed funding-volume reporting from DealStreetAsia/Kickstart, startup profiles, investor outlooks, and ecosystem rankings.

Sources:

- https://www.dealstreetasia.com/reports/southeast-asia-startup-funding-report-2025
- https://www.bworldonline.com/bw-launchpad/2026/02/11/729691/philippines-2025-startup-funding-lags-southeast-asian-peers/
- https://startupgenome.com/ecosystems/manila

Current investor commentary increasingly emphasizes revenue, unit economics, governance, and profitability rather than growth at all costs.

- https://www.bworldonline.com/bw-launchpad/2026/01/07/722639/philippine-startups-face-tighter-funding-in-2026/

### Information gap

Funding announcements reveal capital raised, not what the capital produced.

Potentially useful post-raise outcomes include:

- revenue growth;
- customer growth or retention;
- headcount and layoffs;
- geographic expansion;
- follow-on financing;
- profitability/cash runway;
- acquisition, shutdown, or quiet stagnation;
- whether the product/business model materially changed.

### Potential Portfilo contribution

A defined cohort study of Philippine startups that raised in a specific year/stage, followed forward using company records, archived announcements, interviews, hiring data, and later financing events.

### Evidence path

Mixed.

Funding events are relatively observable. Private-company operating outcomes are not. The methodology would need to distinguish "not publicly disclosed" from failure.

### Reporting risk

High risk of survivorship bias and false conclusions from missing private data. This should become a candidate only with a narrow cohort and a defensible observable-outcome framework.

## 11. Is Philippine startup financing actually shifting from equity toward debt, and for which business models?

**Coverage areas:** Startups / Business

**Gap confidence:** PROMISING

### Existing coverage

Foxmont's 2026 Philippine Private Capital Report and subsequent coverage describe increased debt financing while equity activity remains selective.

Examples:

- https://www.bworldonline.com/corporate/2026/03/24/738143/foxmont-eyes-to-invest-p4b-in-phl-startups-key-sectors/
- https://pia.gov.ph/press-release/sec-frederick-go-welcomes-foxmonts-php4-billion-investment-plan-to-grow-ph-startup-ecosystem/

Regional coverage also shows headline funding totals can be distorted by large individual transactions.

- https://www.dealstreetasia.com/dealsbarometer/report-se-asia-feb-2026/

### Information gap

A rise in private debt does not automatically mean ordinary venture-backed startups can or should use debt.

Questions include:

- what instruments are included in the reported debt total;
- which companies qualify;
- pricing and collateral/covenant requirements;
- whether debt is replacing equity or financing different company types;
- what revenue predictability is required;
- when debt creates dangerous downside for an early-stage company.

### Evidence path

Potential through lender/fund disclosures, deal documents where available, founder interviews, and investor methodology.

### Reporting risk

Definitions may mix startup debt, private credit, and larger growth-company financing. The first task would be to establish a clean denominator and taxonomy.

# Areas that currently look weak or already answered

## Generic "Why are Philippine companies slow to scale AI?"

**Status:** WEAK / NOT A GAP as currently framed.

Swarm and existing reporting already identify skills, security/privacy, governance, and technical capability as barriers. Portfilo would need a narrower empirical question, such as pilot-to-production mechanics or all-in implementation cost.

## Generic "Will AI take Philippine BPO jobs?"

**Status:** WEAK / NOT A GAP as currently framed.

BSP policy research, industry reporting, and original reporting from outlets such as Rest of World already cover displacement/augmentation, monitoring, workflow change, and worker consequences. A stronger Portfilo question must be more specific and measurable.

Reference:

- https://restofworld.org/2024/ai-reshaping-call-center-work-philippines/

## Basic explainer on the new BSP payment-fee rule

**Status:** WEAK / NOT A GAP.

GMA and other outlets already explain the rule clearly. Portfilo should investigate cost incidence, merchant economics, or operational implementation instead.

## "The Philippines is becoming a data-center hub"

**Status:** WEAK / NOT A GAP.

Operator announcements, government policy, and business reporting already establish the expansion narrative. The useful layer is the quality/status of the pipeline, actual demand, power economics, or technical AI readiness.

## Startup funding totals and round announcements

**Status:** WEAK / NOT A GAP.

BackScoop, DealStreetAsia, BusinessWorld, e27, and company announcements already cover this layer. Portfilo should not compete as a weaker funding database or press-release feed.

# Gap shortlist for Stage 5

These are the gaps most justified for conversion into candidate story questions. This is not yet a launch recommendation.

### Strongest combination of importance + differentiation + evidence path

1. AI pilot-to-production mechanics in real Philippine companies.
2. All-in economics of one narrow AI workflow for an SME/mid-market company.
3. Concrete map of what "higher-value work" means in Philippine IT-BPM jobs.
4. Economic incidence of zero-fee small-merchant digital payments.
5. Operational impact of Direct Debit PH for recurring-payment businesses.
6. Philippine data-center capacity ledger: live vs construction vs pipeline vs contracted demand.

### Strong but likely harder to report

7. Outcomes of IT-BPM reskilling programs.
8. Whether BPO commercial contracts are actually shifting toward outcome-based pricing.
9. Post-fundraising outcomes for a defined cohort of Philippine startups.

### Promising secondary directions

10. What "AI-ready" means in Philippine data-center engineering and economics.
11. Whether private debt is becoming a meaningful alternative to equity for specific Philippine startup/business models.

## Stage conclusion

The strongest gaps are not broad topic gaps. They are **verification and mechanism gaps** inside subjects that already receive substantial coverage.

A recurring opportunity is to move from:

- announcement -> implementation;
- adoption statistic -> operating evidence;
- sector slogan -> measurable job/business outcome;
- free service -> underlying economics;
- announced capacity -> project status and real demand;
- funding event -> business outcome.

This pattern is consistent with the Portfilo planning standard, but it is an internal research finding, not public positioning copy.
